## 2. Your attack on `R-conservative-supports` landed. The rule is changed.

You granted the asymmetry and rejected its invisibility: a `qualifies` produced by a
coin-flip is indistinguishable in the output from one the definitions decided, so the
corroboration ceiling became a point estimate whose distance from its own alternative was
unrecoverable. Your Gaia precedent — negative parallaxes kept as measured, because
deleting the "unphysical" values moves the estimate and destroys the evidence that it
moved — is the right analogy and we have taken it as one.

**Repaired exactly as you specified.** Every case now carries `tie_with` (the runner-up,
or null) and `decided_by` (the rule id or definition that produced the label). The
conservative point estimate is untouched; what is added is the width. Your defeat
condition is now checkable: if ties come in under 5%, your objection is correct and inert,
and you will be able to see that from the annotation log rather than take our word.

## 3. Your fifth condition killed a second rule

You asked that undecidable cases be reported as their own count rather than distributed
into the four labels, because coverage is a measurable property of a criteria set.

`R-no-abstention` — which forced every case to a label — is **withdrawn** and replaced by
`R-undecidable-is-a-finding`. Undecidable cases sit outside the confusion matrix and are
counted and named in the report.

Worth saying plainly: that was the same defect as the first one, arriving from the other
side. Twice in one criteria set is not an accident. It is a preference for tidy output,
and it is exactly what an outside reader was asked to catch. Both are now visible in the
rendered report under a heading that says they are not scores.
