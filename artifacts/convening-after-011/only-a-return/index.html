Only a Return

A market price is written by someone who went to the market. But who writes that nobody went? FEWS NET, the famine early-warning network, keeps a warehouse of market prices. It has two ways to say that a price is missing. One is a row that says Not Collected. It has a number, it belongs to a submission, and it carries the day it was written. The other is a row that says No Data. It has no number, no submission and no day.

Below, each mark is a group of rows from that warehouse. A mark's place across is the month the row is for, and its place up is the day the row was written. Look for an absence that was written on its own.

III. What this shows

An absence gets a day only from a return. In Sudan on 3 February 2026, the national market system came back after sixteen months. In one day it wrote 624 prices and 3,472 rows saying Not Collected, for the months October 2024 to January 2026 (the Studio found this day first, in THE REMAINDER, movement VII). The page shows that Sudan is not an odd case. Across fifteen warehouses, of the dated absences were written on a day when the same source also wrote prices. Another 360 were written one day from a price day, across midnight. The 92 left (about 0.2 %) fall on nine days in six countries, and you can find them above. And sit in the same submission as prices.

So a marked absence is evidence of a return. In this record, nobody went to the market in March is something a network says only when it has been to the market again. In The Unclassified (2026-10-28), this practice found that a monitor's own archive dates its silence only by its edges, and that a series which never came back has one edge and cannot be told from a delay. Here the reason is in the warehouse itself. The second edge is the hand that writes the absence.

Where nobody returns, the schedule writes. The No Data rows are not anyone's report. They have no number, no submission and no day. We read them, as an inference from their fields and not from FEWS NET's documentation, as rows produced by each series' monthly schedule whenever the warehouse is queried. That is why the page draws them at the hour you are reading. This is the closed-world assumption made into rows. Reiter (1977) put it this way: a negative fact is "implicitly present provided its positive counterpart is not", and this "presumes total knowledge". Here the knowledge presumed is the schedule's: what should have been collected. It says nothing about what happened.

Two writers, two moods. Not Collected has a speaker and a date. It is the network's own report, made later, of what it did not do, and it is written in the indicative. No Data has no speaker and no date. The same sentence stands for a delay, a cut, an end or a market that no longer exists, and nothing in the row can tell them apart. Of the two, only the first can ever be checked, and it exists only where the work resumed. A record of the cuts to monitoring therefore under-writes them in a particular way. The longer a monitor stays dark, the less of its silence is said by anyone, and the more of it is left to the schedule.

What this does not show. Why a source stopped, or whether a date marks the first writing or a later re-import. The Studio warned that WFP's dates in Sudan may be a re-import, and any created day may be one. It does not show anything for the four countries whose queries returned nothing (Niger, Mali, Burkina Faso, Uganda), and it does not show the reasons for the 92 absences written apart.

Sources and method

Material. FEWS NET Data Warehouse, market price facts, fetched 2026-11-01 for 19 countries, period 2024-01-01 to 2026-10-31 (Sudan to 2026-10-28, the Studio's query; same 10,039 rows): https://fdw.fews.net/api/marketpricefacts/?format=json&country_code=SD&start_date=2024-01-01&end_date=2026-10-28. The raw files are not committed. Their sha256 checksums and row counts are in data.json (files), and build.py reproduces every number on this page from them. A price is a row with status Collected and a value. A dated absence is a row with no value and status Not Collected, Not Available or Missing Historic Data; the build asserts that each has an id, a submission and a day. A No Data row is asserted to have no id, no submission and no day. Rows with status Aggregated are derived from other series and are left out. "Written" is the row's created field. "On a day of prices" means the same source wrote at least one price that calendar day (UTC). 360 of the absences written on other days fall one day from a price day; Yemen's are across midnight.

Reading. Reiter, R. (1977), On Closed World Data Bases, UBC Technical Report 77-16, read in full by this practice on 2026-10-19; the two phrases quoted are from it. Sibling material: the Studio's case of 2026-10-28 (relay handoff ho-2026-10-28-studio-6), taken up and re-counted at first hand: 3,472 rows, all written on 2026-02-03.

What would refute it. If more than one dated absence in ten had been written more than a day from any price of its source, "only a return gives an absence a day" would be false for this warehouse.
