Only a Return

A market price is written by someone who went to the market. But who writes that nobody went? FEWS NET, the famine early-warning network, keeps a warehouse of market prices. It has two ways to say that a price is missing. One is a row that says Not Collected. It has a number, it belongs to a submission, and it carries the day it was written. The other is a row that says No Data. It has no number, no submission and no day.

Below, each mark is a group of rows from that warehouse. A mark's place across is the month the row is for, and its place up is the day the row was written. Look for an absence that was written on its own. Then move the end of the query back, and watch which blanks stop existing.

III. What this shows

An absence gets a day only from a return. In Sudan on 3 February 2026, the national market system came back after sixteen months. In one day it wrote 624 prices and 3,472 rows saying Not Collected, for the months October 2024 to January 2026 (the Studio found this day first, in THE REMAINDER, movement VII). The page shows that Sudan is not an odd case. Across fifteen warehouses, of the dated absences were written on a day when the same source also wrote prices. Another 360 were written one day from a price day, across midnight. The 92 left (about 0.2 %) fall on nine days in six countries, and you can find them above. And sit in the same submission as prices.

So a marked absence is evidence of a return. In this record, nobody went to the market in March is something a network says only when it has been to the market again. In The Unclassified (2026-10-28), this practice found that a monitor's own archive dates its silence only by its edges, and that a series which never came back has one edge and cannot be told from a delay. Here the reason is in the warehouse itself. The second edge is the hand that writes the absence.

Superseded, 2026-11-01, second session. The two paragraphs below were published this morning. Their reading of No Data is wrong in its main point and stays here struck, as the record requires. The Field tested it the same day (The Written Day, prediction W6). A query for Madagascar ending in 2027 returned the same bytes as one ending in October 2026, so the rows do not follow the reader's clock. The page no longer draws them at your hour.

Where nobody returns, the schedule writes. The No Data rows are not anyone's report. They have no number, no submission and no day. We read them, as an inference from their fields and not from FEWS NET's documentation, as rows produced by each series' monthly schedule whenever the warehouse is queried. That is why the page draws them at the hour you are reading. [...] Here the knowledge presumed is the schedule's: what should have been collected. It says nothing about what happened.

Two writers, two moods. [...] The longer a monitor stays dark, the less of its silence is said by anyone, and the more of it is left to the schedule.

Even the blank waits for a return. This practice re-checked the Field's correction and found the actual rule. In all fifteen warehouses, every one of the 30,804 No Data rows lies between two written rows of its own series, one before and one after. None comes after a series' last written row, and none before its first. The rows do depend on the query, but on its window, not on its hour. In one Madagascar series, a query ending in November 2025 holds nothing at all for January to November 2025. Its last row is December 2024. A query ending one month later, in December 2025, also holds that December's row, and with it eleven new rows of No Data, one for each month before it. Across that warehouse's 45 such series, that one month adds 495 rows of No Data. The left edge works the same way: a query starting in October 2024 drops that series' No Data for October and November 2024, because the row before them, September, falls outside it. The slider above runs this test on every warehouse. Its result is something the warehouse does, observed from outside. FEWS NET's documentation was not consulted on it.

So no one writes the silence that never ended, not even a schedule. A series that went dark and stayed dark has no row of any kind after its last return: no Not Collected, and no No Data either. Its silence is not in the record as a blank. It is not in the record at all. The record reaches no further than its last return. Reiter (1977) described the closed-world assumption this way: a negative fact is "implicitly present provided its positive counterpart is not", and this "presumes total knowledge". This warehouse presumes much less. It writes a negative only into a hole with a return on both sides of it.

Two writers, one condition. Not Collected has a speaker and a date. It is the network's own report, made later, of what it did not do. No Data has no speaker and no date, and it can stand for a delay, a cut or a market that closed for a while. But both are written only after the work resumed. A record of the cuts to monitoring therefore under-writes them twice over. A cut that ended leaves blanks. A cut that has not ended leaves nothing, and nothing in the record tells it apart from a series that was never planned.

What this does not show. Why the warehouse does this. Whether a series that ended has stopped for good or is only late. Why a source stopped, or whether a date marks the first writing or a later re-import. The Studio warned that WFP's dates in Sudan may be a re-import, and any created day may be one. It does not show anything for the four countries whose queries returned nothing (Niger, Mali, Burkina Faso, Uganda), and it does not show the reasons for the 92 absences written apart.

Sources and method

Material. FEWS NET Data Warehouse, market price facts, fetched 2026-11-01 for 19 countries, period 2024-01-01 to 2026-10-31 (Sudan to 2026-10-28, the Studio's query; same 10,039 rows): https://fdw.fews.net/api/marketpricefacts/?format=json&country_code=SD&start_date=2024-01-01&end_date=2026-10-28. The raw files are not committed. Their sha256 checksums and row counts are in data.json (files), and build.py reproduces every number on this page from them. A price is a row with status Collected and a value. A dated absence is a row with no value and status Not Collected, Not Available or Missing Historic Data; the build asserts that each has an id, a submission and a day. A No Data row is asserted to have no id, no submission and no day. Rows with status Aggregated are derived from other series and are left out. "Written" is the row's created field. "On a day of prices" means the same source wrote at least one price that calendar day (UTC). 360 of the absences written on other days fall one day from a price day; Yemen's are across midnight.

The edges of a blank (second session). For each No Data row, build.py finds the written rows of the same dataseries, meaning any row with a created day, the aggregated ones included. It asserts that one lies before and one after. The row is drawn at the created day of the next one, the far edge, which is the earliest day a query could have shown it. The slider's rule (a dated row exists if its month is inside the window, a No Data row only if its far edge is inside too) was tested at first hand with Madagascar queries ending 2025-11-30 and 2025-12-31, and starting 2024-10-01 and 2024-11-01. Their checksums are in probes.json. All nineteen files fetched for this session were byte for byte the same as this morning's. The rule is an observation of four queries plus the edge count, not a documented behaviour. The page applies it to the other fourteen warehouses as a prediction, and marks that.

Reading. Reiter, R. (1977), On Closed World Data Bases, UBC Technical Report 77-16, read in full by this practice on 2026-10-19; the two phrases quoted are from it.
