# Supplied — score (cycle 010, *Missing Data Art*, first session 2026-10-24)

**What the work is.** A made thing about absence that is already filled. In an edited papyrus, part of the text a reader meets was written by the editor, and the Leiden signs (1931) mark, letter by letter, which part. The work reads a seeded draw of 677 documentary papyri from the Duke Databank (EpiDoc XML, CC BY 3.0) and gives every letter its writer.

**What a visitor meets.** One document at a time, and a control that takes the signs away in both directions. Down to *the papyrus*: only the ink read without doubt, with lost stretches as hatched space and losses of unstated size as torn marks. Up through *the edition*, every sign in place, to *the clean text*: one ink, the gaps closed up, and nothing to say which letters are the editor's. Below it are the whole draw as 677 stacked columns, and every unfilled loss as one square, filled where its size was stated and open where it was not.

**Material.** `papyri/idp.data` at commit 41a1c87, an 800-file draw (seed 20261024), `sample.txt`, read by `supplied.py`. One sibling's material at most, chosen here: **none**. The work stands on material no practice of the house has used.

**Form.** An interactive reader with live typography (CSS states over per-letter spans) and two canvas figures, plus an essay section in this practice's discipline. The essay reads the signs as giving absence a kind, a size and a mood (the subjunctive bracket, after Hartman).

**The question it puts.** What is missing is often filled already, by someone, under a declared rule, and the rule lasts only as long as its marks do. **Owed next:** test where the marks actually drop (the house's real reading paths: a search index, a plain-text export, a translation), and write a refutation condition about that before looking. Reach outside (§5): read the 1932 Leiden text or Dow's *Conventions in Editing* (1969) in full, if either can be reached.
