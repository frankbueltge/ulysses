# Critique — Supplied, end of session 2 (2026-10-24)
Written by a convened sub-agent that did not build the work; met in a browser at 1280 px and 390 px, all five states, "Another", the draw figure and both added sections.

The reader carries the claim. The papyrus state, hatched and torn, turns "missing" into something you see. Moving from it to the clean text ("166 letters, all in one ink. Nothing on the page says that 56 of them are the editor's") makes a visitor feel how much of a plausible document was written by someone else. The training pair with "show who wrote them" on is the strongest moment in the piece: the repeated blue ξεσται, given to the model as the scribe's ink, shows the new finding better than any paragraph. It works at 390 px with no horizontal scroll and no console errors, and clicking a column loads that document and scrolls back to it.

What fails is that the second session's finding has not reached the words a visitor reads first or last. The lede still promises only "down to the papyrus, or up to the clean text, where the signs are gone". The essay still says a plain-text corpus "drops" the signs and that "the rule survives only as long as its marks do". Yet "Where the marks drop" shows the corpus keeps 98 % of restorations as dashes and keeps the sizes of losses. The sharper claim is that the marks are sorted: expansions pass as the scribe's ink, and the scribe's doubtful ink becomes "lost". The lede and the essay should be rewritten around that claim, and the training pair named in the lede.

The tally contradicts itself. The method says two predictions held and two failed, but the list reads P1 refuted, P2 failed, P3 held, P4 refuted. A visitor who counts will stop trusting the rest.

The corpus card is a wall of text, with an interval after every percentage. Its one key number (51.3 % of the editor's letters reach the model as the scribe's) should come before the bar chart, in large type, with the intervals moved to a note. The chart itself is clear and should come first.

The page says the "with brackets" string holds no bracket at all. Yet the corpus record printed under the reader shows ⟨ιζ⟩ around numerals. Either explain those brackets or qualify the claim.

With the checkbox on, the restored dashes are thin red strokes and nearly invisible. The red needs more weight, or the dashes need a background tint.

The site path is announced as one of three, but it holds nothing a person can see. Either test it with a person's browser or stop presenting it as a path.

The slider has no label and repeats the buttons. The 4,418-square loss grid reads as texture: nothing can be clicked, and the point it makes (a floor and no ceiling) gets lost in the grid. The default document shows line 13 twice with no explanation.

A next session could let the visitor guess, in the clean text, which letters are the editor's before revealing them. That would make the claim felt rather than told.
