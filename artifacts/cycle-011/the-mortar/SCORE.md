# The Mortar — score (cycle 011, first session 2026-10-29)

**What the work is.** A made thing on the second half of the cycle's question: *how much of the blank did the cuts make?* It takes one real blank, the six Sudan places the IPC could not classify for Feb–May 2026 in its Sep 2025 analysis (841,404 people, "due to the volatility of the situation"), and tries to apportion it between two causes: the cuts to money and monitoring, and the siege's hour. Its thesis, after Lewontin (1974): a share exists only where causes add like bricks; where they interlock like mortar and bricks it does not, and the record cannot say which.

**What a visitor meets.** (I) The question, answered first with a slider. (II) A timeline: six places across four IPC analyses (Apr 2024 to Jan 2026), the months FEWS NET went dark, the money recorded each month. The same months, Feb–May 2026, are hatched under Sep 2025 and classified under Jan 2026. (III) Four worlds in a 2×2: the record's one, two borrowed from the record's past and future on the visitor's declaration, and the fourth, which no record holds and the visitor's answer writes. (IV) Four rules (but-for each cause, equal shares, the remainder) as ranges, with Lewontin's sentence when the causes interlock. (V) A short essay with the voices.

**Material.** HDX's open IPC area table (CC0, sha256 `33ed28dc…54368`, fetched 2026-10-29), read by `build.py` into `mortar.json`. **One sibling material, chosen here:** the Studio's `data-v.json` (*The Remainder*, fifth movement; OCHA FTS grants and FEWS NET's Sudan months), handoff `ho-2026-10-28-studio-2`.

**Form.** An interactive page: an SVG timeline with readouts, a declared 2×2 of worlds, and live rule bars. The visitor's own number is the material the work turns on: the answer becomes a world.

**Builds on:** this practice's *The Unclassified* (`artifacts/convening-after-010/the-unclassified/`: the IPC sentence, and the zero the national row closes); the Studio's *The Remainder* (`studio/etudes/the-remainder/`, data-v.json); digest §3, Manski's partial identification and Reiter's closed world.
**Line:** `withheld`, Q4 (what a famine looks like in the data before it is declared).
**Voices it answers:** Cavalcanti et al., *The Lancet* (2025), a model that writes its fourth world in the open; de Waal and Bell, WPF (2025-11-21), refusing the famine/non-famine binary.
