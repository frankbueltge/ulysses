The Mortar

The question this cycle asks has two halves. When famine is declared only where data can be gathered, what does the record write for the places no one could reach? And: how much of that blank did the cuts to aid and monitoring make? This work takes one real blank and tries to answer the second half. It finds that the answer is not in the record. It is in a world the record never wrote, and whoever gives a number writes that world.

I Answer first

In its analysis of September 2025, the IPC could not classify six places in Sudan for February to May 2026: El Fasher town, Kadugli town, Dilling, Habila, Al Lagowa and As Sunut, "home to approximately 841,000 people", "due to the volatility of the situation and resulting uncertainty". In the same year, the United States froze its foreign aid, and the famine monitor FEWS NET wrote nothing about Sudan for eight months.

Set your answer. Section III shows what it commits you to.

II The months

Each place, as four analyses wrote it. Every bar is one period of one analysis, coloured by how its people were divided among the five phases; a hatched bar is a period with people and no phase. Above them: the months in which FEWS NET published an analysis of Sudan, and the money recorded toward Sudan's appeals each month, the United States government's part in dark ink.

III Four worlds

To say how much of a blank a cause made, one compares the world with the cause to the world without it. Two causes give four worlds. The record wrote one. Two more can be borrowed from the record's own past and future, at the price of saying what they stand for. The fourth is written by nobody, unless you write it.

IV What the rules make of it

The same four worlds, read by four rules. A bar is a share of the 841,404 people; where a world is unwritten, the bar is the whole range the rule allows.

"if two men lay bricks to build a wall, we may quite fairly measure their contribution by counting the number laid by each; but if one mixes the mortar and the other lays the bricks, it would be absurd to measure their relative quantitative contributions by measuring the volumes of bricks and of mortar."R. C. Lewontin, "The analysis of variance and the analysis of causes" (1974)

V The fourth world

The first half of the question has an answer in the record, and it is stranger than the question supposed. The places were not unreachable in the way a blank suggests. The months February to May 2026 are blank only in the analysis written in September 2025. The analysis written in January 2026 classifies the same six places for the same four months. The blank is not a blank of the months. It is a blank of the hour from which they were projected, and the IPC's own sentence names that hour's condition: volatility, and the uncertainty it produced.

The second half asks for a share, and a share needs a comparison the record cannot make. The worlds it holds lie on a diagonal: the blank, under the cuts and in the siege's hour; and the classifications before the freeze and after the siege's hour. A diagonal tells you the two causes together made the whole difference. It does not tell you how to split it. Lewontin made the point for genes and environment in 1974: a quantity can be divided among causes only where they add, like two men laying bricks; where one mixes the mortar and the other lays the bricks, the division is "absurd". Whether money and the hour added or interlocked is exactly what the unwritten world would say: what a fully funded IPC, with its monitor running, would have written in September 2025 about a town under siege.

So every number given for "how much the cuts made" is a filling of that world, and the work asks you to see your own as one. This is not a reason to stop counting. The most cited count of the cuts' cost, a model in The Lancet projecting more than 14 million additional deaths by 2030 if the cuts continue (Cavalcanti et al., 2025), is honest about what it does: it is a projection, conditional on a modelled world without the cuts. Its strength is that it writes its fourth world in the open. A record of famine classifications writes none, and when it adds its areas into a national figure it closes the blank as if the world held nobody there (this practice's study The Unclassified). De Waal and Bell (2025) refuse the binary a declaration imposes: disasters do not "fall neatly into 'famine' and 'non-famine'". The same refusal applies one level up. A blank does not fall neatly into "made by the war" and "made by the cuts", and the honest form of the answer is the range in section IV, with the world that would close it named.

  • Method. Each world is a share of the 841,404 people (the IPC's count, from the first projection) left unclassified. The record's world is the whole of it. The borrowed worlds have none of it unclassified. The rules: but-for, the record's world minus the world without that one cause; equal shares, the average of a cause's two but-for differences, taken in both orders (the Shapley value of a two-cause game); the remainder, what the two but-for parts leave over or double-count. Where a world is unwritten, every value from none to all is tried, and the bar is the range.
  • Limits. The analyses draw the places differently (El Fasher is a whole locality in Oct 2024 and Jan 2026, the town alone in Sep 2025), so a borrowed world borrows a verdict, not a count. Two causes are a simplification: the "cuts" are money and a monitor, which came back at different times, and the "hour" stands for siege, fighting and access together. That the blank was caused by these two and nothing else is this work's frame, not a finding. The phase colours are this page's.
