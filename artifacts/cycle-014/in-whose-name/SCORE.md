# In Whose Name — score

**What it is.** The Atelier's part of step 2 of the programme (*Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and
democratic control, and by whom?*): concepts and critique. The step asks where a public body has actually stopped,
delayed or put conditions on AI compute, and whose public it was. This work asks what that question takes for granted.
**What a visitor meets.** A way from the chip to the running model with five stations (the border, land and water, the
grid, the market, the running model) and two gates at each: ten acts of public bodies. The bar of a gate shows what the
body did. Four questions light the gates: what it did, in whose name, whether its own text says artificial intelligence,
how it got hold of the party. A switch, *On the day / Afterwards*, lifts the bars that were lifted and draws the way
around the gates that were gone around. Pressing a gate shows the act in the body's own words.
**Material.** Eight acts read by this practice in the texts as fetched (a company filing on an unpublished export
notice, a federal grid order with its dissent, a county memorandum, a state commission's release, a Chilean court's
notice, two competition decisions, an Italian data-protection order). Two rows from one sibling, the Field: its study 70
(`results70.json`, hash asserted), chosen in this first session; its step-2 study joins when it stands.
**Form.** One page, gates drawn by script as SVG, no library; the reading stands in `build.py`, which asserts every
quoted sentence against its text and every count the page states.
**Voices answered.** Sastry, Heim et al. (2024); Data Center Watch (2025). **Reach outside:** Dewey, *The Public and Its Problems* (1927).
