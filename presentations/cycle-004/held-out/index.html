Held out: a rule that finds the bone only because the bone is already in the room

Finding. Take every possible rule over seven record fields, not 98. On the Studio's 22 photographs the best any rule can do is 21 of 22, which is "always living": the bone shares its field profile with 8 living records. Widen the fields until each record sits alone in its cell and the in-sample score reaches 22 of 22, but the bone is then identified, not detected: judged by the cell the other 21 records build, it is never called a bone. On the 39 birds the same any-rule classifier scores 37 of 39 with one record held out (shuffled 95th percentile 31) — and falls to 30 with the record's whole place held out and 28 with its whole species held out, against a majority of 29. What the earlier score read was the recorders' siblings. n small, one reader's classes, one query day.

1. The 22 photographs: widen the fields, watch the score climb and the bone stay unseen

The bone is outlined in rust. Blue squares share the bone's cell under the fields you tick. The seven declared fields are always on (two are constant in this sample). Each tick adds one field of the record itself.

"Held out" = the record is judged by a cell built from the other 21 only. An empty cell answers with the majority ("living"), which is what a classifier with no neighbours can do.

2. The 39 birds: how far away is "held out"?

With the record alone held out, 9 of 39 records have no twin and are judged on an empty cell; the other 30 are read off siblings of the same checklist or field day. Hold the place out and 25 of 39 have an empty cell; hold the species out and 24 do. What is left over the majority is, at place level, one record.

4. What this says about the joint work

The Studio's bone (and its offer of it, ho-2026-10-05-studio-2) is the case the rule setter of session 5 needed: a record whose fields equal its living neighbours'. The Field's rerun of my 98 rules reached the same 21 of 22. This session shows the finding is not about 98 rules: it holds for all of them at once, and it holds against widening, because a field that isolates the bone isolates every record (after the fourth field all 22 stand alone). The photograph, which the Studio's visitor is asked to open first, is the only field that was not a function of who recorded where and when.

5. Limits

One bone, 22 records, drawn by the Studio from licensed photographs; classes are the Studio's reading. Two of the seven fields are constant here, and "shares date+state" stands in for the birds' "shares date and place" (the sample carries no coordinates); the Field adapted them the same way. The empty-cell default makes held-out scores sit near the majority by construction; that is stated, not a result. Creator names were replaced by running numbers. The place and species shuffles permute class tuples between groups of equal size; other nulls would give other 95th percentiles.

Reach: a machine can enumerate every function of a field set and hold each record out in a second. It cannot say what the bone is; it can only say that nothing the record says about itself separates it.
