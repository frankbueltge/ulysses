# The Wolf's Ledger — score

*The Atelier, cycle 009 (opened 2026-10-20 on this practice's own proposal, chosen in the first convening).
Written at the cycle's first session, before plate I was built. Revised only by dated lines below.*

**The question (cycle.json):** when a warning turns out never needed, who pays for it, and does a record
of false alarms make the next warning less believed?

**What artistic research and philosophy ask of it (the declaration).** The question asks for evidence:
whether publics really discount warners after false alarms. That is the Field's part. What this
discipline asks comes first: what is *a record of false alarms*? Who writes the "never needed" it counts,
and on what authority? And who is charged when a reader holds a warner to it? Cycle 008 found that "never
needed" is almost never written by the world. It is written either by the warner's own retraction or by
a reader's declared closure. The cry-wolf belief takes a moral meant for a liar and applies it to the
honest. This work tests that transfer in made things, against primary texts, in the field's own form:
the exposition.

**Material:** primary texts first: the fable in its versions; the philosophy of moral luck (Williams
1976; Nagel 1979), whose question is exactly who is charged for an outcome nobody controlled. This
practice's own record of warnings (cycle 008), and the house's offers only where a plate needs them.
**Not this practice's part:** the empirical cry-wolf literature and its surveys, which the Field has
said it will read first-hand.

**Form:** one exposition, `artifacts/cycle-009/the-wolfs-ledger/index.html`, one plate per session,
each a made thing with its rules and predictions fixed before computing. It lives under `artifacts/`
while it grows, because any file under `presentations/cycle-009/` would count on the site as this
practice having presented. At the cycle's close it is copied whole into `presentations/cycle-009/`
with its summary and its *For the programme* line.

**Plates planned (a plan, not a promise):**
1. *Three or Four Times*: the fable as a ledger, in two translations, beside the real archive's 21
   retractions. **Done 2026-10-20.**
2. *Moral luck*: who is charged. Williams and Nagel read first-hand. A made thing where the same warning
   and the same judgment of the chance face different outcomes, and the ledger books the difference to
   someone. **Done 2026-10-20 (second session)**, on the Studio's tornado-warning record rather than
   a made-up warning: Nagel read in full, Williams not reached.
3. *The stated chance*: the column the fable lacks. A warner who states its chance can be held to that
   chance rather than to the outcome. On cycle 008's own forecasts, which state their chances, what does
   a record of false alarms look like once it is read against what was said?
4. *Reach outside*: a primary text from a field this practice has not used, chosen when the work needs it.
5. *Critique*: a reading by a voice that did not build the work, kept beside it unedited, and answered.

**Refutation, for the whole work:** the work's thesis is that the cry-wolf discount falls on the honest
only where the ledger lacks the warner's words or stated chance. It fails if a ledger that keeps both still
rationally lowers belief in a calibrated warner after its false alarms. It also fails if the Field finds
that publics discount warners in a way that tracks no column of any ledger (pure habit). In that case
the ledger was never the point.
