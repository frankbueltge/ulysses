## The question, from this discipline
Before anyone asks whether publics discount warners, there is a prior question: what is *a record of false alarms*? Who writes the "never needed" it counts? And who is charged when a reader holds a warner to it?

## What the plates found
1. **Three or four times** (the fable, two translations, Gutenberg): the shepherd's boy is disbelieved because his ledger keeps his laugh. The moral is earned for the liar only. The translations give the last silence two causes, reputation and habit. In 21 real retractions no warner confesses a hoax.
2. **The run before** (Nagel, *Moral Luck*, 1979): over 30,093 times a tornado met a warning, the run of empty warnings before a hit is what the county-year rate predicts. A record of outcomes charges the warner for its luck in circumstances.
3. **The stated word** (the Weather Service's own instruction, NWSI 10-511): the warning keeps a stated confidence, in words chosen at the hour. "Radar indicated" is met 24 % of the time, "catastrophic" 72 %. The false-alarm ratio throws the words away.
4. **The burden and the loss** (law: Hand in *Carroll Towing*, 1947; *Brown v. United States*, 1986): priced by Hand's test, an owed empty warning is the burden of those who took cover. The warner sets that price for them, and nobody writes it down. Which record writes "never needed" decides the verdict on 90 % of warnings.
5. **The uncertain village** (the critique's demand; Williams through Sussman): the score's own refutation condition **fires**. A village that keeps the words and learns from the record still believes a well-calibrated office less after a run of empty warnings (95.9 % of the stated chance). But it believes at 98.9 % on average and at 102.2 % after a hit, and the discount fades as the record grows (7.1 % to 1.2 %). A habit village, after the same runs, believes at 27.2 %. Across 72 offices, the order of warners survives the choice of record (rank correlation 0.90), while a verdict on them does not.

## What it holds (judgment)
The thesis this work began with was false as written, and the work refuted it by the condition it had set itself. A record of false alarms does make the next warning less believed, for a reader who learns from it: briefly, correctly, and repaid at the next hit. Only a reader who counts runs and drops the words charges the honest warner the fable's price, a doubt that never recovers. For a warning that was owed, judged before its outcome, the people who took cover pay for the empty ones, at a price the warner set and no instruction, record or court writes down. Williams adds where the outcome does belong to the warner: in its own regret, not in another's ledger.

## Limits
The villages are models, not publics. Whether real publics learn or count is the Field's question. One country's tornado warnings, 2015–2024, with "empty" taken as the record's word, not the sky's. Hand and *Brown* are read by a non-lawyer, by analogy, and no legal conclusion is drawn. Williams is read only through quotation. The corrections made at the critique are marked in place on the page, struck through and dated.

## Coda II (added 2026-10-22, after the presentation)
**The price of an hour.** The Field priced plate IV's ratio (its study 34, `results34.json`): B is the people under a warning times a quarter of an hour times the hour's worth; L is the warned dead and injured, valued at the government's prices, spread over the warnings the record calls met. Because the dead are the same under both records, B / L moves with the record almost exactly as the chance of "radar indicated" does. Priced, plate IV's amber band (24.0–29.7 %) closes to **$7.09–$7.14 an hour**, and the record no longer decides Hand's verdict. The federal minimum wage, $7.25, lies just above it: there Hand's line charges 92.8 % (hour) and 94.1 % (later) of the empties to the warner as over-warning. Plate IV's claim (11) is qualified in place, dated; plate IV gains a dollar control, and coda II a figure (`price.py`, `price-ui.js`). `verify.mjs` now runs 170 of 170.

## Coda IV (added 2026-10-22, fifth session, after the presentation)
**The number and the sentence.** The Field's study 37 named the tornado that pulls its ratio hardest: McLoud, Oklahoma, 24 May 2011, 32 injured where its model expected 0.6. Read against NOAA's survey narratives, 8 of the 15 hardest-pulling tornadoes have a sentence that counts their injured. Three have no sentence about anyone hurt, and McLoud is one of them. The Norman forecast office's own page lists McLoud with 1 injured, the truck driver. The national database carries 32, with the same narrative minus the clause about the driver. Refitted with the office's count, the Field's model reads 1.334, and the order of the villages reverses. Habit goes first only if McLoud hurt 27 or more. The interval holds all three villages at every count. Which count is true is not decided here (`survey.py`, on the Field's study 37 data and NOAA's files, sha256 asserted). `verify.mjs` now runs 212 of 212.
