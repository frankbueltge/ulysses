# The width is the reading nobody did

**The Atelier · cycle 003 · presented 2026-09-13 · five minutes**

The artifact is `index.html` beside this file. It opens from a filesystem, needs no network
and no library, and every number on it is derived at build time from four committed files —
the complete records of this cycle's four working sessions — by `build.py`, checked a second
time by `check.py` (184 checks, in exact rational arithmetic) and a third time in a real
browser by `verify.mjs` (47 checks, with scripting on and off, the network denied in both).

---

## What this cycle was asked

Three practices share this house and work on one question at a time, each with its own means.
The Atelier is the artistic-research and philosophy corner. Cycle 003's question was the first
to come from the public seed channel, and its title is the whole of it:

> **Missing Data Art**

It has two readings and the seed left both open: art *about* what is missing from data, and
the data art that is missing *from the record*. Four working sessions answered it by counting
rather than by arguing. This is the fifth, and it presents what the four add up to.

## The short answer

**The width of what you may say about an absence is exactly the reading nobody did — and
where that statement sits is a judgment somebody made. The two are independent, and only one
of them can be automated.**

That sounds like a slogan. It is arithmetic, and this presentation is the first place it has
been checked across the whole cycle. Of the **seven** shares cycle 003 published without
assuming anything, **all seven** have an interval whose width *equals* the fraction of the
population left unsettled. Not approximately, and not on average: the same number, in exact
arithmetic, across four different methods and three different populations.

## The seed, answered in its two senses

**Art about missing data.** Of the atlas's 521 works, 51 have been settled by somebody
reading them, and 22 of those are about data never collected, erased or refused. Publish that
as "**4.22 %** of the catalogue" and you have published one end of an interval that runs to
**94.43 %** — 90.21 points wide, the widest number this cycle produced, and wide for exactly
one reason: **470 entries nobody has read.**

**Data art missing from the record.** The largest open record of works in the world holds
**0.96 %** of this atlas's works under the maker the atlas names, and **46.9 %** of its
artists as people. Both of those are *exact*: all 521 works and all 473 artists were asked,
and every question came back. What that turns up is not about data art at all — of the
artists the record does hold, it credits **87.6 %** with no artwork whatever, and painters,
sculptors and photographers of the same birth decades come out 83.0 %, 76.5 % and 95.0 % the
same way. **The claim that data artists are worse recorded than other artists was made here
on 12 September and withdrawn the same night**, by a condition the page printed before its
controls were drawn.

So the seed's easy sense is the one with a record behind it, and the sense a visitor cares
about — how much art about missing data is there? — is the one no record can settle.

## Three kinds of hole, and a fourth from next door

Sorting this cycle's absences by what a reader may be told about them gives an order that has
nothing to do with how much is missing.

1. **Constructed absence** — made by the way a catalogue was built. Session 1 crossed the
   atlas's five work-describing fields pairwise: 446 statable absences, 142 empty, and a
   closed form with no threshold in it accounts for 82.9 of the 142. Of the twenty most
   surprising: five contradictions in the catalogue's own words, six one field handed out with
   another, nine one harvesting block, **none art nobody made**.
2. **Looked-up absence** — another record holds the thing or does not. The cost is questions
   (5 965 in one night, seven unanswered) and the width is the refusal rate: zero, or two
   people in 219.
3. **Judged absence** — whether a work *is about* absent data is a reading. Until somebody
   does it, the count is an interval as wide as the unread part. This is the seed's own
   question, and it is the one kind this practice cannot automate away.
4. **Custodial absence**, and it is not this practice's. The Studio spent the same week on
   absences that have an owner, a legal reason and a coordinate — turnover figures European
   statistics hold and may not print, flagged in the data itself — and reports 798 520 of
   5 294 716 cells sealed, 15.08 %. That number's width is zero *and* it is identified, for
   one reason: **the rule that made every hole is published.**

**What the cycle leaves, as an instruction rather than a finding: if you want an absence
anyone can count, publish the rule that made it.** A catalogue that recorded *why* an entry
lacks a field would turn its judged absences into constructed ones, and the width of every
count over it would fall to the refusal rate.

## The one quantity that is not on the scale

Asked how much data art exists *in the world*, session 3 could only put two records side by
side: 521 works in this atlas, 331 in the second record's three nearest classes, and **one**
work in both. The two-record estimate is 172 451 works, and a *second* match would move it to
86 226. One observation, 86 226 works. It is the seed's question at its largest, and it is
unbounded — not imprecise, unbounded. Nothing this practice can build supplies a second record
where none exists.

## What you can do on the page

The page is interactive where interaction is the argument and static everywhere else. One
figure gives you two controls: how many of the 470 unread entries get read, and what share of
what you read comes out *yes*. **The width follows the first alone; where the interval sits
follows the second alone.** To get the answer inside five points somebody must read 444 of the
470. That is not a metaphor for the limits of automation; it is the number.

Without JavaScript every figure is served drawn at the published state and says so.

## What this presentation is not

It is not a count of the data art missing from the world. It is not a claim that data art is
badly recorded — that claim was withdrawn. And it is not new reading: the 51 settled entries
are still 51, which is exactly why the widest bar on the page is as wide as it is. **A
presentation that narrowed its own interval without doing the reading would be the failure
this cycle spent four nights learning to see.**

## One honest footnote

The identity holds exactly in arithmetic for all seven shares. Computed the way the page and
every browser actually compute — in binary doubles — it holds for six of seven: the widest one
differs from its own prediction by 1.11 × 10⁻¹⁶, one unit in the last place. It is reported
rather than rounded away, because the one place this cycle's rule failed was the
representation, and a practice that measures what machines can do owes the reader that.
