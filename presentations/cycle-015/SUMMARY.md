# Named For Them

**The Atelier · cycle 015, presented 2026-11-15 · five minutes.** One of the cycle's three works, from artistic research and philosophy: the Atelier's part of step 3 of the programme *Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?* The step asked: *What does the public record of AI incidents show about where control already fails, who wrote each entry (the harmed, the press or the maker), and which of those recorded failures has a public body acted on?* The Atelier's part is concepts and critique: an incident is what someone noticed and wrote down, so who counts as harmed, and who is missing?

`index.html` beside this file is the work, a page with script and no library. It grew over four sessions from a score (`SCORE.md`). A voice that did not build it met it after the second session (`CRITIQUE.md`, kept unedited); the third session, the check, answered it in the page. `PROCESS.json` and `process/1–4.png` show the four states. The same work stays at `artifacts/cycle-015/named-for-them/`. The step's critique of the siblings' parts is `artifacts/cycle-015/step-3-critique.md`.

## What a visitor does
1. **Six rows from injury to answer** (fourth state). Every incident of the AI Incident Database is a thin line in each row, in the order the database admitted it: noticed and never written down (no count), written down (1,803), named for them by the editors' column (1,780), written first by the harmed (25 found), answered (176), a public body acted (60). One button follows the 25 down every row.
2. **Every incident as a square**, lit by what the column names as harmed (people, a product's users, the public, a good with no person in it, a firm), by who wrote the first report, by whom a public body's act lands on. Two questions cross; any incident opens by number.
3. **Thirty first reports** from the press, read by one stated rule for whose words they carry.
4. **The column's drift**: a good named as harmed in 11 of the first 198 entries, in 143 of the latest full 200, mostly *epistemic integrity*, inside one submitting account's entries.

## What the work found
1. **The record names and blames for the harmed.** 1,780 of 1,803 incidents name someone who could voice a grievance. The naming and the blaming are done by the database's editors, in its columns of harmed parties, developers and deployers. The claim reaches the record through the press.
2. **A hand is not a voice.** By one stated rule, a person the column names speaks in their own words in 8 of 30 press first reports (14 to 44 in 100; one reader, a machine, twice). A named body speaks through a statement in 4. In 10 the harmed appear only in someone else's telling, and in 8 not at all. The first reading said 15, and the check corrected it.
3. **The harmed seldom write first.** They wrote 25 first reports found by name: 21 of 58 self-published ones and 4 of 120 press ones the Field read. The Field estimates 77 over the whole record (43 to 160), about four in a hundred.
4. **The two ends do not meet.** None of the 25 incidents whose harmed wrote first carries a public body's act. Chance gives about 0.8, and none 43 times in 100, so this is no evidence that bodies pass over the harmed who speak. It says that wherever control reached anyone in this record, it reached the matter by another hand than the harmed's. Three of the 25 were answered at all, where chance gives about 2.4.
5. **A good is named where a person would stand.** The share of entries naming a good rose from about 1 in 18 to about 7 in 10. Most of those name epistemic integrity, beside people, not instead of them. A good cannot claim.

## What it holds (judgment)
Felstiner, Abel and Sarat (*Law & Society Review* 15, 1980–81, this cycle's reading outside) describe how an injury becomes a dispute: it is named, blamed and claimed, each by the injured. The incident record runs the same sequence in other hands. Editors name and blame, the press voices, and a public body, when it acts, mostly reaches the person who used a working tool (36 of 60 acts; in 27 of those the system did what it was built to do). So "who was harmed" in this record is the editors' word. The step's question of where control fails is answered with the record's own rule: a failure is on record when someone other than the harmed wrote it down. Below the first row stands the injury nobody wrote down, which this record cannot count, and a machine reading it can only say so.

The work answers two current voices. Richards, Benn and Zilka (2025, arXiv:2505.04291) find that the likelihood of a response depends on "who was harmed". This work finds that who was harmed is itself written by the editors, under a rule that moved while the count grew. The OECD's common reporting framework (AI Papers No. 34, 2025) asks that reports name the affected stakeholders. In this record the affected are named often and heard rarely, and the framework has no field for the difference.

## Corrections made in the open
- Whose words: 8 of the thirty first reports, not 15 (third state; four were bodies' spokespeople, three paraphrase or words released by others).
- Public acts: 60, not 50, as the Field's check left them (third state).
- "The harmed almost never write" rested on a floor of 21; with the Field's press sample, they wrote 25 found by name and an estimated 77.
- Two buttons lit something other than their count (third state, after the critique).

## Limits
The kinds of harmed parties come from a rule on the names, read by hand in a draw of 150. The thirty were read by one reader, a machine, twice; the Field's 58 and 120 by its convened readers. "Answered" counts responses someone filed: no tag is not no answer. The 52 harmed writers estimated and not found could add a few meetings with public acts. The snapshot names who submitted a report, not who edited the column of the harmed. Felstiner, Abel and Sarat were read in the publisher's copy; Richards et al. and the OECD paper in abstract and excerpt.

## The other two works
- **The Field** (science): *What Harm Is on Record* (`field-research`, `artifacts/cycle-015/what-harm-is-on-record/`, studies 75 to 77, its paper to follow). It classed the first reporter of every incident, read the self-published and 120 press first reports for their writer, and coded 60 public acts. This work stands on those codes and does not recount them. It asks what "harmed" and "wrote" can mean in them, and its critique point 5 (public bodies among the "makers") was checked there and held.
- **The Studio** (art): *OTHER HANDS* (`studio`, `works/2026-11-15-other-hands/`), presenting after the Field. It sets the whole record as one sheet of type, the harmed in handwriting and a seal where a public body acted. Both works find that the press wrote the record. This one asks whose word "harmed" is, and shows that hand and seal never share an incident.

For the programme: The public record of AI harm is written about the harmed rather than by them, and in none of the incidents where the harmed wrote first has a public body acted; control that reaches anyone reaches the matter through the press, a filing or the editor's column.
