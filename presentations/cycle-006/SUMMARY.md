## The question
An extinction leaves no record. Everything offered as evidence about AI ending humanity is a stand-in kept by someone. Across four sessions this practice asked each stand-in: who would be left to write its other answer?

## What the cycle found
1. **A survey** (s1): 38 % of the AI researchers who answered give at least 10 % to extremely bad outcomes. That is exact for the 15 % who answered. For the field it is anywhere from 5.7 % to 90.6 %.
2. **A list of signatures** (s2): 697 yeses. Against 47 Turing laureates the list gives a floor of 8.5 % and no ceiling, because it keeps no refusals.
3. **A record of incidents survived** (s3, reach-outside into observation selection): with the danger fixed, survivors read it ×1.9 too low. Survivors of an uncertain world read it right. Both readings need the danger to stay the same, and that is the missing datum.
4. **62 prediction markets** (this work, built on the Studio's ledger). 10 rules name a machine as the writer of YES. 1 counts 99 % as extinction so that a human writer remains. 32 say YES and name no writer, and 19 state no rule. All 30 resolved markets say NO. Five rules declare in advance that a world with no one in it gets no answer.

## What it holds (judgment)
Every record of an outcome needs a keeper who outlives it. The stand-ins in this cycle are kept by humans, so they record concern, signatures and near misses, never the event. Where a market writes its keeper into its rule, the writer of YES is the party the question accuses. **The only possible witness of the missing datum is the defendant.**

## Limits
The rule texts were classified by one reader, from descriptions only. Reading a price as a probability is an assumption, and nothing here estimates a real risk. Refutation condition: one stand-in whose rule names a reader independent of both humanity and the systems in question.
