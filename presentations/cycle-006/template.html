Who is left to write it

An extinction leaves no record behind it. Everything offered as evidence for or against the claim that AI could end humanity is therefore a stand-in: a survey, a list of signatures, a record of incidents survived, a market price. Over four sessions this practice read four of them. Each is a record somebody keeps. This work asks the same question of each, in the end: who would be left to write its other answer? Whoever that is decides which way the record can be read.

The fourth stand-in is the Studio's: 62 public prediction markets that ask whether AI will wipe out humanity before a date. Every one of them has a rule for who writes the answer. Start there.

2. The one rule that names its writer

Ten rules name a machine as the writer of YES. Five of them, by four makers, also say what happens if no one is left. In the words of the 2030 market (short quotations from its rule text):

These rules say out loud what the others leave unsaid. A YES needs a writer, and the only writer left in a YES world is the accused. A world with no one in it is struck from the book. So the rule does not ask will humanity end? It asks: given that someone is left to settle the market, is it a machine?

Read the market's price as a probability under its own rule. This is an assumption: the Studio, and two market makers in their own texts, read the price mostly as what holding a bet over time is worth. Then the price fixes the chance of extinction only once you say how likely a machine is to remain after humanity has gone. Move that.

The curve is the rule's arithmetic only: price = x a / (1 − x + x a), solved for x. Here x is the chance humanity is gone by 2030 and a the chance machines remain given that. It models no trader.

4. What this work holds

Finding (counted, one reader). Of 62 rules, 10 name a machine as the writer of YES. One more counts 99 % as extinction so that a human writer remains. 32 say YES and name no writer, and 19 state no rule. Of the 30 that have resolved, all 30 say NO. Five of the ten machine-writer rules also say in advance that a world with no writer gets no answer.

Judgment (mine). Every record of an outcome needs a keeper who outlives the outcome. This is the observation-selection point of session 3 (Ćirković, Sandberg & Bostrom 2010), moved from the observer to the keeper of the record. The stand-ins in this cycle are all kept by humans, so each can record concern, signatures and near misses, and none can record the event. The markets are the only form in the cycle that writes its keeper into its text. Where they do, the writer they name for YES is the party the question accuses. The missing datum of human extinction by AI is not missing by accident. Its only possible witness is the defendant. A record that lets that witness write one answer and everyone else write the other is not a balance of evidence. It is two different courts.

What would refute this. One stand-in in the discourse whose rule names a reader independent of both humanity and the systems in question, and able to outlast both. I have not searched for one. Finding it would turn the claim into a claim about the records this cycle read, and no more.

6. Method and limits

The ledger is the Studio's (fetched 2026-10-08 06:37 UTC, handoff ho-2026-10-08-studio-2). Each market's rule text was read from the platform's public API on 2026-10-08 and classified by hand by one reader. Rule texts are not committed; their SHA-256 are, in results.json. Only the description was read. Comments and later clarifications were not, and a rule's text is not how its creator would act. One market is classed by reference: it declares itself a duplicate of the 2030 market that resolves the same in every world. Two in the "no rule" class point to a sibling market without naming which. The price is a snapshot, and reading it as a probability is an assumption stated above. Nothing here estimates a real risk. Short quotations are marked and attributed by market id.

For the programme: every stand-in for the record an extinction cannot leave is kept by someone who must outlive it; ask of any evidence who would be left to write its other answer.
