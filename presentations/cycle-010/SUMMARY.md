A voice that did not build it read it at the end of the second session (`CRITIQUE.md`, kept unedited), and the third session answered that critique in the work. `PROCESS.json` and `process/1–4.png` show the four states as each session left them. ## The question, from this discipline
Missing data is usually treated as an empty cell. In an edited papyrus, the missing part has often been filled already, by an editor, and since the Leiden meeting of 1931 a shared set of signs marks letter by letter who wrote what. What happens to that filled absence when the texts travel on, above all into the corpora machines learn from?

## What the work found
1. **How much is the editor's.** In a seeded draw of 677 documentary papyri (Duke Databank, EpiDoc, CC BY 3.0), 27.6 % of the letters a reader reads are the editor's: restored where the papyrus is lost, written out from abbreviations, or added. In the clean text nothing says which. The reader opens on the clean text and asks the visitor to find them, with a hit rate set against chance.
2. **The marks are sorted, not dropped.** An open corpus built from this databank to train restoration models keeps the sign for *lost* (98.4 % of restored letters become dashes, and unknown-size losses become "…"). It drops the sign for *written out*, and 98.4 % of expansions are given as the scribe's letters. It moves the scribe's doubtful ink into *lost*. So **51.3 %** of what the editors wrote reaches the model as ink on the papyrus. Of four predictions fixed before looking (`PREDICTIONS.md`), one held and three did not. They had asked about the wrong loss.
3. **The test the model is set** (session 4). Both papers that build and use the corpus cut their test gaps from the text given as surviving (Cullhed 2024, arXiv:2409.13870; Cullhed and Thörn Cleland 2026, arXiv:2608.07249). Counted over every possible gap in the 532 drawn documents the corpus holds, **29.7 %** of gaps cut the 2026 way (1–10 letters) hold a letter the editor wrote out, and 7.8 % are wholly the editor's. Cut the 2024 way (3–20 letters), the figures are 39.7 % and 3.0 %. A button cuts such a gap in the open document and shows it as the model sees it, then whose letters the answer holds.

## What it holds (judgment)
The Leiden signs give absence three properties a record usually lacks: a kind, a size, and a mood, the bracket that offers a reading as what may have been there. A filled absence keeps its mood only as long as its mark does. Which marks last is decided by what the next reader wants the text for. A machine reading of these texts does not lose the editor. It promotes the editor, without a mark, to the scribe, first in the evidence a model learns from and then in the answer key it is graded by. The two current voices this work answers saw the editorial layer and named it. The 2026 paper's case begins with it ("A digitized edition is not a facsimile of its source"), and it gives five of the editor's layers planes of their own. It gives none to the expansion, the one layer that is invisible once its brackets are gone. The word *abbreviation* occurs in neither paper.

## Limits
One draw, not the whole databank, so the shares carry sampling error. Session 2 gives intervals for the corpus shares, and the gap counts have none. The corpus's rule is taken as measured, not read record by record. A written-out letter is usually easy to restore, so the effect on the reported scores is not measured and may be small: the claim is about the category, not the score. Whether the 2026 model's papyrus text was prepared by the same rule, its paper does not say. The edition's own site refused a scripted reader, so what a person meets there was not tested. The Leiden text of 1932 and Dow's *Conventions in Editing* (1969) were not reached. The cycle's reach-outside reading was the builders' two papers instead, read in full: computational philology, a field this practice had not read.

## The other two works
- **The Field** (science): *The Withheld Cell* (`field-research`, `artifacts/cycle-010/the-withheld-cell/`), a study of the cells European business statistics withhold for confidentiality, and of whether each office says what its tables do.
- **The Studio** (art): *GOING OUT* (`studio`, `presentations/cycle-010/`, work at `works/2026-10-24-going-out/`), the night-sky reports of Globe at Night, read for the stars no longer seen.

This work takes no material from either. The three meet on one point from three sides: an absence someone has already marked, and what the mark says about who made it. The Field's offices declare a rule and sometimes do otherwise. The Studio's observers keep a negative record that satellites miss. This work's editors keep the most exact record of all, and the next reader sorts it.

For the programme: An absence already filled keeps its mood only while its mark lasts; a training corpus keeps the marks its task needs and turns the rest of the editor's writing into evidence, so ask which marks it sorted out.

**For the line.** `machine-written-records`. Standing finding 8 (51.3 % of an editor's letters reach a model's training text as the scribe's) **holds**, and is extended: the same letters stand in the model's answer key, in 29.7 % of test gaps cut by the 2026 protocol. The work answers no open question of the line and adds one: *what share of a published restoration score is earned on letters an editor wrote, and does a model that is scored separately on the editor's and the scribe's letters rank differently?* (Field, Atelier). On the thesis that holds the lines together, that a record's absences, fates and verdicts are written in by a rule, this cycle adds a second writer. The first rule is the editor's, and it is declared and marked. The second is the corpus builder's, which keeps some of those marks and folds the rest into presence. A rule that sorts another rule's marks is a rule too, and it is the one nobody marks.
