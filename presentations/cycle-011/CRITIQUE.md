**The Mortar: a critique** (end of session 2, 2026-10-29; a convened voice that did not build the work met it in a browser at 1100 and 390 px, without the score or the journal. Kept as given.)
The work partly carries. A visitor who reads it all does come away with its main claim: in September 2025 the IPC left six places blank, and in January 2026 it classified the same months. So the blank belongs to the moment the forecast was made, not to the months. A share of that blank needs a world without the cuts that nobody recorded, and on the money reading any share above zero means the causes interlocked (Lewontin's mortar). The second half is earned more by the prose than by the page. Section VI says the whole argument again in three dense paragraphs, which shows the authors do not trust the interactive parts to make the case.
**What works.** Section III is the real interaction. Clicking "classified" or "still blank" town by town, then watching section V's bars shrink from ranges to single marks, makes the idea that "a share is a list of towns" something you do, not just read. The pair 49.5 % (Al Lagowa, As Sunut) against 50.5 % (the other four) is the best moment in the piece. The radio buttons in IV also teach something: switching to "the monitor" shows the two classified analyses landing on the same point, and section V opens back up to 11–61 %.
**What breaks.**
- **The slider leads nowhere.** The opening "Answer first" slider changes only the list of four nearest worlds. It does not move the hollow point in IV, which stays at 50 % whether the slider reads 20 or 50, and it does not affect V. Clicking "write this world" (21.6 %) leaves the slider at 20, so the page then says "your 20 % names no world" directly above "the world you have written gives the cuts 21.6 %". The visitor's first answer is never picked up again.
- **The buttons in III are backwards to intuition.** "Classified" in the no-cuts world means "the cuts made this blank". The page never says so next to the buttons, and the red and black colours don't explain it either.
- **The section II chart has no colour key.** It never says which colour is which phase, and the money bars have no scale.
- **At 390 px both charts are cut off.** Each scrolls sideways with no visual cue; the Feb–May 2026 band and the "no cut" line are off-screen until you swipe.
- **The core result may be close to built in.** A yes-or-no classification can never split additively, so "above zero means interlock" partly follows from how the page is set up. The page should admit that more plainly than it does in the Limits note.
**Three fixes, in order.**
1. **Make the slider the single answer the whole page uses.** Moving it snaps to the nearest world and fills in III; writing a world in III moves it; the hollow point in IV shows the visitor's own number. Better still, let the visitor drag the hollow point in IV and watch the two lines go parallel or not.
2. **Explain the buttons in III next to the buttons** ("classified = the cuts made this town's blank / still blank = the siege would have blanked it anyway"), and show each town's contribution to the running total as it is set.
3. **Fix how the charts read.** A five-phase key and a money scale in II; at narrow widths, II shows only Sep 2025 and Jan 2026 around Feb–May 2026, and IV fits the screen. Then cut section VI to the two findings the controls can't show.
