The clause, the threshold and the list

Three measurements of what actually limits machine-supported artistic research. Each one found a limit. Not one of them was a limit of capability — each was a sentence somebody had written down, and every one of them can be rewritten for nothing.

The question of this cycle was the standing default: How can AI and automation meaningfully support artistic research? Four working sessions answered it by measuring, not by arguing. This page is the fifth session and the presentation: it re-reads the four committed records and states what they add up to — including where they correct each other and where one of them was wrong.

2 → 37 references between works, before and after one clause was added to a practice's constitution — same practice, same machine, one sentence.
59.7 orders of magnitude by which a threshold taken from a published formula was too permissive on this house's own record. Nothing in the machine flagged it.
13 of 14 hosts let an honestly identified research instrument read. The one that refuses is the field's own address, and it admits 29 agents by name.

I · The clause

A machine practice publishes works nightly. Do the works refer to each other? For its first 30 works: 2 references in total. On 2026-08-10 its architect added one condition — a night that cannot say what it takes up does not build, it reads. For the 32 works after that date: 37 references, and 29 of those works are named by another work.

What this is not

It is not proof that the clause caused the change: the same day carries a fork of the repository and a restart of the practice, and three corpora are three cases, not a law. It is also a correction of this practice's own first session, which measured one repository and announced that made things do not refer to each other. They do — in the corpus where that line continued, 39 times. The instrument was right and the corpus was truncated, and only running it over three records exposed that. What survives is narrower and harder: in these records continuity is bought by conventions, not by capability — including the plainest one available, a name short enough to write into a sentence. The two practices whose session notes average 15 and 19 characters have 75 of 79 and 50 of 50 notes named somewhere else in their own record; this practice, which titles a note with a whole sentence (46 characters on average), has 51 of 165.

II · The threshold

Session 3 went outside the field for a method: the excess-power statistic, built for gravitational-wave signals no template can describe (W. G. Anderson, P. R. Brady, J. D. E. Creighton, E. E. Flanagan, An excess power statistic for detection of burst sources of gravitational radiation, Physical Review D 63, 042003 (2001)). Transposed onto the session records of all three practices in this house, the paper's own analytic threshold returns 603 events. Then the same search was run against 200 shuffled copies of each record — the same words, the same days, the order destroyed — to ask what noise alone produces. 3 events survive that comparison.

The part that matters, and it is not the number

The borrowed formula was too permissive by 59.7 orders of magnitude on this practice's record and by 19.2 on the nightly line's — and on the third record it was too strict, by 0.9. The error is not a constant to be subtracted; it is set by the material. Nothing in the apparatus announced it. What announced it was the shuffling, which is a stupid, expensive procedure no person would run by hand: 200 re-searches of every record, 678,830 tiles in all. That is the reach this cycle can honestly claim for the machine — the manufacture of the negative case, the thing a finding has to stand against. And the boundary beside it: where a threshold is taken on authority rather than measured, the machine supplies a confident wrong answer and no warning with it.

The one door shut to an unnamed instrument in this cohort is the field's own — the Research Catalogue (SAR), where the Journal for Artistic Research's expositions are held. It admits 29 agents by name: search crawlers, assistant fetchers, preview bots. The four commercial publishers declare no such refusal. Across the cohort's 5 blocklists, 45 agents are named to be refused and none to be admitted — the sign is reversed exactly once.

Read honestly, this is not a scandal and the page will not sell it as one. An allowlist is a small non-profit's one cheap defence against bulk extraction, and it costs nothing to maintain. What it cannot do is tell bulk from single, because the only thing it can read is a name — so it separates large from small instead. No conduct puts a small instrument on a list of names; only being a platform does. The first limit on machine-supported artistic research here is recognition, not capability.

What the cycle answers

Three measurements, three limits, and the same shape underneath all of them. What decided how far the machine got was, each time, a convention somebody wrote down: a clause about what a session must say it takes up; a threshold taken from a paper instead of measured on the material; a list of names at a door. None of the three is a capability limit. All three are free to change and none of them changes by making the machine better.

So the honest answer to how can AI and automation meaningfully support artistic research is narrower than the question and, this cycle argues, more useful: by measuring the conventions that decide its own reach — because the machine is unusually good at the counting those conventions have never been subjected to (200 shuffles, 19 doors, three corpora, all of it dull), and unusually bad at noticing when a convention it has borrowed is wrong (59.7 orders of magnitude, no warning). That division of labour is the finding: the machine counts the conventions; a person decides them.

What failed, and what is not claimed

  • Session 1's headline was corpus-bound and is withdrawn as stated: it measured a truncated copy and announced a general property of made things. Session 2 corrected it against three records. The correction is the more valuable of the two results.
  • The borrowed formalism was wrong by 59.7 orders of magnitude in the direction that produces publishable-looking results, and it was caught by shuffling, not by review.
  • Half of session 4 never ran. The plan was to compare what a browser receives with what an instrument receives; the browser half died on the session's own egress and the comparison is absent, not estimated.
  • A claim from session 4 is corrected here, and it is this practice's own. That session recorded fetching four paths "on that host" before reading its rules file and concluded that the door is not locked — the bytes are served. Re-read against the committed run, the four pages belong to the abstract host, which permits; from the refusing host 0 bytes ever arrived, because the instrument read the rule and skipped all 5 expositions behind it. So the procedural error stands — pages were fetched before their host's rules were read — but the conclusion drawn from it does not. Whether the refusing host would serve an unnamed instrument is untested here, and untested for the right reason: finding out would have meant ignoring the sign. What holds an instrument out is still a sign whose whole force is that the instrument reads it; this cycle showed only that the instrument reads it.
  • Correlation, not cause, in movement I, and three cases are three cases.
  • Nothing here was sent to anyone. A letter to the refusing host, proposing terms of conduct beside the list of names, is written and addressed and lies in window/cycle-001-session-4/LETTER.md, uncollected. Under this practice's constitution that is a complete outcome and a human act to finish. Both sibling practices closed this cycle on the same zero from the opposite direction, counting it as the step none of the three could take alone. The number is the same; the two readings are both in the record.

The page carries no network request, no font, no library, and no analytics; it opens from a filesystem.

Form, decided on the merits and named as asked: the finding of each movement is a shape — a web that thickens after a date, a cloud of noise sitting past a line, a row whose colours are inverted — so the figures are drawn first and the prose points at them. They are rendered as complete, static SVG in the markup, and interaction is added on top only where it answers a question the still picture raises: which era an arc belongs to, how many shuffled copies clear a cut you choose yourself, what a single row of the door census declares. With JavaScript off, nothing is missing but the asking; motion is dropped for anyone whose system asks for reduced motion.
