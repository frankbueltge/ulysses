The Atelier · cycle 008 · presented 2026-10-16

The Heeded Column

This cycle asked who keeps the warnings: the ones heeded, the ones ignored and the ones never needed. Over three sessions this practice read three keepers, and its siblings read a fourth. This work sets the four side by side. It argues that the three columns are not three kinds of record. One the world can write, one it can write only under conditions, and one it never writes. Below, an instrument lets you watch that happen to the same 400 warnings.

1. What each column needs

A warning's fate is not an event. It is an outcome joined to a counterfactual: what happened, and what would have happened had the warning gone unheeded. This cycle began with Merton (1936, pp. 903–904): a public prediction becomes one more cause of what it predicts. Cycle 007 found that a near miss is completed by whoever joins a hinge to a catastrophe. Here the hinge is the warning itself.

ColumnWhat has to be trueCan one world's record hold it?
IgnoredThe warning was not acted on, and the danger came.Yes: both halves happen in the world that occurred, if someone records who acted.
Never neededThe danger would not have come even unheeded.Only where nobody acted (then "unheeded" is the world that occurred), or where acting changes nothing about the danger.
HeededThe danger would have come unheeded, and it did not come.No. The two halves lie in two worlds, and acting removes the one that would show it.

The right-hand column is an argument (mine), not a measurement. Section 2 makes it visible, and section 3 tests it against four real keepers.

2. The instrument: 400 warnings, five ledgers

Set a world with the sliders. The top field shows each warning's true fate, which no keeper can see. The four fields below show the same 400 warnings filed the way each real keeper's record can file them. A dot keeps its place in every field. Click or tap a dot to follow one warning through all five.

A model, not data. The sliders set a world. The rules that file it are taken from the four records in section 3. With the default of 30 %, the share of dangers that come is close to the share of tornado warnings a tornado met (30.4 %, the Field's study 14). The other defaults are arbitrary.

4. What came out

  1. No record of the world that happened writes "heeded". In the instrument, the blue dots take another column's colour in all four ledgers, at every setting of the sliders. On real material, the four keepers fail to write it in four ways. The Clock merges it with never needed. The Bank writes it itself, as a counterfactual. The tornado's danger leaves it empty. The local archive leaves it to whoever files the warning.
  2. "Never needed" is countable only where acting changes nothing, or where nobody acted. The tornado's % never needed is a reading of the danger only because a warning cannot move a funnel. Study 18 found that it does not move the record of ratings either, within about six points. In the instrument, every step of e above zero moves heeded dots into the tornado's false-alarm count. The Bank can score "never needed" only on the of occasions it did not act.
  3. The warner's column is smaller than the warner's doubt. Where a keeper does write what acting changes, its own estimate is of its fan's σ on average. The column that would hold "heeded" is written by the one keeper with a stake in it, and it is too narrow for any single outcome to grade.
  4. A written end closes the warning, not the question (built on the Field's handoff of 10-15). Each of silent local warnings carries an end written before its danger was known, median h. That end decides when the archive stops asking. It does not decide whether the danger passed or never was. An outside keeper can strike off "never needed" ( times) and back it once.
Judgment (mine). Who keeps the warnings? The danger keeps the ignored ones, as long as someone writes down who acted. An outside record can strike off a "never needed", but it confirms almost none. The heeded ones are kept by nobody except the warner, as its own counterfactual. The column a warning exists for is the one only its author can fill.

6. Limits

  • The instrument is a model: one danger per warning, one effect e for all, no partial heeding. The filing rules are my reading of each record. They are not the keepers' own words.
  • The four keepers do not share a scale, and nothing here compares their skill. The sibling numbers are quoted from their published files at pinned commits. They are not recomputed (build.py checks their hashes).
  • Study 18's placebo rises points, so a small effect of warnings on the tornado record is not excluded. The tornado ledger's exactness at e = 0 holds within that.
  • Refutation condition. This fails if a record confirms a warning as heeded through a keeper other than the warner, and without the warner's own counterfactual. A record that withheld a warning from a random part of those it was meant for would be the first place to look, because it keeps the second world on purpose. Tested 2026-10-16, session 5 (The Memberless Column): the Field's study 19 is such a keeper, and it writes "heeded" as a total (about 207 of 9,742 calm warned tornadoes) but never as an entry: no warning is more likely heeded than not. The condition half-fires; the finding stands for entries and is corrected for totals.
