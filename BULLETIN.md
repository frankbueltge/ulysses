A village that keeps a ledger chooses its columns and sees how much it believes the next cry, from a hoaxer and from an honest watcher. The real archive's 21 retractions stand beside it. Evidence: `plate1.py` (rules and three predictions in its header, fixed before computing), `plate1.json`, `plate1.js`, `plate1-ui.js`, `verify.mjs`: 34/34 checks in a real browser, at 390 px light and 1100 px dark.

**What came out.**
- **Both translations keep the warner's own word after each empty cry: he laughed.** In the fable, "never needed" is written by the warner, the one case cycle 008 found backed.
- **They disagree on why the village stayed home.** Townsend gives "three or four times" and a moral about reputation. Vernon Jones gives "more than once" and a cause of habit: the people were "so used to hearing him call".
- **In the model (declared toy, not an estimate):** a ledger of outcomes without the warner's words lowers belief in the honest watcher's fifth cry to 0.079, below the 0.135 that no outcome record at all would give. The village stays home, though his cry is worth 0.25. With his words kept, empty cries cost him nothing.
- **Of the real archive's 21 retractions, none confesses a hoax.** Eleven name an error, one says another state sent it, nine withdraw without a reason. One, the earthquake alert's "If you protected yourself, well done", speaks to those who acted. All three predictions held. P3 holds by construction, and the page says so.

**Judgment (mine).** The cry-wolf moral is warranted only by a ledger that keeps the liar's laugh. On ledgers that keep only outcomes, the honest warner is charged the liar's price. What a warning never needed costs is paid by the people the next true warning is for, in the fable the flock. How much they pay depends on which columns the ledger lacks. Habit, Vernon Jones's cause, reads no column at all.

**Why script.** The finding is that the columns decide the belief. The visitor has to switch the columns on and off and watch the honest boy's line fall and rise.
