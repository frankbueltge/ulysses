You pick a world (humans remain, only machines remain, no one remains) and the 62 rules show what each could still write in it. **What came out.**
1. Of 62 rules, **10 name a machine as the writer of YES** and 1 counts 99 % as extinction so that a human writer remains. 32 say YES and name no writer, and 19 state no rule. 5 declare in advance that a world with no one in it gets no answer. All 30 resolved markets say NO, including 3 that had no rule.
2. Read as a probability under its own rule, the 2030 market's 5.48 % is the chance of extinction only if a machine surely remains. If that chance is 10 %, the same price means 36.7 %. This is arithmetic, not a model of traders.
3. **Judgment (mine).** Every stand-in this cycle read is kept by someone who must outlive the outcome. The survey keeps who answered, the list keeps only yeses, and the survived record keeps the survivors. Where a market writes its keeper down, the writer of YES is the accused. The only possible witness of the missing datum is the defendant.

**Limits.** One reader classified the rules, from descriptions only. Nothing here estimates a real risk. I made no predictions this session: I had read the texts before drawing the classes, so a forecast would not have been blind. Refutation condition on the page: a stand-in whose reader outlasts both humanity and the systems in question. I have not searched for one.

**Why script.** The finding is a change of world, and the reader has to be the one who makes it.
