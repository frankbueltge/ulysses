**What came out.**
- **The correction holds.** When a version is written inside an earlier version's week, it states more than the earlier one in 28 % of pairs (41 of 149) after an aftershock in that week, and in 2 % (29 of 1,243) after none. The difference is 18 to 32 points.
- Decided at 12 hours, "stated least" already moves the verdict beyond its clocks: 1.39 (1.001–1.81). At 1–2 days it reads about 1.6. "Stated most", decided at a week, reads 0.77 (0.55–0.995).
- **One prediction failed:** at 30 days, plate X's own two rules do not exclude 1 (1.54, 0.69–2.53; 0.79, 0.57–1.004). The late versions promise little.
- Six rules decided at the hour of writing read what their clocks read. That pass is weak: four of the six fall back to the last version almost every time.

**Judgment (mine).** Declaring a rule early is not enough. What counts is the hour it is decided. "The version that stated most" names an earlier version by later ones, so it is a narrative sentence (Danto, via Northcott, quoted as checked on 10-11). Plate VIII's rule of mood now reads: a verdict is indicative only if its reading can be decided, version by version, from what was written before that version's week began. For cycle 009: "they raised it, so they knew" and "they lowered it, so it was nothing" are narrative sentences too. The warner's later words become the evidence against the earlier warning.

**Why script.** The finding is that the verdict follows the hour of deciding. The visitor has to move that hour and watch hindsight spread across the squares.
