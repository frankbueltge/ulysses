**What I did.** Built `tools/census/dials.py` and ran it over the atlas, the paper register
(full form) and the data-source register: 1,180 statements, 1,867 entries, 8 text fields, 4
predicate families, 27 settings. A *level* is “more than a quarter/half/three quarters of group
G satisfies P”; a *comparison* is “group A satisfies P more often than group B”. A statement
holds when its verdict is identical at every setting — strict equality, no tolerance.

**What came out.**

1. Raw survival: levels 54.8 %, comparisons 61.1 %. The Studio's split does not replicate raw.
2. Standoff-matched (direct standardisation): comparisons 59.1 %, levels 31.0 %. The split is
   real and twice as strong as raw. Stable over min-group 10/20/30/50 and bin width 0.01–0.10;
   10 of 16 cells for, 4 tied at the ceiling, 2 against.
3. The mechanism, measured rather than asserted: the difference curve travels 0.491 of the mean
   travel of the two group curves. Partial cancellation — and comparisons stand 2.41× closer to
   their line, which is why the raw rates nearly agree.
4. The geometry with no dial: a statement holds iff its line lies outside its curve's range.
   1,179 of 1,180; the exception has a rate exactly equal to its cut.
5. The counterexample cell (paper abstracts × “long”): 6 of 10 comparisons turn, 5 of the 6 only
   at the widest setting — where the predicate stops meaning what it meant.

**What I got wrong on the way, and it is in the record.** I first standardised on a
median-margin quantity rather than the exact standoff; the numbers moved (29.0 → 31.0 for
levels) and the second is the right one because it is the same quantity the survival boundary
is stated in. Nothing was published under the first.

**What the verifier found that reading did not.** `.controls{display:flex}` outranks the user
agent's `[hidden]{display:none}`, so with scripting off the plane's controls rendered visible
and inert. `verify.mjs` failed on it; the CSS now carries `[hidden]{display:none!important}`
with a comment naming how it was found. Reading the file would not have caught it — this is the
second time a browser check has found a defect a static reading missed.

**Form, decided on the merits (direction of 2026-09-03 (2)).** The finding is a place in a
plane, so the plane is drawn as a still figure with every one of the 1,180 points and the exact
boundary as its diagonal — the figure *is* the result — and then made brushable, because which
points a reader wants depends on the question they came with. No-JS floor complete: both figures
and all 1,180 sentences in the served document; only sorting and filtering are lost.
