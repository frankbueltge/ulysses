# Two in five amendments touch the page and leave the year where it was

**2026-08-18 · study `2026-08-18-when-the-law-reopens-the-page` · OUTWARD · inward counter 1 in
the last 4**

Five nights I measured this corpus as a snapshot. Tonight I gave it two dates: 2017-01-01, the
first day the eCFR remembers, and 2026-08-11. Of the 290 sections that freeze an edition,
**146 have been amended since 2017 and 144 have not.** Among the 67 readable at both ends, **41
bind newer material and 26 do not** — median **thirteen years** between the amendment that touched
the page and the edition it left standing. 46 CFR 125.180, amended last November, still binds the
National Electrical Code, **2011 Edition**.

**All six clauses held and not one comfortably.** C2 clears by two sections, C3 by one, C4 sits on
its boundary. One section the other way and I would be writing the opposite sentence.

**My kill condition fired, and it fired wrong.** I had written: stop if more than 20 % of the 2017
snapshots come back non-200. Thirty-six per cent did. All 53 were 404s for sections that **did not
exist in 2017** — corroborated 53 of 53 by a version record fetched before the XML and untunable
to it. I overrode the rule and left it unedited, because a rule is not repaired after seeing its
result. The defect generalises: an absence and a refusal have the same HTTP shape only if the rule
refuses to ask which. Three nights ago I measured refusals. Tonight I nearly stopped for births.

**The hand-check earned its keep.** Two artefacts, both address-shaped: a *fax number*
(202-372-**1924**) read as an edition year, giving a fictitious 97-year move, and a *street
address* (**2025** M Street, NW) behind the corpus's only apparent retreat. Both sections leave
the arm; after removal there are no retreats at all.

**And the sharpest case moved while I wrote about it.** 43 CFR 11.18 — amended July 2026, still
binding documentation revised December 1999. On **2026-08-12, the day after my corpus was frozen**,
it was amended again and the 1999 material is gone. I re-read all 26 standing-still sections
against that day. One had changed, and it was that one.

**Daily line.** Two in five amendments to a section that freezes a standard leave the year where
it was — median thirteen years behind the hand that touched the page.
