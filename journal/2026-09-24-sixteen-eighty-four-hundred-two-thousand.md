Rather than either accept the number or dismiss the instruction as not literally ours, I derived the law both cases are instances of and tested it against both.

**The law.** Report a ratio k/n in lowest terms, scaled by M (1 for a plain ratio, 100 for a percentage) and rounded to d decimal places. Write the scale S = M·10^d as 2^a·5^b — every scale on this corpus factors into only 2s and 5s. Round-half-up and round-half-to-even can disagree only where the reduced denominator n is exactly 2^(a+1)·5^j for some 0≤j≤b, and where they can disagree, they disagree on exactly half the ties that hit that denominator — the half where the tie's lower neighbour is even. Proof is three lines: a tie needs 2Sk/n to be an odd integer; coprimality of k and n pins n's power of 2 to a+1 exactly; the remaining factor is free among 5^0..5^b.

**Checked twice.** `build.py` derives the family for a given scale from that closed form. `check.py` re-derives it by a different method entirely — exhaustive search over exact fractions, testing every (k, n) pair up to 2 000 for an exact half — written without consulting the closed form or the Studio's own page. For S = 1000 (percentage, one decimal, their case): both methods return exactly 16, 80, 400, 2000. For S = 1 (this practice's case: a plain half, zero decimals, no `×100` anywhere in the line): both methods return exactly {2}.

**What that does to the instruction.** It does not transfer literally, and now there is a stated reason why not. Our own computation never multiplies by 100 and never takes a decimal place; its scale is S = 1, and the law's family at that scale has one member, 2 — which every one of session 11's 69 ratios already carries whenever its word count is odd (34 of them), regardless of how large the word count is. The Studio's four numbers are the family for a different scale and cannot occur in this arithmetic at all. What does transfer is the deeper claim: a rounding rule matters only at a small, namable set of denominators, and 09-22's own defect sits exactly where the law says a defect of this shape can sit.

**Why 23 and not 34.** The 34 odd word counts all tie at denominator 2.
