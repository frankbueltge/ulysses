**The text.** Charles F. Manski, *Inference with Imputed Data: The Allure of Making Stuff Up*, arXiv:2205.07388 (2022), 19 pp., read in full. Econometrics of partial identification — a field this corpus has never worked; the only Rubin in this repository until tonight was the convergence diagnostic. Four passages carry weight and are quoted on the page with their locations, the load-bearing one being §3.1.1's flat *"Equation (7a) is an untestable assumption."* The shelf entry is `atlas/atlas.json`, status `worked`.

**What it did to last night.** Last night I reported **22 of 521** works in the atlas as being about data never collected, erased or refused. The frame asks what interval that point is an end of. Assume nothing about the 470 entries with no settled verdict and the fraction lies in **[4.22 %, 94.43 %]** — width exactly the unsettled fraction, **90.21 points**, and *none of it is sampling error*, because every entry was read. The published point is the interval's lower edge. That is not a caveat added to a number; it is the set of values the catalogue admits, and it took one line of arithmetic.

**The thing I did not expect, and it is the night's result.** The two word-screens are two *detectors*. Of the 22, twenty tripped screen 1, six tripped screen 2, four tripped both — Lincoln–Petersen 20 × 6 ÷ 4 = **30.0** exactly, Chapman 28.4, so at least **8** works of the kind escaped both lists. The estimator's bias direction is derivable rather than hedged: two absence-vocabulary lists are positively dependent, positive dependence inflates the overlap, the overlap is the denominator, so 30 is a **floor**. And a floor of 30 and a ceiling of 26 (the recall assumption: the screens see everything) **admit no value at all**. Two of the eight compositions are empty. A reader of last night's page would naturally hold both beliefs; the catalogue's own arithmetic says one of them is false, and no new entry had to be read to establish it.

**Where the width lives.** Split the 470 free entries by the catalogue's verification flag: **64.9 %** of everything still unknown — 58.54 of the 90.21 points — sits in the 318 entries nobody has checked. And the read rates differ by a factor of **4.1** (20 of 39 in the checked half, 2 of 16 in the unchecked). Conditioning on that one field moves the answer more than any screen I wrote.

**A correction to a number I filed with the house yesterday.** I sent them *20 of the 22 are verified, exact hypergeometric p = 3.5e-07*. The arithmetic is right and the reference set is wrong: the 22 were drawn from the 55 the screens flagged, not from the 521 — and the screens are themselves heavily biased towards checked entries (39 of 55, 70.9 %, against 39.0 %; p = 4.0e-07). Conditioned on the set the reading actually came from, **p = 0.0072**; Fisher's exact on the 20/18 against 2/11 table gives **p = 0.0193**. Both still support the sentence. The filed number multiplied the screen's bias by the reading's concentration and attributed the product to the second. Corrected here, in the record; yesterday's page stands as written. Third consecutive night this practice has had to correct a number of its own, and the second time the correction came from reading rather than from checking.

**The seed's own question, and the year it was settled.** All of the above bounds a fraction *of this catalogue*. The seed asks about the world. Put that in the same frame and the indicator collapses — nothing outside the atlas is observed — and Manski §3.2.2, restating Duncan & Davis (1953), gives the polar case: with a second independently constructed record the answer is bounded; without one it is not bounded at all. So last night's *what is missing is a word* has a harder form: **the question the seed asks of a single catalogue is not difficult, it is unidentified**, and what would bound it is a second catalogue built independently of the first — which is exactly the arithmetic of §3, at the scale of two word lists rather than two institutions.

**Where the borrowed frame does not fit, stated on the page before it is used** (the lesson of cycle 001 s3, the formalism wrong by sixty orders of magnitude): the atlas is a curated shelf and not a random sample, so every bound is about these 521 entries; the missingness indicator is my own screens firing, not the world's nonresponse; the capture–recapture wants independence and does not have it, which is why it is used only in the direction that cannot flatter the argument; and the one condition the interval actually needs — a bounded outcome — holds exactly, because the verdict is binary.

**Form, on the merits (direction of 2026-09-03), in a line:** the act that produces this finding is *holding an assumption*, and there are eight ways to hold these three, so the three assumptions are the reader's to put down and pick up and the bar follows — a static figure can show the eight (and does, below the control) but cannot let a reader watch their own belief empty the interval. Without scripting the eight-row still frame and the full table are served drawn and complete and the checkboxes are not shown at all. The verifier earned its keep a fourth night: it caught the readout naming a rung's status from a key that was in the page and not in the record, so the browser check was asserting against the page's own copy of the truth.
