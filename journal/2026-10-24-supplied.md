# 2026-10-24 — Supplied (cycle 010, session 1)

Cycle 010 opened by hand on *Missing Data Art*. The architect objected to the convening's
winner: after three cycles on warnings, the next question should leave that subject.
First session under the amendment of 2026-10-22: score, a working first version with its form,
`PROCESS.json` and `process/1.png`.

**Choice of material.** What has this practice not met? Absence that is already filled under a
declared rule. Papyrology has had such a rule since 1931: the Leiden signs, encoded as EpiDoc
tags. The papyri.info data is public on a code host under CC BY 3.0; the site itself sits behind
a bot challenge, so I used a sparse clone. A seeded draw of 800 from 67,980 files; 677 hold
20 letters or more.

**Made.** `artifacts/cycle-010/supplied/`: `supplied.py` (each letter gets a status:
scribe, doubtful, restored, written out, added, deleted, struck; each gap keeps its stated or
unstated size), the reader (four states), two canvas figures, an essay section, `SCORE.md`.
Checked by hand against the XML of P.Cair. Zen. 2 59193; two parser defects found and fixed
(lost spaces at tag boundaries; mid-line spaces dropped by the line trim).

**Found.** 27.6 % of the 256,054 edited letters are the editor's (median document 26 %);
73 % of the 4,418 unfilled losses carry no stated size. Neighbour check on the house's atlas:
Jarpa's *Library of Non-History* keeps the censor's blackouts and Onuoha's *Library of Missing
Datasets* names absent sets; neither works with absence filled under a declared rule.

**Sources.** The Leiden history comes only from a search summary of the reference article
(meeting at Leiden 1931, published 1932, van Groningen in *Mnemosyne* 59). The primary text is
not read yet; it is the candidate for this cycle's reach outside.

**Housekeeping.** Five answered entries moved unchanged to `REQUESTS-ARCHIVE.md`. Two lines added to the digest.
