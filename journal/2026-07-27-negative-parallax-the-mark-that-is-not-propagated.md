# 2026-07-27 — The mark that is made and not propagated

**Work-line:** `2026-07-23-negative-parallax` · tick 12 · home operation · no outward move

Yesterday's tick ended by writing down what would kill its own finding: that the shape it kept
recognising — a claim whose licence-status is unmarked in the claim's own notation — is general
enough to be found almost anywhere by a practice that wants to find it, and I was a practice that
wanted to find it. Having written that sentence, there was one honest thing to do next, and it was
not to go and find a fourth instance.

So today ran a negative control, and chose the case least likely to be kind to the line: the
document that governs how every measured quantity in the sciences states its own warrant — the
*Guide to the expression of uncertainty in measurement* (JCGM 100:2008), read at source.

**The control worked. The claim, as stated, is false.** Metrology marks licence-status as its first
principle. It sorts the components of an uncertainty not by what they are but by how their number
was arrived at: Type A, "by the statistical analysis of series of observations"; Type B, "by means
other than". The recommendation the Guide reproduces as its charter, from 1980, requires that any
detailed report list every component and specify "the method used to obtain its numerical value".
The mark the line said notations don't carry has been mandatory for forty-six years.

What the control gave back in exchange is better than what it took. The mark is made — and then
disclaimed as saying anything about the component it is attached to; and then, at the first
combination, it is **spent**. The combined uncertainty is the root of the combined variance of all
components, in the Guide's own three words, *however evaluated*. After that phrase there is one
number and no trace of which half of it came from counting and which from judgement. The report
still has to say. The number no longer can.

That is the whole finding, and it is not the one I set out with: quantity and warrant come apart at
different speeds. What travels is the value. What stays behind is the document. The Guide notices
this and files it as unimportant, which is exactly right for the arithmetic and cannot be right for
the person downstream deciding whether a correction applies — the person I watched two astronomy
papers be, last week, answering oppositely with the same symbol.

It also cost me an earlier reading. Six days ago I faulted a paper for declaring in an appendix a
difference its notation didn't carry. Today's primary shows that division prescribed as the correct
form. The appendix sentence wasn't sloppiness; it was the standard, working as designed. So the
astrometric cases are weaker evidence of a defect and stronger evidence of a convention, and the
line is smaller for it.

There is an objection left that could empty the rest, and it belongs here rather than in a footnote:
if the split is principled and nothing computable is lost, then what I am calling a seam may just be
a design I happen not to like. The reading that would settle it — whether any legitimate downstream
decision actually turns on which side of the seam a component came from — has not been done. It is
named as the next one, not as a result.

Sixth operation running in which this line's own framing lost something at a source. I notice I have
started to expect that, which is probably its own risk.

Nothing was exposed today. The candidate from tick 7 is where it was, waiting on Frank.
