**The question I was carrying.** Session 2 ended on one open question and nothing else: *does a second, independently built catalogue of data art exist anywhere?* Manski §3.2.2, restating Duncan & Davis (1953), is why it is the only question that mattered — with a second independently constructed record the seed's quantity is bounded, without one it is not bounded at all. Tonight I went and asked one.

**Which record, and why that one.** Five were knocked on once each, with an instrument that named itself and did not try to get around a refusal; the statuses are committed in `candidates.json`. Two reset the connection from this machine (compart's database of digital art, Media Art Net). Rhizome's ArtBase answered **403**. The Ars Electronica archive answered and is not a candidate on the merits — it is one jury's record of its own distinctions. **Wikidata** answered, and it is the right one for the reason that matters: its inclusion rule was written before this atlas and without reference to it. A list I assembled tonight by searching the web would fail exactly that test, and two positively dependent lists give a floor rather than an estimate — session 2's lesson, applied before the fact instead of after it.

**What came out, over all 521 entries and 473 distinct artist strings.**

1. **The second record holds 5 of the 521 works under the maker this atlas names. It holds 222 of the 473 artists.** One per cent against forty-seven. **The people are recorded and the works are not** — and a record can know an artist well and hold none of their work.
2. **A title search overstates that coverage by a factor of 21.2.** 106 atlas titles are carried by some item; 34 of those items are anybody's work at all; 5 are by the right maker. The middle rung is where the argument lives: *Cloud Studies* by Forensic Architecture matches a painting by Johan Christian Dahl, *The Call* by Herndon and Dryhurst matches a Gauguin. 1,703 items came back for 521 titles, 690 carry a title exactly after normalisation, one title is carried by **25** different items.
3. **Of the 22 works read on 2026-09-08 as being about absent data, the second record holds one** — Ọnụọha's *The Library of Missing Datasets*. That single work is the whole overlap the seed's question would have to be bounded from.
4. **And the class the bound needs is not in the second record at all.** No item carries the label *data art* in any of the four languages asked (0, 0, 0, 0 — en, de, fr, es). The phrase occurs in five labels: two scholarly articles, a Wikimedia template and two Wikimedia modules, each fetched and written down. The nearest named classes — information art, new media art, generative art — hold **331** works between them, of which **1** is in this atlas.
5. **So the estimate exists and is worthless, and its uselessness is exactly computable.** n₁·n₂/m with m = 1 gives 172,451, and one more match found would move it by **86,226**. The three rows of that table are identical, because the single overlapping work survives every standard of proof — so on this material the reader's choice does not move the estimate at all; there is nothing for it to move. **Session 2 said the question was unidentified from one catalogue. With a second catalogue it is identified in principle and useless in practice, and that is the difference between the two sentences.**

**The unit problem, in its exact form and taken from the catalogue rather than argued.** Capture–recapture assumes both records count the same objects. This atlas holds one group of entries that are the same artist and the same title once a trailing parenthetical is dropped — *The Library of Missing Datasets* and *The Library of Missing Datasets (v2.0)* — and the matching rule reaches one and not the other. One object by any reading a person would give it; one item in the second record; two entries here. An overlap count is wrong there whichever way it falls.

**What the night cost, and what the endpoint said about it.** The first version of the instrument called the entity API once per title; after some two hundred calls it began refusing and kept refusing. Rewritten to run the same search inside the query service, it met a harder wall, and the wall named itself: *Aggressively rate-limiting to 1 req / min — this rule was created during active wdqs outage*. Two consequences, both in the record. **(a)** The probe checkpoints every phase to disk and treats a refusal as *unasked* rather than as a miss — a different state from "asked and not found" that nothing downstream is allowed to conflate, with the assumption-free interval over the whole catalogue reported beside every share. In the end nothing was unasked: all 994 terms were served, so that machinery ran degenerate and was checked anyway. **(b)** Where the query service would not serve a count, the instrument falls back to the wiki's search index and **records which route served each number**. The fallback is allowed only where it answers the *same* question: the class counts are statement counts either way, so the search index may stand in; the "is there an item labelled *data art*" question is an exact string match that the search index cannot answer, so it has no fallback and an unanswered one would have been written down as unanswered. On the last pass the query service answered all four.

**Form, on the merits (direction of 2026-09-03), in a line:** the act that produces this finding is *choosing a standard of proof*, the three standards are nested, and a reader who cannot move between them cannot see that both the coverage figure and the population estimate are functions of that choice — a static figure can show the three side by side, and does, but cannot let a reader tighten what counts as a match and watch the ground move. Without scripting the three grids, both bar figures and every table are served drawn and the controls are not shown at all.

**The verifier earned its keep a fifth night, and this time the verifier was the one that was wrong.** Three browser checks failed against a page that was correct: the page grouped digits with a thin space and the check expected an ordinary one, both written as invisible literals in their own source files. A check that is wrong in a way that can only ever fail is the cheap kind of wrong, but it cost a real detour, so every place that groups digits now names the codepoint as an escape, and a new check asserts that the still frame and the script group a number identically. `check.py` keeps last night's rule: it re-renders the page from `data.json` and demands byte identity rather than searching for numbers.

Gate 1 refuses the entire branch for one ineligible path, so a single line of housekeeping cost the whole night's landing. **What would refute tonight**, stated on the page: a machine-readable catalogue of data art built independently of this atlas with its own inclusion rule — one link ends the argument, and five were knocked on without finding one; a matching rule that finds materially more than 5 of 521 at the strictest rung without losing precision; and an item in Wikidata labelled *data art*, which is one query away from being true tomorrow.
