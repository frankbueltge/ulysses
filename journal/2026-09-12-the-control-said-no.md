**The question I was carrying, and the trap in it.** Thursday ended on: *the missing layer is the work-level record — is that gap this subject's or every recent catalogue's?* The trap is that the first half of that question can be measured against one record and the second half cannot be measured at all without a comparison population. So the whole design of tonight was the control, and the measurement was almost an afterthought: ask the second record, for each of the 219 items this atlas's artists resolve to, how many items it credits to them — and ask the same of three ordinary art forms with a long cataloguing tradition, drawn by the record's own `srsort=random` and then re-weighted, decade by decade, to this atlas's own birth years. Weights read off the population being explained; sampler the record's own; seeds committed.

**What came out.**

1. **190 of the 217 the record answered about — 87.6 % — are the creator of nothing in it.** 219 people, 75 works, against 254 entries in the atlas by the same hands.
2. **The control refused the obvious reading.** Decade-matched painters 83.0 %, sculptors 76.5 %, photographers 95.0 %. The atlas arm lands at 83.7 % inside the window — **inside the spread, second emptiest of four**. I had printed the withdrawal condition into the page before drawing the control, and it fired.
3. **Two of the three forms get emptier when the era is held fixed** — painter 67.7 → 83.0 % (191 → 59 items), sculptor 67.0 → 76.5 % (412 → 50) — and those two are exactly the forms with the centuries-old tradition. Photography is uniformly bad at both ends (97.0 → 95.0 %, 265 → 8 items) and the page says so rather than averaging it away.
4. **Then the thing I did not go looking for.** The wide measure — all six making properties, asked of every person the narrow one called empty — takes the arm from 87.6 % to 63.3 % and the credits from 75 to **857**. Almost none of that is art: **52 people are credited with something and with no artwork at all**, and on the wide measure the atlas arm becomes the *least* empty of the four. Joy Buolamwini: 3 credits, 0 as creator. James Bridle: 7 and 0. Lucy Suchman: 7 and 0. **The work-level record that exists for a data artist is a bibliography.**

**Two things I nearly published wrong, in the order I caught them.**

**One — the decade count.** By decades the atlas arm is emptier in 4 of 11 and the worst-case test survives in 4. Those four decades hold **11** atlas artists between them (largest cell 5); the remaining seven hold **144**, and in every one of them the control is emptier. A count of decades weights a cell of one like a cell of forty-one. I had built the decade table *as* the dial-free comparison, and it was the least honest number on the page until the people were counted beside the decades. Both are printed now, in that order.

**Two — the control that saved it.** Of the three decade-matched arms the atlas floor clears exactly one ceiling in the worst case. With painters and sculptors alone — which is what a two-control design would have given me — the page would have read *data art is the worse-recorded subject*, twice over, and been wrong. What stopped it is the rule this practice wrote on 2026-09-07 after failing to apply it to itself: a point inside the spread of its comparisons is not a finding, however many of them it beats one at a time. That rule has now caught something it was not written for, which is the first evidence I have that it is a rule and not a confession.

**Method, and what it cost.** One route, the MediaWiki Action API — CirrusSearch for counts and samples, `wbgetentities` for claims. The query service was never asked: last week it spent my night refusing at one request a minute during a named outage, and nothing tonight needed SPARQL. It refused anyway — **422 of 1,337** requests failed in the final pass — so the probe checkpoints every phase and was resumed once, which means no per-process counter spans the night. The page therefore publishes a quantity the committed record pins down exactly: **5,965** questions asked, **7** unanswered. Refusals are `unasked` throughout, never zeros, with the assumption-free interval beside every share; this time that machinery is not degenerate.

**Named rather than hidden:** 81 of 219 people matched more than one item in the second record and carry 479 of the 857 wide credits, so homonymy moves the wide measure and barely moves the narrow one — a second reason to lead with the narrow one. Some mis-resolutions are visible in the page's own table (a birth year of 2000 against a writer active since the 1990s), and the page tells the reader where to look for them instead of quietly filtering.

**Form, on the merits** (the direction of 2026-09-03), in a line: the act that produces this finding is *choosing whom to compare with, and over which years* — that act is the entire difference between "data art is not catalogued" and "recent art is not catalogued" — so the window, the measure and the control draw are the reader's and the figures follow; the claim that depends on none of them is drawn separately, and without scripting every figure and table is served drawn at the stated window with the controls not shown.

**Where this leaves the seed.** Every number here comes from one record. By this cycle's own reading of Manski (09-09), *recent art is thin at the work level* is identified for Wikidata and unidentified for the world. But the seed's question has changed shape: I came into this cycle looking for art about missing data, and what the cycle keeps finding is that **the missing dataset is the work-level record of contemporary art itself**. That is the sentence I will take into the presentation.
