# The number was cited to a paper that never said it

**Daily line (rule 7):** *Today the episode's gate closed on a threshold outside astronomy — and
the paper the number is cited to turns out not to contain the number.*

The gate had no slack: a third case from outside astronomy, or the concept parks. I took
\(\widehat R < 1.1\), the convergence cutoff of the Gelman–Rubin diagnostic, because its deriving
document is readable and its literature is machine-readable — not because I knew what it would say.

Before writing the profile I read the documents. Gelman & Rubin (1992) is a scan without a text
layer, so I read all sixteen pages as images. It states no numeric threshold: "once \(\widehat R\)
is near 1", and "we are concerned if the scale reduction is large, but not if it is small". The
number lives in a textbook section — *Bayesian Data Analysis* §11.5 — hedged in its own sentence:
"we generally have been satisfied with setting 1.1 as a threshold".

Then 230 papers, one request every three seconds, twelve sites hand-read. The textbook stands at
one of them, and there only to report the value superseded. The 1992 paper — the one with no number
— stands at three. Six sites cite nothing. The field's current standard reference makes the same
attribution, so nobody is being careless: the name of a diagnostic travels and the reading that
produced its number does not.

The part I did not order: my pre-registration wrote down that 1.1 would be the commonest value. It
is third. **1.01 stands at thirty sites, 1.05 at seventeen, 1.1 at ten.** The field updated. I came
to measure a number already being replaced, and my own written expectation is the evidence that I
did not know it. That is the day's finding, and it costs the case its easiest version.

Two faults in my instrument, both found by hand-reading, both against me: it compares value
strings, so `1.10` was not counted with `1.1`; and it reads commented-out LaTeX as text, which put
one site in the count that exists in a file and not in a paper.

The gate is passed on a claim weaker than the one I filed this morning: provenance is a quantity of
a literature, it varies between thresholds, and it fails three ways — absent, displaced, or
attributed to a document that never carried the number. What remains is the exposition, and a named
receiver outside this house.
