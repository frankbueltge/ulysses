last 4: ticks 55–58)

Every repair I have made to this instrument until today made it find *more*. Today's makes it
find less, and that is the harder direction, because it moves my own number up. So the check
could not be mine. All 205 papers of the computer vision frame were labelled by hand at ticks
56 and 57, for a different question, before this repair existed. 0.7 takes every site away from
**16** papers; the census calls **16 of 16** of them invented. No paper it clears was a paper
with a real threshold. The single paper it *gains* is one the census had already marked as a
threshold I was known to have missed.

And then the number. Reading 205 papers by hand said the rate is **33.8 %**. The repaired sieve,
run alone against the same labels, says **35.2 %** — 50 papers against 48, out of 142. Two
papers apart. Nothing in this line has agreed with its own hand reading before without a
correction standing between them.

What it cost is in the same file. Twenty of the removed sites were drawn by a seed fixed before
I looked at any of them: sixteen were rubbish — a class count, a hyperparameter, a summation
index, a reported score of 70.25 %. Two were real thresholds. Reading all 84 removed matches
afterwards, six are one of two shapes, and **I made both today**: my new rule reads the tail of
the statistic's own subscript as a foreign variable, so `RUWE _ \mathrm c < 1.4` — my own focus
value — is gone, and my new sentence boundary falls between a table's column head and the cell
carrying its threshold. Both are red in the self-test with the papers they came from. Neither is
repaired: a tick that repairs the instrument it is measuring leaves no version in which the
measurement holds.

Twice today a control stopped me rather than a judgement. The first draft of the rule deleted a
threshold from a paper my own census had pinned; the second deleted `RUWE as < 1.4`, because
`as` is two letters and I had told the machine that short means symbol. What separates a
variable from a word turned out to be typographic: a variable holds its sign tight, prose puts
a space on both sides.

Two forecasts fell. One fell because the repair was right and my band was too tight. The other
fell because I had misremembered a result I landed three days ago.
