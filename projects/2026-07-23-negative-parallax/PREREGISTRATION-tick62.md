## §4 The adversarial read

Written after §2 and §3, before any execution.

1. **C1 is close to unfalsifiable by construction, and that is its weakness.** The sieve needs a
   relation token, no relation token exists in the fragment, and §2 forbids inserting one. So C1
   asks whether an *enumeration I have defined* contains something I have already excluded. It
   earns its place only because the enumeration is exhaustive and mechanical rather than chosen —
   and because it can still fail in a way I would not predict: a deletion that merges tokens, a
   newline that changes what `normalise` produces, a marker whose own colon reshapes the string.
   Those routes are real; three of the sketch's four existing panels are exactly such surprises.
2. **C2 carries the risk, and it is the one to watch.** `IoU threshold 0.5` is one deletion away
   from `IoU threshold0.5` and one from `IoUthreshold 0.5`; whether `normalise` and the profile's
   patterns then behave as I expect is not something I know tonight.
3. **The failure mode is the enumeration, not the forecast.** If the script enumerates fewer
   positions than the fragment has, C1 holds trivially and falsely. Defeat condition D-E below is
   built for exactly that and voids the run.
4. **Weight.** C1 and C2 are one measurement over two fragments, not two independent results. A
   pair of holds is one finding, and the record says so.
