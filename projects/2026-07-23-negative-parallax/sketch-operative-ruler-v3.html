The operative ruler — the border qualified

One measured parallax. You cannot touch the number — only the error bar you are willing to claim for it. The axis below is not blank: it is carved by limits the discipline published. Both limits carry the sentence their authors wrote next to them. Move the precision, and watch where your fixed number comes to rest on someone else's map.

v3 of the medium-necessity sketch, revised 2026-07-30 after this project's own record (TRACE tick 15) found that v2 presented as fixed cartography a limit its source calls an illustration. The verdict captions and concluding glosses removed at v2 stay removed (§5.4 test 4: an interaction must alter the situation, not reveal a prewritten explanation); what v3 adds is not a judgement of your value but the qualification its own author attached to the border. Two published limits, two purposes, one axis: Fabricius et al. 2021 (A&A 649, A5, §3.2) use φ/σφ < −5 as an explicitly illustrative selection limit and report 3.04 million EDR3 sources beyond it (against 192.21 million past +5, giving the ≈1.6% matched-negative estimate that the +5 mirror shows); Rybizki et al. 2021 (MNRAS 510, 2597; arXiv:2101.11641) independently define their training sample of “manifestly bad astrometric solutions” as “parallax that is negative at > 4.5 sigma”. Neither paper draws the other's line; placing both on one axis is this practice's own juxtaposition, made so that the border can be seen as a choice rather than a fact. Further sources: “r > 0 by definition” — Bailer-Jones 2015 (PASP 127, 994); zero-point offset −17μas, published as discretionary — Lindegren et al. 2021 (A&A 649, A4). The paragraph that reaches the “clearly spurious” verdict also disqualifies this axis's own unit on the way — “Formal uncertainties can, however, be misleading. They are based on the assumption that the source is undisturbed” — and that tension is left standing, not resolved. Values as read at primaries (TRACE ticks 2, 8, 15, 16).
