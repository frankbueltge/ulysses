## The five topoi

**Connectivity.** It connects at two ends. Outward: 29 CFR 1910.6 is a live section of US federal
regulation, retrievable by anyone, and the census is reproducible from the stored XML and the
parser in this directory. Inward: it is the work-line's question at a site where the pointer is
legally mandatory instead of merely conventional — a use site that *must* carry its deriving
document, and still leaves the document unreadable.

**Consistency.** The study did what its pre-registration said, including the part that cost it.
Rules, forecasts, the adversarial read and the blind step were written before the parser existed;
the blind step then voided four of six clauses and they are recorded VOID rather than repaired into
passes. The one repaired run is verified against a second sample the repair had not seen, and the
one residual fault found afterwards is reported in the text rather than patched out of the number.

**Function-testing.** It tested an instrument and the instrument lost. A 19 % misparse rate sat
behind a median and a decade histogram that looked entirely credible. That is the finding with the
longest reach: in this material, plausibility of the aggregate carries no information about the
correctness of the parse.

**New-production.** The fact that OSHA enforces decades-old consensus editions is known and is not
claimed as new — its own rulemakings are cited. What is produced is the complete dated census of
the section, the 206-entry table behind it, and one number nobody had put a figure on tonight:
**zero** free routes to the incorporated text.

**Caution balance.** Rights are clean (a public-domain regulation; no incorporated document was
retrieved or reproduced). The claim is deliberately narrow: one section, one title, one night, and
the score names what was not tested — whether the frozen editions can still be bought at all.

## Why close rather than continue

The study was declared as one night's work and it finished the object it declared. Continuing would
mean opening the same census across other titles, which is a **line's** worth of work, not a
study's — and this practice already holds two work-lines, both past their bounds, one awaiting the
monthly review's disposition. Opening a third tonight would be capacity the mandate does not have
and a decision the review should make, not a tick.

**The reverse caution question — what does closing lose?** The momentum, and nothing else. Nothing
here is mid-flight: the source is stored with its hash, the parser runs, the residual fault is
named, and the extension is written down in the score for whoever takes it up. A study that has
answered its own question and recorded its instrument's failure rate does not lose anything by
being closed; it loses something by being kept open as a placeholder for enthusiasm.
