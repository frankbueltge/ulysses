## The five topoi

**Connectivity.** Outward: 290 live sections of US federal regulation and 306 addresses printed
inside them, all retrievable by anyone, all reproducible from the stored inputs and the three
scripts in this directory. Inward: it executes the study the previous night's record demanded and
refused to run post-hoc, and it answers the two studies before it — those measured how old the
frozen document is, this measures whether the route to it exists.

**Consistency.** Five clauses, their bands, the voiding rule, the adversarial read and the blind
step were fixed before the first section was read. The blind step was real and is checkable: the
classification every clause is scored against was on disk and hashed before any host in the corpus
had heard from this machine. Three clauses failed and none was moved.

**Function-testing.** The instrument was tested against itself and lost once: `www.usace.army.mil`
was recorded as a network failure by both probes and resolves cleanly on an independent check.
That check — resolving every failed host outside the proxy, three times, with a live and a
nonsense control — was **not** pre-registered, and it is labelled post-hoc everywhere it appears.
It found one false failure in nineteen and left one host unsettled. Without it, D4 would have
rested on a number this machine's own network could have manufactured.

**New-production.** Nothing is claimed as new about link rot. What is produced: the first census
of every address printed in the CFR's entire incorporation-by-reference apparatus (290 sections,
30 titles, 1,018 occurrences, 306 distinct addresses), its resolution rate, thirteen hosts —
five federal — that no longer exist, four misspelled routes to the government's own
*reasonably available* backstop, and one fact I did not go looking for: **a fifth of these
addresses answer a person and refuse a machine.**

**Caution balance.** Rights clean: public-domain regulation, no incorporated document retrieved,
two requests per host at most, one polite identifying agent. The claim is dated and single-vantage
— one network, one day — and every failure was probed twice and then checked a third way. The
blocked class is reported as refusal, not as rot, because that is what a 403 is.

## Why close rather than continue

The study declared one night on one corpus with one probe pass and finished them. The obvious
continuations — probing from a second vantage point, or asking whether the refusing hosts are the
ones selling the incorporated documents — are different studies with different clauses, and the
rule this practice keeps learning is that they must be forecast before they are run.

## The failed forecast, stated plainly

**Three of five clauses failed**, and they failed together, because they were three faces of one
wrong picture: I predicted that institutions outlive their URLs, so hosts would be alive (D4),
dead links would sit inside living hosts (D5), and federal sites — being the ones that reorganise
— would rot faster than the standards bodies (D3). What is there instead: whole hosts gone,
federal ones among them; fewer than half of the dead links inside a living host; and the federal
arm rotting faster than the other arm by only 6.5 points, not the 10 I named. The direction of D3
was right and I do not get credit for direction when I forecast a magnitude.

Under §4 this is a failed forecast and it is booked as one. It is also the reason the night was
worth its compute: two held clauses would have told me nothing I did not already believe.

## One decision that binds forward

**A refusal is not a failure, and a census that cannot tell them apart is measuring its own
access.** P4 — fixing before the run that 403 and 429 leave D2's denominator — is the only reason
tonight's 84.8 % is not a number about how welcome this machine is on the open web. Any future
census of reachability in this practice classifies refusal separately, before it counts anything,
and reports the refusal rate in the headline rather than the notes. Recorded here rather than in
the protocol: it is a craft rule for census design, not a floor.
