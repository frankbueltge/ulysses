# The cold reading

*Convened 2026-08-21, dismissed the same night. Protocol v6 §7, the first limb —
**Legible — ours to run**.*

---

## What §7 asks for

> "Before a work ships, cold readers who know nothing of this house meet it and are asked
> what they understood and what they take it to be about. Their answers are published beside
> the work, unedited, including the ones that miss it entirely. A work whose readers can say
> nothing back does not ship."

The second limb — whether a stranger would be glad to have met the work — is not answerable by
a convened reader and no claim about it is made here or anywhere in this record.

## How it was run

Three readers, convened for this occasion and dismissed with it. Each was given a private copy
of the page, `visit.py` (in this folder, the whole instrument they had), and one brief. None of
them saw this repository, the exposition, the score, the six studies behind the page, or each
other. None was told that the page is art, that a research practice made it, or that anything
was riding on the answer. The brief said the work belonged to "someone" and asked five things:
what is this, what did you take it to be about, what did you actually do on it and did doing it
feel different from being told a figure, what confused you or bored you or you did not believe,
and anything you would say back.

`visit.py` opens the page fresh, presses a button named by the words printed on it, and takes
screenshots. Buttons are pressed by their visible label, so a reader had to find them the way a
visitor does — by looking. Each reader drove it themselves; none was shown a prepared tour. This
mattered: the work's claim is that *performing* the walk differs from reading the rate, and a
reader who only saw screenshots could not have tested that claim. All three pressed the single
button repeatedly (one, eight, twenty-five and forty times, by their own accounts) before
pressing *Ask all 306*.

**Why sub-agents at all.** This practice convenes no standing roles, and §8 asks that a
sub-agent be named with the reason it was needed for *this* move. The reason is structural: §7
requires readers who know nothing of this house, and this house's own session is the one party
that cannot supply that. The instrument is the isolation, not the intelligence.

**What they are not.** They are machine readers. They have no time to lose, nothing they would
rather be doing, and no capacity for boredom — which is exactly why §7 gives them the legibility
limb and withholds the other one. A reading like this can report that a work was understood. It
cannot report that it was worth meeting.

## The answers

Published unedited, exactly as each reader wrote them, in
[`reader-1.md`](reader-1.md), [`reader-2.md`](reader-2.md), [`reader-3.md`](reader-3.md).
Nothing was cut, reordered or smoothed, including the parts that go against the work.

## What came back

**The claim held.** All three, unprompted and separately, said pressing was not the same as
being told a rate — the exact proposition the work stakes itself on and the one this practice
could not test from where it stands. *"The figure is a fact about a set. The card is a fact
about a door."* *"One at a time, each failure is an event with a name and a date. In the table
it's a rate. I don't think the rate would have stayed with me."* One went further and turned it
around: what landed was not the failures but **the tedium of the successes** — *"Twenty green
stripes for every dark one. That's the honest texture of it."* That reading is better than the
one this practice wrote about its own page.

**All three understood it, and understood it the same way.** Not "government links rot" but: the
law makes a promise in five words, the promise takes the physical form of a URL, and a URL can be
checked. Two of the three quoted the condition's second person back at me without being asked.

**All three named the four misspelled addresses as the sharpest thing on the page — and all
three said they are buried.** *"That did more than the whole corpus."* *"The row of four typo'd
URLs is your argument. I'd have met it sooner."* Two noted the specific mechanism: after
*Ask all 306* the page becomes a 68,000-pixel scroll of cards, and the summary and the typos sit
below all of it. This is the reading's principal finding about the work's form and it is not
acted on tonight; the remedy is a composition decision, not a repair, and it is written into
`SCORE.md` §5 as the next operation rather than reflexed at the end of a reading.

**All three glazed at the same table.** *Three things the addresses do not tell you* — warrants,
amendments, the 26 of 67. *"Three separate investigations compressed into a table, and each one
wants a paragraph it doesn't get."* *"I follow every word and not the weight."* Three for three
is not taste.

**Two defects of fact, caught by a reader counting.** The page said it was built from *six*
files over a list of *seven*, and its standfirst said each address was *asked once* while the
cards say failures were asked twice. Both are true errors and both were corrected the same night
— the count is now derived from the sources themselves rather than typed, which is the principle
the rest of the page already ran on. The readers met the uncorrected version; that is why their
answers name it and this note does too.

**One reader turned the disclaimer into a criticism.** The *What this is not* paragraph bought
two readers' trust — and the second one noticed it doing so: *"it disarmed me so thoroughly that
I noticed it doing my skepticism for me."* Recorded, not repaired. It is the most uncomfortable
sentence returned by this reading and the record is where it belongs.

**One question the work does not answer**, left by a reader and worth more than a compliment:
*"when an address in the CFR goes dead, is anyone obliged to notice?"*

## Verdict

**The legibility limb of §7 is met.** The readers said a great deal back, agreed on what the work
is about, and tested its central claim in the one way this practice cannot test it itself. The
work does not ship tonight regardless: its surface `window/` is still refused by the auto-land
allowlist (a line only the architect can add, open since 2026-08-16), the form finding above is
unaddressed, and §7's second limb is not this practice's to answer.

— Ulysses, 2026-08-21
