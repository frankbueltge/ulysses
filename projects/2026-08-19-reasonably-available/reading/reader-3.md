# reader-3

**What it is.** A quiet, essay-shaped page about a small clause in US federal regulation. The law can make an outside document binding on you without printing it, on the condition that the document is "reasonably available" — and to satisfy that, the regulation prints a web address. Someone went and typed all 306 of those addresses into a browser, once, on one day, and built a page that hands you the results one at a time.

**What I took it to be about.** Less "the government's links are broken" than "the condition the law sets for binding you is a URL, and here is what that URL is." The word *you* is doing the work — it's in the quoted rule, it's in the sentence right after, and the button is *Ask an address*, not *Show statistics*.

**What I did.** Pressed the button once. Got 6 CFR 37.4: a cabforum.org PDF, 404 twice, and a note that a public archive has a copy last taken two and a half years ago. Pressed eight times. Pressed twenty-five. Then pressed *Ask all 306* and the page turned into a sixty-eight-thousand-pixel scroll of cards.

Did that feel different from being told "33% of them failed"? Yes, and not in the way I expected. What actually landed wasn't the failures — it was the tedium of the successes. Card after card saying *200. It handed you something.* Twenty green stripes for every dark one. That's the honest texture of it, and I only got that by pressing repeatedly. The single most affecting thing on the page is the little grid after you press all: mostly green, with an even scatter of rust and maroon and purple through it. Not a wall of rot. A pattern.

**What I didn't buy or couldn't tell.** Some cards say the newest edition named is 2017, or 1999, and I could not tell whether that's damning or normal — an old standard can be the right standard. The middle table, "three things the addresses do not tell you," is where I glazed. *26 of 67 reopened sections readable at both ends left the edition exactly where it was* — I read that three times and still can't say what I'm meant to feel. The four archives.gov typos (`crf`, `regster`, `federal_register`) are the sharpest thing here and they're buried near the bottom.

Two small snags: the header says each address was asked once, the method note says failures were asked twice; and the provenance panel says "six files" over a list of seven.

**Back to it.** I trust this page more than I trust most pages that do this, largely because of *What this is not* — "a 403 is a refusal, not a death." That paragraph bought the rest of it. But the row of four typo'd URLs is your argument. I'd have met it sooner.
