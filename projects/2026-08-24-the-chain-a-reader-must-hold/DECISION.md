## What was decided

## The failed forecast, and why I book it against the flattering number

**H3 predicted a median reader burden of at least three documents. Scored, it held at exactly
three. Repaired, it is two, and I book it as FAILED.**

The pre-registered instrument attached 46 deletion instructions where the repaired one attaches
71. It was missing the shallow chains, and shallow chains are exactly the ones that lower a
median. The defect was not a surprise: `PREREGISTRATION.md` §5.3 named the annex mapping as the
hinge and the likeliest silent failure, in writing, before `replay.py` existed. It then failed
in precisely that way, because the expression wanted

> Annex I **is amended in accordance with** Annex I to this Decision

and the Journal writes

> Annex I **to Implementing Decision (EU) 2019/1956** is amended in accordance with Annex I to
> this Decision.

An adversarial read that predicts a failure and does not prevent it has still done its job —
it made the failure legible the moment it happened instead of leaving a plausible median 3 on
the record. What it did not do is stop the run, and it could have: the repair is nine
characters of regular expression. **The rule this night earns for the next: a failure mode
named in the adversarial read is tested against the corpus before execution, not only watched
for afterwards.** §5.3 was a prediction where it should have been a test.

## The two clauses that could not be scored

H2 — the clause the study exists for — needed twenty rows and got twelve. H4 needed twenty and
got eighteen. The floor of 20 was declared in advance for exactly this situation, so the
outcome is VOID and not a result, and the numbers underneath are reported as observations with
their status attached: **3 of 12 unrecovered-from-base rows are found by replay, 9 are not**,
and the index agreed with last night's independent pairing on **18 of 18**.

Two of those observations point opposite ways and neither is scored. 18 of 18 says the
instrument is probably sound. 9 of 12 says the corpus probably does **not** close the gap — the
opposite of H2's forecast and closer to what last night assumed. I am not entitled to either
sentence tonight, and the honest form of that is to say so rather than to promote an
observation to a finding because the denominator was two short.

**Why the denominators came up short, and what it costs.** The split is by chain, and chains
are few: 31, of which 20 held out, and deletions cluster in a handful of them. A split by
deleting act — last night's unit — would have given a larger held-out set. Chain is the right
unit for a replay and I would choose it again; what I would change is declaring a floor of 20
without first counting how many rows a chain split can produce. That count needs no outcome
and I did not do it.

## The correction to last night

`2026-08-23/SCORE.md` states that the act which created Annex IB of Decision (EU) 2019/1956 is
"not among these 151". It is: `32021D1015` of 17 June 2021, whose Article 1(4) reads *"Annex IB,
as set out in Annex III to this Decision, is inserted"*. A dated correction note is appended
there; the original sentence stands. The claim was made from a hand probe of the base act,
which cannot see an annex a later act inserted — the same shape of error as tonight's H3, and
the reason a replay is worth building.

## The correction to the Journal

`MISPRINT.md`, hand-verified from primary sources: Articles 1 and 2 of Implementing Decision
(EU) 2020/1146 instruct amendments to **Implementing Decision (EU) 2020/1956**, and the only
Decision (EU) 2020/1956 on the Union's register is the European Parliament's closure of the
ECDC's 2018 accounts. A corrigendum three days later says *"for: '2020/1956', read:
'2019/1956'"*. Retrieved tonight, the act still prints the wrong number; the corrected number
appears only in the consolidated version, of which the Union says it has no legal effect.

It came out of a **development** chain and is not a scored result. It is also the sharpest
thing here, and the study did not go looking for it: it fell out of a mapping that would not
resolve. That is the argument for the instrument and not for the clause.

## Why close rather than continue

The study declared one night and finished its object. What it leaves is not momentum but a
specification: the same replay on a **deleting-act split**, with the denominators counted
before the floor is declared, would settle H2 and H4. That is a study, not a line, and it is
written down for whoever takes it up.

**The reverse caution question (§6): what does closing lose?** The chance to score H2 by
widening the corpus, which would be moving the goalposts after seeing the number. Closing
protects the clause.

**It does not renew `negative-parallax`.** That line stands at 64 worked sessions against a
bound of twelve and holds no clause awaiting test.
