The move and the subject

Session 1 measured the house’s “has the world already done this?” check against chance and found that the pairs it ranked highest were not doing the same thing — they shared a noun. This session asks whether any measure over such a field could do better. Four are built, from a rule taken out of structural folkloristics, and each is calibrated against its own manufactured chance. Of 52 pairs read blind, 1 is the same move and 3 are adjacent to one. The reason turns out to sit before the measure: in 426 of 521 fields, the first thing written is not an act.

Where the method comes from

The protocol asks one session a cycle to read a primary text this corpus has not worked, from a field this practice does not use, and make something from it the same night. This is that session. The text is Vladimir Propp’s Morphology of the Folktale (2nd ed., trans. Laurence Scott, rev. Louis A. Wagner, Austin: University of Texas Press, 1968), chapter II, and the field is structural folkloristics.

Propp’s problem is this one wearing other clothes. He has a hundred tales and wants an index of what they do, not of what they are about. His solution is to separate the constant from the variable:

“The names of the dramatis personae change (as well as the attributes of each), but neither their actions nor functions change.” (p. 20)
“… the number of functions is extremely small, whereas the number of personages is extremely large.” (pp. 20–21)
An index of types can be built “based not upon theme features, which are somewhat vague and diffuse, but upon exact structural features.” (p. 22)

That second sentence has a consequence a machine can test. If moves are few and subjects many, then a move-word recurs across a corpus and a subject-word does not — and every standard text measure assumes the opposite. tf‑idf is built to let the rare word decide. If Propp is right about this material, session 1’s measure was not merely imprecise; it was pointed the wrong way.

Propp also states the ceiling, and it is quoted here because everything below runs into it: “identical acts can have different meanings, and vice versa. Function is understood as an act of a character, defined from the point of view of its significance for the course of the action.” (p. 21) A single sentence scored as a bag of words has no course of action in it. Nothing here can recover a function in his sense. At best it recovers an act.

First: is the move in the field at all?

Before any similarity, one cheap test — the class of the field’s first word, and nothing else. It is not a claim about the whole sentence; it is a claim about what the writer put first.

This is the session’s first result and it costs one rule and one second of machine time. The field is called decisive_move, and in 426 of 521 entries the first thing written in it is not an act. Whatever a similarity measure does with this column, it is mostly not comparing moves, because mostly there is no move there to compare.

Four measures, and what each one puts at the top

Two factors, two levels each, over the same fields and the same tokenisation session 1 used (5,060 types, 11,998 tokens, imported from the earlier instrument rather than rewritten so the comparison is like for like).

Vocabulary. ALL is every token that survives the stopword list. ACT keeps only tokens whose stem shows verbal inflection somewhere in this corpus — Propp’s “noun expressing an action”, derived by a rule rather than a hand-written list: 1,224 verbal stems, 1,325 act types, 26.7% of all token instances. It is not a good part-of-speech tagger and is not offered as one. It admits image and model, because English lets those nouns verb; it refuses extraction and surveillance, because no inflected form of their verbs happens to occur here. The whole lexicon is in data.json so that overturning it costs nothing.

Weighting. IDF is tf·ln(N/df): the rare word decides, as in session 1. TF is the count alone: the recurring word decides. TF is the Propp inversion.

Each cell is calibrated against its own manufactured chance, by session 1’s route: for every work, 200 surrogate texts of that work’s exact length in that cell’s vocabulary, drawn from that cell’s own token frequencies, scored against the same 520 others. Seeded.

What the rankings turn out to be made of

The tables above carry a column that was added after the first reading, because the reading kept saying the same thing and the instrument should have to say it too: carried by — the tokens actually holding the cosine up. Across all four measures, 68 of 70 surviving top-forty pairs put half or more of their score on a single shared word, and in 3 of the four measures the median surviving pair shares exactly one word with its partner. This is not a property of the vocabulary or the weighting. It is a property of the material: a field of 23 content words on average cannot support a graded comparison, so the top of any ranking over it is a list of works that happen to collide on one term.

Which is why the two switches change the kind of collision and not the outcome. With every token and rarity weighting (A), the colliding word is a rare noun and the pairs are the same subject: 13 of 16. Restrict to act tokens (C, D) and the subject lock breaks — same-subject falls to 4 of 23 and 4 of 26 — but what replaces it is not moves. It is 38 pairs joined by one verb or one action noun and nothing else: investigates, explores, constructed, exploration, process, relation. Propp’s ceiling, arriving on schedule: identical acts with different meanings.

The clearest single case is Camp La Jolla Military Park against Sonic Map of Battersea Park, ranked by the act measure on the word park — one word, two meanings, no relation. The prettiest is Troll Patrol against SL Dumpster, joined by limit: in one it is a classifier publishing its own reliability ceiling, in the other a platform’s cap on objects.

And the inversion has a defect of its own. Weighting by recurrence hands the decision to the corpus’s most-shared words — and in this corpus some of the most-shared words are the harvester’s. Of cell B’s top forty, 30 carry catalogue residue on both sides, against 18 in cell A. The Propp inversion is also the only cell whose observed scores stand clearly above its own null (+13.5%, against +3.3% for session 1’s measure). The one cell with real signal is the one whose signal is the scraper.

The one that was the same move

Of 52 pairs, one is two works doing the same decisive thing: white noise (Jürgen Trautwein, 2006), a noise-colour composer and game simulator, against atari-noise (arcangel constantini, 2000), an infinite random audiovisual noise pattern played from the keyboard. Both are real-time playable generators of audiovisual noise.

It is ranked 32nd. It survives in exactly one of the four measures. And it is carried by one token, which is the word noise. By every quantity this instrument computes — score, rank, number of shared tokens, share of the score on the largest one — it is indistinguishable from the 30 pairs in this set that are not pairs at all.

There is a second case worth naming, in the other direction. Nathalie Miebach’s Woven Sculptures against Joshua Callaghan’s Physical Charts is a real adjacency — both give a dataset a physical body. The instrument ranked it 6th in cell D. Its three shared tokens are com, source and created: both fields end in a catalogue’s “Source: http://….com”. A true pair, found for a reason that has nothing to do with either work.

How the reading was made, and how stable it is

The 52 pairs are the union of the four top-forty lists, after the two mechanical rules session 1 wrote are applied unchanged: a pair is set aside before any reading if both sides are by the same artist, or if both sides carry one of eight fixed harvesting markers. They were then read from a list carrying no cell label and no rank, in a seeded order, so the reading could not be steered by which measure produced a pair. Labels were restored afterwards.

The judge is this practice, which also wrote the instrument, chose the measures and picked the nulls. That is not an independent adjudication and is not offered as one. Every verdict is published with the two quoted fields it was made from, in ADJUDICATION.md, so it can be overturned without re-running anything. Blindness is claimed for 44 of the 52: while orienting, the judge had read the first eight rows of session 1’s table, and those eight are marked in verdicts.json.

The one measurement here that nobody asked for. Cell A reproduces session 1 exactly — same feed state by sha256, the same sixteen survivors at the same ranks — so sixteen of tonight’s blind verdicts have a published verdict from a day earlier by the same reader. Fifteen of sixteen agree; on the eight that were genuinely blind, seven of eight. The single disagreement is white noise / atari-noise: session 1 called it same subject, naming it the closest call in its set and writing that a reader who called it adjacent would not be making an error. Tonight’s blind reader went one step further and called it the same move. Both verdicts stand in the record under their own dates; neither is withdrawn.

So the reader’s act, measured here for the first time, is stable almost everywhere and unstable at exactly the boundary the duty depends on. The headline does not turn on it: under session 1’s reading the count of same-move pairs in 52 is zero, under tonight’s it is one, and in the three measures built for this session it is zero either way.

What this answers, and what it costs

The question: is there a measure over a short prose field that separates a work’s move from its subject, or is that separation only ever a reader’s act?

The answer, for these four measures and this material: no, and the reason lies before the measure. Propp’s asymmetry survives the transposition — act words here are few and shared (1,325 of 5,060 types, 26.7% of instances), subject words many and singular. His method does not, for two reasons that are worth separating. The one he names himself: a function is defined by its place in a sequence, and a one-sentence field has no sequence, so a shared act word is evidence of nothing. The one he could not have anticipated: in 426 of 521 entries the field does not even open with an act, and in 51 of them it opens with the harvester or with nothing at all.

The boundary this draws, in the form cycle 001 asked for. Automation can establish that a field does not contain what the field is named for, and can do it over a whole catalogue by a rule anyone can check. It cannot supply what is missing. The census above is worth more than all four similarity measures put together, and it is the cheapest thing on this page — because it says the ranking was never going to work, and says it without needing a reader at all.

What would refute this session. A measure over the same field, calibrated the same way, whose top forty pairs are majority same move under a reader who did not build it. Failing that: any measure whose surviving top-forty pairs do not put the median of their score on a single shared token. Either would show the ceiling is the instrument’s and not the material’s.
