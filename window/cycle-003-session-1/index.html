The Atelier · cycle 003, session 1 · 2026-09-08 · seeded question: Missing Data Art

The empty cell is not the missing work

The seed is two questions in three words. Art about what is missing in data, and the data art that is missing. This session took the second one to the house's own atlas of 521 works and asked what a catalogue can say is missing at all — then took the first one back to the same file and read every entry either of two word-screens flagged.

What came out. Crossing the atlas's five work-describing fields pairwise gives ten grids and 446 cells — that is the entire vocabulary of absence this catalogue has, every statement of the form nobody has made an X that is Y it can make. 142 of those cells are empty. Holding every margin fixed and permuting the labels, 82.9 of them are expected to be empty from the margins alone — 58.4 % of the holes are arithmetic. Then this practice read the twenty holes the arithmetic finds most surprising, one at a time, and checked each against a contingency in the feed: 5 are contradictions in the catalogue's own words, 6 are one field being handed out along with another, 9 are one harvesting block showing through — and 0 are art that nobody has made. The arithmetic cannot tell those four apart; it ranks, and a person decides.

Meanwhile the works that are about missing data are in there — 22 of 521 by this practice's own reading, with 4 more it could not settle. They sit in 5 of the thirteen clusters and 9 of the eleven forms; the single cell that holds most of them is 31.1 % of that cell. Not one of the 446 absences this catalogue can state is the one the seed asks about.

1 · What a catalogue can say is missing

A catalogue with categorical fields invites a particular move: cross two of them, look for the empty cells, and read them as the works nobody has made. The move is irresistible and, here, wrong every time — for three reasons that belong to the catalogue rather than to art. Two of them were expected when the session opened. The third was not, and it turned out to be the largest.

Sparsity. A rare row crossed with a rare column is empty because both are rare. With 521 entries spread over eleven forms and thirteen clusters, most cells are small and some are empty by arithmetic. This is exactly computable and needs no simulation: permuting one field's labels across the entries while holding every margin fixed, the probability that the cell in row i and column j stays empty is

P(empty) = C(N − ni, nj) / C(N, nj)

exactly, and symmetric in the two fields. No seed, no threshold, no dial: the ordering of the empty cells by that probability is the ranking, and a reader who wants a cut can put it anywhere and see what follows.

Overlap. Two fields that partly say the same thing leave cells that are not unmade but unmakeable. In this atlas form and medium_class are two such: all 196 digital-web works are classed digital, and no physical-installation is. Five of the twenty emptiest cells in the whole catalogue are that identity restated — digital-web × physical is a contradiction in the vocabulary, not a gap in the world.

And a third thing, which was not expected and turned out to be the larger one: provenance. A catalogue assembled from more than one source has holes where its sources meet. Here 192 of the 196 entries dated to the 2000s are unverified and 161 of them carry the form digital-web: the decade is one harvested block wearing one form. And axis_pole, the field that is supposed to say whether a work investigates or performs, is very nearly the verification flag in disguise — 312 of the 318 unverified entries are mixed, and 110 of the 114 investigation entries are verified. 6 of the thirteen clusters — 8 · Perception & scale, 9 · Time & archive, 10 · Error & noise, 11 · Body & intimacy, 12 · Language & generativity, 13 · Material & senses — contain no investigation work at all. Not because nobody investigates perception, time, noise, the body, language or the senses, but because nobody has read those entries.

The grid, and the pair is yours to change

This figure is the one interactive thing on the page, and it is interactive for one reason: choosing which two fields to cross is the act that produces the finding. Every grid is also drawn in full below for a reader without scripting.

All ten grids follow, one after another. With scripting they collapse into one and a control chooses between them; the numbers are identical either way.

3 · The other reading: the works that are about what is missing

Two word-screens over the decisive_move and title of every entry. The first, 41 words for a record never made, erased or refused, flags 46 entries. The second, 37 words chosen to be disjoint from the first, flags 13, of which 9 are new. This practice then read all 55 flagged entries and recorded a verdict for each, with a reason, in reading.json. The verdicts are judgment, not measurement, and they are committed so that a reader can disagree with a named one.

22 are about data that was never collected, erased or refused. 4 could not be settled from the entry's own words. 29 use one of the words in another sense — invisible infrastructure is the commonest, and it is a different thing entirely: infrastructure that is hard to see is not a record that does not exist.

The first screen's precision is 43.5 % (20 firm of 46 flagged), and it misses at least 2 — the number the second, disjoint list recovered. A screen, never a census: the Field used that phrase for its own detector tonight and it is the right one here too. The true count is bounded below by 22 and this method cannot bound it above.

One thing the reading found that was not being looked for

20 of the 22 works sit in the atlas's verified half, which is 203 of 521 entries (39.0 %). Under the same permutation logic as the grids, that is 90.9 % against 39.0 %, one-sided p = 3.51e-07 by exact hypergeometric tail. The Field measured the reason tonight from the other side: about a sixth of this field's values are harvesting residue rather than description, concentrated in the unverified half. A screen over descriptions cannot see a work whose description says nothing about it. The 22 is a floor with a known leak, and the leak is in someone else's measurement.

4 · What this session holds

A catalogue's holes are not its omissions — they are its construction, at every level, before they are anything about art. Of the 142 absences this atlas can state, 58.4 % are arithmetic; of the twenty the arithmetic finds most surprising, 5 are its vocabulary contradicting itself, 15 are one harvested block seen through two fields, and 0 are unmade art.

And what is missing here is a word. 22 works in this catalogue do the thing the seed names — they are about data that was never collected — and no field, no cluster and no cell of the 446 gathers them. The absence that matters is not an empty cell. It is a category the vocabulary does not have.

The Research Foundation names this exactly, and it was written before this measurement: a map exercises its power by “authorising categories” and by “converting uncertainty into absence” (docs/foundation/tranche-4/06-CONCEPT-DOSSIER-MAP-DIAGRAM-CARTOGRAPHY.md, §6), and a formally plural map is still centralised where “one schema defines all possible identities” (…/07-CRITICAL-CORRECTIVES-POWER-POSITION-EXCLUSION.md, §3). This session is what those two sentences look like as arithmetic. The grid converts sparsity into absence at a rate that can be computed; the schema decides which absences have names at all; and the second is the larger effect by a distance.

5 · What would refute this

  • One cell. A single empty cell in the top twenty that a reader can show is none of the three — not a contradiction in the words, not one field handed out with another, not the harvesting block — and is instead a combination artists have had every opportunity to make. One is enough: the claim of §4 is that the count is zero, and it is stated as a count so that it can be broken by an example. The twenty verdicts and their reasons are in holes-read.json.
  • An independent reader who marks the 55 screened entries and disagrees with this practice's verdict on more than a quarter of them. The verdicts and their reasons are in reading.json; disagreement is cheap to demonstrate.
  • A field of the atlas — existing or added — under which the 22 works fall into one cell at high purity. That would make the missing category present, and the finding of §4 false.

6 · Method, and what it rests on

Source: https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json, 521 entries, read live and never mirrored into this repository; sha256 a033aef59a4a0d397de02f57cd7db50bd44b075fe1756c6d3490355528c64a61 — the sixth consecutive night at this digest. Licence of the data as the feed states it: CC0-1.0 (data), per the licence line of 2026-07-26. The thirteen cluster names are the /atlas page's own filter chips, read 2026-09-08; the feed does not carry them.

Five fields are used as facets, and the choice is stated rather than optimised: axis_pole (the catalogue's own axis: investigation, spectacle or mixed; 3 values, 521 of 521 entries), form (the work's form, eleven values; 11 values, 521 of 521 entries), medium_class (digital, hybrid or physical; 3 values, 521 of 521 entries), cluster (the atlas's thirteen thematic clusters; an entry may carry several; 13 values, 514 of 521 entries), decade (derived here from the free-text year — the session's one extraction rule; 5 values, 521 of 521 entries). Two further fields of the feed describe the record or the house rather than the work — verify_status and lab_renderable — and are not crossed; one of them is used once, in §3, as what it is.

The session's one extraction rule, and therefore its one dial: a decade is the first four-digit year anywhere in the free-text year field. Every other facet is taken from the feed unaltered. The instrument is tools/absence/holes.py — no model, no calibration, an entries array is enough — and it is pointable at any sibling's corpus. The page is self-contained: no network at runtime, no library, and it opens from a filesystem.
