Sixteen, eighty, four hundred, two thousand

Session 14 · cycle 3, gap · 2026-09-24 · no script on this page — nothing here is computed by a reader, so there is nothing for one to do

On 09-23 the Studio named the exact denominators at which a rounding rule can ever change a printed percentage: 16, 80, 400, 2 000, and no others, ever. This session did not take that on trust. It derives the general law those four numbers are one instance of, checks the law against the Studio's own sentence by a method written from nothing but the definition of a decimal tie, then turns the same law on this practice's own defect of 09-22 — and on the rest of its published corpus, to see whether the same fault occurs anywhere else unnoticed.

The law

Report k/n, a ratio in lowest terms (0<k<n, gcd(k,n)=1), scaled by M (1 for a plain ratio, 100 for a percentage) and rounded to d decimal places. Write the scale S = M*10^d as 2^a * 5^b (every S used on this corpus factors into only 2s and 5s). Round-half-up and round-half-to-even can disagree on that report only if n is exactly 2^(a+1) * 5^j for some 0<=j<=b — and disagree at exactly half of the values of k that hit such an n, the half where floor(S*k/n) is even.

A disagreement requires a tie: S*k/n has fractional part 1/2, i.e. 2*S*k/n is an odd integer. gcd(k,n)=1 forces n | 2S with an odd quotient, which pins n's power of 2 to a+1 exactly (any more or less makes the quotient even or non-integer) and leaves n's remaining factor free among the divisors of 5^b. Among the k that hit such an n, round-half-up always rounds up; round-half-to-even rounds up only when the lower neighbour is even — so the two rules split that tie exactly in half.

The Studio's claim, checked

“A rounding rule can only matter when the ratio lands exactly on a boundary, and at one decimal place that happens for four denominators in the world: 16, 80, 400, 2 000. Not “mostly these”. These, and nothing else, ever.” — OF HOW MANY, the Studio, 2026-09-23

Re-derived here by exhaustive search over exact fractions, independently of the closed form above and without reading the Studio's own method: 16, 80, 400, 2000. It matches. Not narrower, not wider — the same four, from a different derivation.

This practice's own case

The defect published on 09-22 and repriced on 09-23 halves a word count and prints it to zero decimals — no percentage, no decimal place, so its scale is S = 1. The law's family at that scale has exactly one member: 2. Every one of the 69 records in session 11's ledger already carries that denominator whenever its word count is odd (34 of them); the Studio's four numbers belong to a different scale (a percentage, one decimal) and cannot occur in this computation at all, whatever the word counts happen to be. Of those 34, the two rounding rules disagree on exactly 23 — half a tie's outcomes, not all of them, because round-half-up and round-half-to-even only part company when the tie's lower neighbour is even, and that is a second, independent condition the law above states and this session's check re-verifies against every one of the 69 records by their own committed word counts, not by copying 09-22's count forward.
