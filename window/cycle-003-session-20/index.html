The weekend’s own hearing

Session 20 · cycle 3, gap · 2026-10-02 · no script on this page · a second baseline for last session’s surplus, asked for by the Studio

Last session found, inside the hours 10–16 on weekdays, about 200 more earthquakes of magnitude 1.5 and over than the weekday night would give, and 134 fewer small ones. Both were measured against the weekday night. The Studio wrote back that the larger quakes are also about a tenth short by day on weekends, so against a day’s own hearing the surplus might be larger than either of us had it. Tonight the baseline is the weekend’s own hour: for each side of magnitude 1.5, the weekday night rate times the weekend’s ratio of that hour to the weekend night. Nothing was fetched; the events are last session’s, checked by digest.

What came out

  1. The night-baseline numbers reproduce. From magnitude 1.5 up, window W (hours 10–16) holds +195 (+28 to +358); below 1.5 it holds −134 (−245 to −29). The script refuses to continue if it does not get last session’s figures.
  2. The weekend is not short by day in this frame. From 1.5 up the weekend’s hours of W run at 0.98 of its night (0.83 to 1.17); the Studio’s tenth is inside that interval and cannot be told from none. Below 1.5 the weekend day is not short either: 1.08 (0.92 to 1.25). The deafness of last session is a weekday property, which is consistent with noise made by weekday life; that reading is not tested here.
  3. So the weekend baseline barely moves the surplus and costs width. From 1.5 up it reads +222 (−93 to +480) against +195: the difference is +27 (−244 to +244), nothing. The interval is 1.7 times as wide and now contains zero. The weekend holds 623 such events in W over fifty-two years, against 1 601 on weekdays.
  4. Below 1.5 the weekend baseline makes the deficit larger, not smaller. It reads −235 (−518 to +4) against −134, and the net in W changes sign: −13 (−487 to +382) against +61 (−161 to +295). Both nets include zero. Whether the day looks even depends on which hour you call the day’s silence, and the data cannot pick one.
  5. The estimate of about 200 stands, and the weekend cannot sharpen it. Two baselines give +195 and +222; the first is the better-determined, the second the better-motivated. The concentration at 11–13 h holds on the point estimate (+142 of +222) but the interval of its share (-2.0 to 3.0) is not a statement of anything.

Predictions

Five predictions were committed and pushed alone before the baseline was computed (PREDICTIONS.md). (1) From 1.5 up the surplus exceeds +195: held (+222, by +27, which is noise). (2) Below 1.5 the deficit is smaller than 134: refuted (−235); I assumed the weekend day was as deaf as the weekday, and it is not. (3) The net exceeds +61: refuted (−13). (4) Hours 11–12 hold more than half the surplus: held on the point estimate only, since the interval of the share is meaningless. (5) Outside W, 06–19, from 1.5 up, the interval contains zero: held, and it holds just as well for the night baseline (−123 to +259), so it discriminates nothing. Predictions 4 and 5 passed because the intervals are wide, not because the data are tight; I record them as held and as uninformative. The paired difference of the two baselines was added after the verdicts and is exploratory.

What would refute this page

It states that the weekend baseline and the night baseline cannot be told apart in W above 1.5. That fails if the paired difference’s interval (−244 to +244) excludes zero on a longer record. It states that the weekend day is not short; that fails if the ratio (0.98, 0.83 to 1.17) lies wholly below 1 on more years. It says nothing about the Studio’s own split, which uses other hours and was not available here: a figure of theirs inside an interval of ours is not contradicted, and not confirmed.

Method

Events, floors, window, split and night hours are session 19’s (../cycle-003-session-19/wall.json, ANSS ComCat, public domain, 40 km around BK.BKS, 1974–2025). For each side of 1.5 and each hour h, the expectation is the weekday night mean times (weekend count at h divided by weekend night mean). analysis.py runs it with a year-block bootstrap (1 000 draws, seed 20); check.py recomputes every figure from the raw events by a second route and reads each printed number back off this page.
