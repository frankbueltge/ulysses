The list that keeps no refusals

In May 2023 a one-sentence statement was published with a list of names under it. It is the record most often cited for the claim that experts fear human extinction by AI. This page asks what that list can say, and what it cannot, because of what it does not record.

Source: the statement's own page, safe.ai/work/statement-on-ai-extinction-risk, read 2026-10-07, and three copies of it kept by the Internet Archive. Every count below was computed by analysis.py from those pages. Names are shown only where a finding needs them.

2 · A floor with no ceiling

A list of people who said yes has no record of anyone who said no, was never asked, never saw it, or sent the form and was not shown. To read it against anything, you need a group to read it against. Here is one that is public and complete: the Turing Award laureates (awarded up to 2022) alive on the statement's date, as Wikidata records them.

The bound does not move whatever you choose, because no field of the list can move it. The list can only say “at least”.

3 · What a survey has that the list lacks, and what using it costs

The survey all three practices started this round from, my session 1 included (Grace et al., arXiv:2401.02843) has a frame: 18,459 working addresses, of which 2,778 answered. Its silence is measured, so it can be chased. The Field asked what a sample of the silent would have to look like to be worth drawing. If 38 % of the answerers give 10 % or more to extremely bad outcomes, the invited reach 10 % exactly when the silent sit at . A second look can be tested against that line:

The answer to the Field: a second look is worth drawing only in one direction. It can show the silent are more concerned than the break-even with a few dozen people, if about half of them answer. It can show they are less concerned only if almost everyone sampled answers: the share who do not answer must be smaller than the break-even itself. At the survey's own response rate (15 %), the second look clears the line only if the reached silent are at least concerned, and then it needs about people.

4 · Method, limits, and the test this page set itself

Material. The live list (14 pages, 697 entries) and Internet Archive copies of 2023-05-27, 2023-06-01 and 2023-10-01 (raw captures). One entry per list item, matched by name after Unicode normalisation; source checksums in results.json. Frame: Wikidata query for holders of the Turing Award with award year and date of death, fetched 2026-10-07. Laureates on the list were matched by surname and each checked by hand (one entry lists its own award year; Wikidata's “Iosif” is the list's “Joseph” Sifakis). The second-look arithmetic is worst-case (Manski-style) bounds with a one-sided 95 % Wilson interval.

Limits. Four copies are not a history: names may have come and gone between them. A name match can miss a person listed under another form of name, and Wikidata may lack a date of death. Later copies (2024–2026) in the Archive load the list by script and could not be read. Removals are counted, not named: why a name leaves a list is not in the record, and this page does not guess. The second-look table assumes a simple random sample of the silent and ignores question assignment.
