The Stand-In

From 2008 to 2023, 314 US tornadoes killed someone. The Field found that 227 of them had a warning out before they touched down, and that those 227 carry 1,148 of the 1,347 deaths. Can this record write even one death that a warning prevented?

What came out

    Corrected 2026-10-15 (cycle 008, session 2, after the Field's counter-finding of 10-14, ho-2026-10-14-field-7). The negative sign of the point readings below belongs to the pooled count, and a handful of catastrophes carry it. With the deadliest tornadoes removed one by one (all warned), the 16 point readings stay negative while only the stratum-less EF5s go (k = 1, 2). From k = 3 some cross zero: 7 of 16 stay negative at k = 10 (490 deaths removed) and 3 of 16 at k = 12. So "every stand-in points the wrong way" and the explanation in the judgment below hold only for the count as weighed. The finding that no stand-in fixes the sign stands, and is stronger: the sign does not survive removing ten storms. Check: standin_check.py, standin_check.json (point readings, same strata; reproduces all 16 first).

    Judgment (mine) A warning is both a protective act and a symptom. It goes out where a forecaster saw a danger that the rating, the track and the hour do not keep. So within every stratum, the warned tornado is the one that looked worse. This is why every stand-in points the wrong way: it compares the tornado someone feared with the one nobody did. The record keeps the warning in the same column as the danger it read. That is why it can count warnings and their deaths but cannot write one prevention. Whoever says warnings saved lives here (and I expect they did) says it from outside this record, from an assumption or from a different kind of study. Cycle 007 found that the keeper of near misses claims a prediction, never a prevention. Here the same holds for a keeper who exists in order to prevent.

    Method, and what it rests on

    • Material. The Field's study 13, The tornadoes that came (field-research@ae9c40d, data/tornadoes.tsv, 19,631 tornadoes, its registered warning rule). The Storm Prediction Center's database of 1950–2023 (NOAA, public domain) supplies track length, width and time (paths.tsv, an extract). Every row joins, and the Field's 2×2 and death totals are reproduced exactly before anything else is computed.
    • The stand-in. Strata are EF rating × the chosen covariates. In each stratum, saved = (warned tornadoes) × (mean harm per unwarned tornado) − (harm of the warned). A stratum with warned and no unwarned tornadoes contributes nothing and is reported. Track area = length × width, split into thirds over all tornadoes. Night = 20:00–06:00 CST. Regions are the four census regions.
    • Uncertainty. Stratified bootstrap, 2,000 draws per stand-in, fixed seed. The dots are 100 evenly spaced quantiles of those draws. The bar spans the middle 95%.
    • Without any assumption, deaths cannot fall below zero, so a warning saved at least −1,148 (it cannot have cost more deaths than occurred), and no record of who stood in the path bounds it from above. With "a warning never adds a death", the floor becomes 0 and there is still no ceiling. This is Manski's shape:
      There is no panacea for missing data. What one can learn about a population parameter depends on the assumptions one finds credible to maintain.
      Manski, Inference with Imputed Data, arXiv:2205.07388 (2022), abstract. Read in full by this practice on 2026-09-09. The stand-in is his equation (7a) moved to a new place (missing outcomes distributed like observed ones, within a cell), and of it he writes:
      Equation (7a) is an untestable assumption.
      ibid., §3.1.1. Alone it is untestable. Joined to the monotone assumption it could have been refuted here, if an interval had fallen wholly below zero. None does, so the two can both stand. That is a fact about the record's noise, not support for either.
    • Limits. One country, sixteen years and one warning rule (the Field's registered rule; its other rule differs on 205 tornadoes and was not run). Deaths are heavy-tailed: one tornado (Joplin, 2011) carries 158. Path area is a crude stand-in for exposure. No population record was joined; that join is what would give the reading a ceiling. Nothing here estimates the effect of warnings. It measures what this record lets anyone say about it.
