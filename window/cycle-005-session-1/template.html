Whose frames: 135 photographs from 44 people stand in for 1,397 from 777 others

Finding. The Studio read 135 licensed photographs of one tortoise and found 5 that show no living animal (1 bone, 2 empty frames, 2 unclear). The 135 are not 135 independent looks: they come from 44 observers, mean cluster __BBAR__, and the 5 odd frames sit with 5 different observers. Priced as clusters (Kish's design effect, with the unequal-cluster correction of Lynn & Gabler 2005) the 135 are worth __NEFF__ independent frames at ρ = 0.05 — a loss of one fifth, not the problem. The problem is that the other 1,397 photographs are by 777 observers, none of whom is among the 44, and that one of them alone holds 82 frames. No intracluster correlation closes that gap; the clustering price is computable, the transport is not. Only a draw from the 1,397 can. Below: the 135 as their observers, the price as a slider, and the designer of that draw. Estimates throughout; one query day (2026-10-07); n small.

1. The 135 as 44 observers

One column per observer, one dot per read frame; rust = the Studio's non-living readings. Choose what is counted.

2. What the clustering costs inside the 135

Two prices for the same sample. Kish's deff = 1 + (b − 1)ρ is exact only for equal cluster sizes and equal weights (Lynn & Gabler 2005, eqs. 1–2). With unequal sizes b is replaced by b* = Σbc² / Σbc (their eq. 3, all weights equal). Here b* is __BSTAR__, about twice the mean cluster size; at ρ = 0.05 the shortcut says the 135 are worth 122 independent frames where b* says 108. A percentile bootstrap over the 44 observers gives __BOOT_LO__ – __BOOT_HI__ % and is not wider than Wilson (a shifted interval: with five events it is not an instrument for widening). The odd label's own ρ̂ is +__RHOHAT__, inside the range chance gives (__RHOLO__ to __RHOHI__): five events cannot estimate it. Slide ρ; the page does not pick for you.

3. The draw that would say something about the other 1,397

Licence forbids the Studio to show the 1,397, not to read them. Design a draw. Frames drawn at random from the 1,397, or whole observers drawn until the target is reached; the clustering of each design is simulated on the real cluster sizes (1,500 draws per row, seeded).

The interval is Wilson at the effective size (n/deff, with the expected count scaled the same way) — an estimate, not a coverage guarantee. "Chance of reading no odd frame" is (1 − share)n/deff, approximate. ρ is the slider above. The grey bar is the 135's own interval (1.6–8.4 %), the rust bar the draw's expected one.

5. What this changes in the round

The Studio's interval for "no living animal in frame", 1.6–8.4 %, is arithmetically sound for 135 independent frames and loses about a fifth of its sample to the observers. That is the smaller finding. The larger is that the 135 and the 1,397 are separated by observer: the Field showed the licensed 135 draw on 44 observers where a random draw gives 107–123, no observer appears in both groups, and 80 % of the 135 are from 2024 on. A quantity measured on one side of that line is a statement about that side. What would cross it is a read of a random sample of the other side, and this page prices it: 135 frames drawn at random from the 1,397 are worth about as much as 135 independent ones (half-width × __FR05__ at ρ = 0.05), because the 777 observers rarely repeat; the same number of frames taken as whole observers costs × __OB05__.

6. Limits

The odd class is the Studio's single reader's; "unclear" is a class. Five events. Observer = GBIF recordedBy string, a display name; one person under two names would be two clusters and two people under one name one. The unit is the record (one photograph read per record), not the photograph. Licence status is the Field's rule (media licence CC0 or CC BY); 3 licensed records the Studio did not read are left out. ρ is unknown and not estimated; the effective-size interval assumes a common ρ. The draw designer simulates sizes of clusters, not the odd label. Observer names are replaced by running numbers in everything committed.
