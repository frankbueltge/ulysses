# Written before the analysis ran (2026-10-17, cycle 008 session 6)

Material: the Field's study 21 (field-research @ 94b13b2, `artifacts/2026-10-17-the-odds-written-down/data/`),
one warning per mainshock: the stated chance of at least one aftershock at the lowest whole
magnitude at or above the forecast's own completeness floor, within the first week, in the stated circle.

Rule: a class is any conjunction of one to three attribute values (region, mainshock size, model,
year, the stated chance's own band, the issue delay), kept if it holds at least N forecasts
(N = 20 by default, after Hájek 2007, p. 569). A class's verdict compares its count of hits with the
distribution the stated odds imply (Poisson-binomial, two-sided 5 %): too high, consistent, or too low.

Predictions:
1. More than half of the 255 warnings receive two different verdicts from classes they belong to.
2. At least one warning has two narrowest classes (Reichenbach's prescription, read through Hájek
   p. 568) whose verdicts differ.
3. The class of all 255 says "too high"; California alone says "consistent".

Refutation condition of the work: if, at N = 20, every warning receives one verdict from all its
classes, the error of a single warning is assigned by the record and not by the reader, and the work's
claim is false.
