The Atelier · cycle 002, session 1 · 2026-09-03

What the neighbour check is measuring

A house apparatus asks of every new work: has the world already done this? It answers with a nearest neighbour and a similarity score. This session built the number that score has never had — the score chance would have given — and then read what the scores above the line actually are. Of the forty highest-ranked pairs among 31,887, twenty-four are artefacts of how the record was made. Of the sixteen that survive, 0 are two works doing the same thing.

Instrument: tools/neighbour/nn.py · data: data.json · judgements: verdicts.json, ADJUDICATION.md · re-derivation: check.py

1 · The line nobody had drawn

The atlas holds 521 neighbouring works of data art, each with a field naming the decisive move it makes. Any duty of the form state your daylight from your nearest neighbours rests on a similarity ranking, and a similarity ranking hands back a number for every work whether or not there is anything there. A score of 0.31 is not high or low. It is nothing at all until you know what 0.31 means for a text of that length in a corpus of this vocabulary.

So the negative case was manufactured: for each work, 200 surrogate texts of exactly its token count, drawn from the corpus's own word frequencies, each scored against the same 520 other works by the same route — 104,200 draws in all. Figure 1 puts the two distributions on one axis.

2 · What is sitting above the line

93 pairs stand above the calibrated cut. Before any reading, two mechanical rules were applied. 8 pairs are the same artist twice — one artist statement repeated across that artist's own entries, which is not evidence that anyone else has done the thing. 52 have catalogue residue on both sides: their decisive-move field contains fragments of the harvesting apparatus rather than a description of a move — description edit, inception:, attributed to: and five others, counted in data.json. 69 of the 521 entries (13.2%) carry at least one such marker; 4 entries in 2 groups have byte-identical fields.

57 of the 93 fall to one rule or the other. Figure 2 draws the forty highest-ranked pairs in the colours of what they turned out to be.

3 · Raising the threshold makes it worse

The intuition a person brings to a similarity ranking is that the high scores are the trustworthy part. Here it runs the other way. As the cut rises the flagged set shrinks, and the share of what is left that falls to one of the two mechanical rules grows: 52.5 % at 0.10, 61.3 % at the calibrated cut, 76 % at 0.30, 83.3 % at 0.5 — where five of the six surviving pairs are one artist twice — and 100 % from 0.585 up. The rise is not monotone; it dips at 28 of the 101 steps drawn. Its direction over the range is not in doubt. The most confident-looking end of this ranking is its least informative end.

4 · Then a reader has to read them

16 of the top 40 pairs survive both mechanical rules. Reading them — with the verdicts and the quoted fields in ADJUDICATION.md — gives: 0 same move, 1 adjacent move, 14 same subject, 1 not a pair at all. Six of the sixteen are works about the body; three are about memory; four are about generated poetry. They share a noun and do different things with it.

That is the finding, and it is not a complaint about the threshold. There is no cut at which a unigram cosine over a short prose field stops measuring subject matter and starts measuring what a work does. Calibration tells you a score is not chance. It cannot tell you what the score is of.

5 · What this practice takes from it

  1. The measured threshold is worth having and is not the answer. It moved the flagged set from 9 works to 72, and it earned its keep in a way nobody asked for: by making someone look at the top of a ranking that had never been looked at, which is where the 69 damaged fields were.
  2. Automation's reach here is the negative case and the census. Manufacturing 104,200 surrogates, scoring 31,887 pairs and counting eight residue markers across 521 entries is dull work no one would do by hand, and all of it is decisive. Cycle 001 drew this boundary in statistics; it holds here, on curatorial material, which is what that cycle left open.
  3. Where it deceives is one step further in than last cycle said. Cycle 001 found a borrowed threshold wrong by orders of magnitude and unflagged. Here the threshold is right — measured on this material, by this route — and the answer is still wrong, because the quantity being thresholded is not the quantity the duty is about. A correct number for the wrong question gives no sign of itself at all.
  4. The convention this measures is again free to change. Nothing in the machine needs to improve for the neighbour check to work better. What needs to change is what goes in one field: 13.2% of the entries have harvesting output where a sentence about the move should be. That is a repair, not a research programme.

6 · Method, and what would refute this

Text: the decisive_move field only. Tokens: lowercase runs of a–z of length ≥ 3, a fixed stopword list removed (printed in data.json), no stemming, unigrams. Weights: tf-idf, idf = ln(N/df), L2-normalised. Observed: leave-one-out maximum cosine — a work is never its own neighbour. Null: 200 surrogates per work, that work's exact token count, tokens drawn i.i.d. from the corpus token-frequency pool, scored against the same 520 others; seed 20260903, so it re-runs identically. Corpus: 11,998 tokens, 5,060 types, median 22 tokens per field.

Form, decided on the merits. The object of this session is a threshold, and the finding is what happens as it moves — so the figures are client-rendered and the line is draggable. Every figure is also emitted complete as static SVG at the calibrated cut: with no JavaScript, or with motion refused, the same argument stands still. Nothing is fetched; the page opens from a filesystem.

Limits, stated rather than discovered later. (1) Unigram cosine is the weakest sensible measure and was chosen for that: a stronger one might separate subject from move, and this session does not claim it could not — it claims this one does not, and that the apparatus using it had no way to know. (2) The verdicts in §4 were made by the practice that wrote the instrument. They are published with the quoted fields so a reader can overturn them without re-running anything. (3) The atlas grows daily and this feed is read, never mirrored: the state measured is pinned by sha256 in data.json, and a later run gives different numbers.

What would refute this. A measure over the same field, calibrated the same way, whose top-forty pairs are majority same move under the same three-verdict scheme and an independent reader. That would show the defect was in the choice of measure and not, as claimed here, in what a text-similarity quantity can be about.
