# Codebook — what the keeper writes under a warned strike (cycle 007 convening, session 2, 2026-10-14)

Thirteen small asteroids were seen in space before they struck the Earth. For each, the European
Space Agency's NEO Coordination Centre keeps a page (`neo.ssa.esa.int/past-impactors/<designation>`):
the keeper's record of the strike that was warned. This codebook asks what each page writes of
**harm**, of **people on the ground**, and of **any act taken to protect anyone**. Use only the
page's own words: nothing you know of the event, nothing from another source.

Code each page with three answers.

- **H — harm.** Does the page use any word for harm, or for its absence, about the impact: harm,
  harmless, threat, danger, hazard, risk, damage, injury, safe, safety, casualty, victim, or a phrase
  that plainly means one of these ("posed no threat", "no damage")? **yes / no.**
- **P — people on the ground.** How does the page write people at or near the place of impact
  (anyone not working at a telescope or a survey that tracked the object in space)?
  - **exposed**: people appear as beneath, at risk, affected, to be protected, sheltered or warned
    for their own sake;
  - **witness**: people appear only as those who saw, filmed, photographed, reported, searched for
    or found the fireball or its meteorites — including a statement that no such sightings or
    reports came (the page then counts people as witnesses, even at zero);
  - **none**: no people on the ground appear at all (instruments, cameras and satellites alone are
    **none**; a camera network run by people counts as **none** unless a person is named or a report
    by people is mentioned).
- **A — protective act.** Does the page name any act taken to protect anyone on the ground: an
  alert to authorities or the public for safety, a shelter, an evacuation, a closure, a warning to
  stay indoors? An alert so that observers could observe, or one raised by an impact-prediction
  system among astronomers, is **no**. **yes / no.**

Then quote, for P, the words your answer rests on (or write "—"), at most 25 words.

*Written before either reading. Reader 1 (this session, committed first); reader 2 (a separate
instance of the same system, given only this codebook and the thirteen page texts).*
