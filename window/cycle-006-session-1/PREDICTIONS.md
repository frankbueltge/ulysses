# Predictions — cycle 006, session 1 (committed before any interval is computed)

State of knowledge when written: the 2023 Expert Survey on Progress in AI paper (Grace et al., arXiv:2401.02843)
has been read for its fielding numbers (20,066 emails; 18,459 working; 2,778 responses) and its headline shares
(38 %; 41.2–51.4 %); the published response file has been opened only far enough to confirm that the question
columns exist and that its identity, time and sector columns are redacted. No interval below has been computed.

- **P1.** The file reproduces the paper's N and shares exactly (extinction questions 1,321 / 661 / 655; the long-run
  impact question 2,704 answering, 37.8 % at 10 % or more).
- **P2.** For the 37.8 % statement the sampling half-width (Wilson 95 %) is under 2.0 points and the worst-case width
  from non-response (taking the 18,459 working addresses as the population) is over 80 points: the second is more
  than 40 times the first.
- **P3.** "More than a third of researchers" survives only if non-respondents are at least 80 % as likely as
  respondents to give 10 % or more (break-even selection ratio k between 0.80 and 0.95).
- **P4.** The median of the population is not bounded by these data at all (identification set the whole 0–100 %
  scale), because the response rate is below one half.
- **P5.** Among the 2,704, the 70 who did not finish the survey differ from those who finished in their share at 10 %
  or more by less than 10 points (n too small to show a difference).
- **P6.** The published file cannot support any check of non-response by wave, sector or stated prior thought,
  because those columns are redacted. (Stated as fact if true; no model of them is built.)

Refutation condition for the page: if a reader can set a selection ratio inside the range the survey's own
demographic comparison supports (k from 0.6 to 1.0) and the page's "over a third" verdict does not change across that
range, the page's claim that the headline is an assumption rather than a measurement is overstated.
