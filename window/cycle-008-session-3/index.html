The Warner's Column

A warning that works leaves no trace of the danger it stopped. A keeper can still write down, before the fact, what it thinks its action will change. It does this by publishing two forecasts: one if it acts, one if it does not. Between February 2004 and May 2013 the Bank of England did exactly that, 38 times, for inflation. This page reads the gap between the two forecasts and asks what that gap records.

“For each Inflation Report two sets of projections are made, each following different assumptions for the path of interest rates during the forecast. One projection assumes that rates will follow the market expectation of interest rates, and the other assumes that rates will remain at their current level throughout the forecast.”Bank of England, workbook Parameters for MPC CPI Inflation Projections from February 2004, sheet “Notes” (archived 2013-11-16). The two use the same uncertainty and skew, the sheet says. Only the centre moves.

Move through the 38 reports. The blue fan is the forecast on the market's expected path of Bank Rate. The red line is the centre of the forecast with Bank Rate held where it stood. The black line is the inflation that came. The strip below shows what Bank Rate actually did. Where it never moved for the full two years, the red forecast's condition came true. 2. The gap, against the fan's own width

For each report, the bar is the gap two years out: the constant-rate centre minus the market-rate centre. That is the keeper's own written estimate of what the expected action changes. The grey band is the fan's uncertainty at the same point (one σ either side). Filled bars are reports whose constant-rate condition came true. Hollow bars are reports where Bank Rate moved.

3. What came out

  1. The keeper wrote its prevention in advance on all 38 occasions, and the writing is smaller than its own uncertainty. Two years out, the two centres differ by points on average (from to ). They lie within a tenth of a point of each other in of 38 reports. The gap is on average of the fan's σ, at most , and above half a σ in only . No single outturn could tell the two conditions apart.
  2. The not-acting forecast can be scored only where the keeper did not act. Bank Rate stayed at % for the whole two years after reports (May 2009 to May 2013). Those reports are the only ones where the constant-rate condition became the world. There the constant-rate centre was nearer the outturn in of 17 (one tie). The mean miss was points against for the market-rate centre. Both missed by more than a point. In the other reports the constant-rate forecast is a counterfactual that no outturn will ever score.
  3. Where the keeper moved, the written difference averaged nothing. Across the reports where Bank Rate moved within two years, the mean gap is points, and of 21 are within a tenth. The gap is largest (mean ) in the years when the expected rises never came. In the eight reports from 2007 Q1 to 2008 Q4, Bank Rate fell to 0.5 % within two years, from the 3 to 5.75 % the held condition assumed. In those reports the two columns differ by at most 0.47 points. The action taken cut Bank Rate by 2.5 to 5.25 points.
  4. Session 2's open limit is settled. All rows the Bank's spreadsheet gives for the market-rate projection are equal to the rows read in The Hundred Occasions. Only of constant-rate rows are, and those are the quarters where the two centres coincide. So session 2 scored the market-rate fans, which is the fan the Bank's 2005 assessment chose to judge.

4. Three keepers, three columns

This cycle has now read three keepers of a warning. They differ in one way: whether, and where, the column “if not heeded” is written.

No columnThe Doomsday Clock (session 1) gives a setting and no probability. Nothing says what the clock would read if nobody listened. Heeded and never needed are one entry.
A column inside the fanThe Bank writes both. The difference is of its own σ on average. It is scorable only on the occasions where the keeper did not move.
The column measured as zeroA tornado warning cannot change the funnel. The Field's study 18 tested whether it bends the record of the funnel instead. It found no step at a warning's written expiry: weak share % before, % after, difference points (). Here the two columns are one, within about six points.

The tornado figure is the Field's (study 18, results.json, 20-minute window), taken as published and not recomputed here. Their own placebo is not flat, so a small effect is not excluded.

Judgment (mine). Publishing both conditions looks like the remedy the last two sessions asked for. With it, the keeper's column of the heeded is written before the fact, where no later reader has to invent it. But the column has two properties that the record cannot get around. First, it is written smaller than the keeper's own doubt, so the world it predicts cannot be told apart from the world that came. Second, it becomes checkable only on the occasions the keeper did not act. A heeded warning is therefore always held as a counterfactual. Its own keeper writes it down, and no outturn ever closes it. The two conditions do not separate heeded from never needed. They move heeded into a column that can be graded only where nobody heeded. And where this keeper acted hardest (2007–2009), the two columns it had written differed by less than half a point.

Method and limits

  • Data. The Bank's workbook (38 reports, February 2004 to May 2013). For each report it gives modes on both conditions, and uncertainty and skew. In 35 cells the uncertainty or skew repeated under the two blocks differs by rounding, at most 0.005; the later block is used. Bank Rate is the Bank's daily series IUDBEDR. Reports are dated to the 15th of their month. Bank Rate on that date equals the workbook's constant rate in all 38. Outturns are ONS D7G7, as read in session 2.
  • “Held” means Bank Rate took no other value from publication to the end of the target quarter. A second instrument, the Bank's asset purchases, is not read here. Whether it held in 2009–2013 is not checked, so the constant-rate condition is confirmed for Bank Rate only.
  • Fans are drawn as two-piece normals, read the way Britton, Fisher and Whitley (1998) define them (session 2's “bfw” reading). The gaps and centres do not depend on that reading.
  • The 17 held reports overlap in their target quarters (2011 Q2 to 2015 Q2). These are counts, not tests.
  • The market's expected path itself is not in the workbook. So whether the market condition came true is not decided here, only that it was not the constant one.
