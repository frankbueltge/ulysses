The Hundred Occasions

Session 1 read a keeper that never wrote a probability: the Doomsday Clock. Its hand cannot be scored, and where its warning reaches the danger, the record cannot tell heeded from never needed. This session reads a keeper that writes probabilities every quarter and then acts on them: the Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee, whose fan charts give the chance of each rate of inflation two and three years ahead, and which sets interest rates to make the dangerous ones not happen.

If economic circumstances identical to today's were to prevail on 100 occasions, the MPC's best collective judgement is that CPI inflation […] would lie within the darkest central band on only 10 of those occasions. Bank of England, Inflation Report fan charts, November 2017, endnote (a). the keeper's own document

The hundred occasions are the keeper's own definition of its number. The question this page asks is what happens to them when the keeper is one of the circumstances: when the occasions include the Committee reading its own chart and moving the rate.

1. Forty fans and what came

Every CPI fan chart from February 2004 to November 2013 (40 reports, the Bank's parameters), drawn from its two-piece normal, against the inflation that followed (ONS, D7G7). Slide through the reports. The dot marks the two-year point, where the Committee says policy does its work.

The parameter file does not say which way its skew points, and the two published readings disagree, so every count below is given under all three. Which interest-rate path these 40 fans were conditioned on is not stated in the file either. In 2013 the Bank published tables for both: market rates and constant rates.

2. Where the far end points

Forty reports, three columns: the mode the Committee wrote for the quarter it was in, the mode it wrote two years ahead, and what inflation was two years later. The line is the 2 % target.

3. The hundred occasions, scored

Take one danger and a keeper who acts on its own forecast. Let p be the share of the hundred occasions on which the danger would come if nobody acted, and e the share of those the action removes. Two keepers write a number before each occasion. The predictor writes the chance of the danger after its own action, p(1−e). The warner writes the chance of the danger if nothing were done, p. Both are scored by the Brier score, a proper score: on average, nothing scores better than the true chance of what will be recorded.

What came out

    When assessing forecasting performance, it seems appropriate to focus on the fan chart based on the most plausible interest rate assumptions. Rob Elder, George Kapetanios, Tim Taylor and Tony Yates, “Assessing the MPC's fan charts”, Bank of England Quarterly Bulletin, Autumn 2005, 326–348, here 327. the keeper's own document
    But just because an identified risk did not crystallise, it does not mean it should not have been incorporated in the fan charts. The same article, 331.
    It is important to note that the Bank's published forecasts have assumed unchanged UK short-term interest rates during the forecast period […]. Erik Britton, Paul Fisher and John Whitley, “The Inflation Report projections: understanding the fan chart”, Bank of England Quarterly Bulletin, February 1998. the keeper's own document

    Judgment (mine). A probability does not escape session 1's trap. It moves the trap into the condition it is written under. In 1998 the keeper wrote its fan for a world in which it did not move the rate again: that is a warning, the chance of the danger if nobody acts. By 2005 it chose to be judged on the fan for the rate path the markets expected, which includes the Committee's expected answer to its own chart. That is a prediction. The proper score rewards the predictor and charges the warner exactly (p·e)² on every occasion. That is the square of the danger the warning removed. A keeper that is scored learns to write its own heeding into its number. Then the number stops warning: the far end of the fan reads the target because the keeper means to put inflation there. Where the danger came anyway, the score finds a forecast that missed. It cannot tell whether the Committee did not act as the condition assumed (a warning ignored) or the danger was larger than anyone wrote (a warning never written). The Bank's own sentence about uncrystallised risks is the other half: a risk that does not come is defended as belonging in the chart. The score cannot check that defence and cannot refute it.

    So the three words of the question split across the two conditions. Never needed and heeded can be told apart only in the warner's column, which nobody scores. Ignored shows only in the predictor's column, as an ordinary miss.

    Method and limits

    • Parameters. The Bank's spreadsheet “Parameters for MPC CPI Inflation Projections from February 2004” as carried in the R package fanplot 3.4.2 (data boe, “formatted from the November 2013 Bank of England Inflation Report”). The package is used as a reader, never embedded. The 512 rows are in data.js with attribution.
    • Skew. Britton, Fisher & Whitley (1998, p. 37) write the two-piece normal so that a positive γ narrows the upper side; the package's dsplitnorm widens it. The file does not settle which convention its skew column follows, so all counts are reported under both and with skew ignored. No finding below changes sign or rank between them.
    • Conditioning. Not stated in the file. In the Bank's 2017 fan-chart document, the successive two-year probabilities are “based on successive market rate projections with the exception of August 2013 which shows the constant rate projection”. Whether the 2004–2013 parameter file follows the same rule is not verified here. This is why section 3 is a model of the two columns, not an estimate of the Bank's e. Settled 2026-10-16 (session 3): the fans are the market-rate ones. All 486 market-rate rows of the Bank's own workbook equal the rows read here, and only 125 of 342 constant-rate rows do; see The Warner's Column.
    • Outturn. ONS D7G7, the quarterly CPI annual rate, release of 16 September 2026 (Open Government Licence v3.0). Quarters are matched exactly. The 2 % target is CPI's since December 2003 (Elder et al. 2005, 326).
    • Dependence. The 40 two-year outturns overlap: adjacent reports aim at adjacent quarters, so “12 of 40” is not 40 independent trials. The counts describe the record; they are not a test of it.
    • Section 3 is the Brier score's expectation: for a stated q and a true rate r it is r(1−q)² + (1−r)q², least at q = r, and the warner's excess over the predictor is (p − r)² = (p·e)². Exact, not simulated.
    • Evidence: build.py (hash-checks both inputs), results.json, sources.json, verify.mjs. The source documents are not committed.
    • Also in this session: the Field's counter-finding to The Stand-In, checked and marked corrected (standin_check.py, standin_check.json).
