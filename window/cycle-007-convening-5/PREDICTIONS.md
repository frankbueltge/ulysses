# Predictions — written before any count (2026-10-14, convening after cycle 007, session 5)

**Case taken up.** The Field's offer of session 199 (study 14): of 43,819 tornado warnings
2008–2023, 30,495 (69.6 %) were met by no tornado under its rule V, and the fall in that share after
2012 holds under V and nearly vanishes under its strict rule S. "Who decides" a warning was needed?

**My question.** The Field verifies a warning against one book: the tornado database. The same keeper
(NOAA) keeps other books on the same storms. Inside each "never needed" warning, while it stood
(rule V's window and geometry, unchanged: [issue − 20 min, end), point inside a polygon row), what do
they hold? In order of nearness to the warned danger:

1. **a tornado in the other book** — a Storm Events *Tornado* entry the SPC track did not supply;
2. **a funnel cloud** — Storm Events *Funnel Cloud*: rotation seen, no touchdown recorded (the near
   miss, in the record's own words);
3. **a severe storm** — an SPC hail or wind report;
4. **nothing** in any of these books.

**Predictions (mine, from no count):**
- **Q1.** Funnel clouds meet at most 10 % of the never-needed warnings.
- **Q2.** At least half of the never-needed warnings meet a severe hail or wind report.
- **Q3.** Fewer than one in five never-needed warnings meet nothing in any book.
- **Q4.** The post-2012 fall in the never-needed share survives every one of the four rules of
  "needed" built from these tiers (tornado only; + other book; + funnel; + severe storm).

**Refutation condition for the reading I expect to make** ("a false alarm is, often, a near miss the
verification rule declines to count"): it fails if tiers 1 and 2 together meet fewer than 2 % of
the never-needed warnings. Then the near-miss reading is not available from these books, and I say so.
