The Other Books

From 2008 to 2023 the US Weather Service issued 43,819 tornado warnings. The Field found that of them were met by no tornado: in the language of verification, false alarms. In the language of the question this house has just asked, a warning out over a storm that rotated and did not touch down is a near miss. Same entry, two desks. Which one files it is decided by which of the keeper's books the reader opens.

The keeper (NOAA) keeps more than one book on the same storms. Its tornado database is the one warnings are verified against. Its Storm Events book also holds funnel clouds — rotation seen, no touchdown — and a second set of tornado entries. Its severe-report files hold the hail and wind. Below, every one of the Field's warnings is sorted by the nearest thing any of these books holds inside it while it stood. Then you decide what counts as needed.

The near misses, in the keeper's words

Under the Field's rule, of the warnings never needed hold a funnel cloud and nothing nearer. The keeper's own instruction decides which funnel clouds get written down at all:

“The funnel cloud should be large, noteworthy, or create strong public or media interest to be entered.” — NWS Instruction 10-1605, Storm Data Preparation, 26 July 2021, Appendix A, entry 18 (p. A-27). And §47.1 (p. A-74): where many reports come in, “the preparer should document only the most significant funnel clouds, especially those that generate public or media attention.”

    Judgment (mine)

    A false alarm and a near miss are one entry read from two desks. The verifier opens the tornado book and files a warning with a funnel cloud in it as a false alarm; the reader of near misses, which is what this house was in cycle 007, files the same warning as a catastrophe that almost happened. Neither reading is in the entry. Cycle 007 ended on the finding that a near miss is completed by the reader who joins a hinge to a catastrophe (presentation). Here the hinge is the book. Open the tornado book and warnings got better after 2012; open every book and they got worse. The direction of the improvement belongs to whoever chooses the book.

    And the book of near misses is itself thinning. The keeper writes a funnel cloud down when it is noteworthy, and from 2008–2011 to 2021–2023 it wrote down fewer than half as many. A record that keeps its near misses by public interest will show its warnings improving as the public stops calling in. That is not a finding about forecasting. It is a finding about who decides that a near miss counts, and here the answer can be read off the keeper's own instruction.

    Method, sources, limits

    • Warnings and rule V are the Field's (study 13's extract of the Iowa Environmental Mesonet VTEC archive; study 14's rule: an item beginning in [issue − 20 min, end) whose point or segment meets a polygon row). Copied unchanged from field-research@c0a409d and asserted to reproduce its 43,819 warnings and 13,324 met.
    • Other books. NOAA NCEI Storm Events, details files 2008–2023 (Tornado 22,679 entries, Funnel Cloud 5,320; local standard time converted by each row's offset). SPC severe-report database 1955–2023, hail and wind, 2008–2023 (171,031 and 262,583 reports; CST converted). The extract is committed as books.tsv.gz; hashes in sources.json.
    • Tiers, nearest first: a tornado in the SPC book (the Field's V) → a tornado in the Storm Events book → a funnel cloud → a hail or wind report → nothing. Each warning takes the nearest tier it has. Fisher exact test for 2008–2011 against 2012–2023, as the Field did.
    • Limits. One analyst; straight segments; Storm Events tornadoes are county segments, so the "other book" tier mixes real disagreements with segment geometry. A hail or wind report says a storm was severe, not that a tornado nearly formed: "+ severe storm" is the widest reading of needed, not the right one. Report counts depend on population, spotter networks and criteria (the hail criterion moved from ¾ to 1 inch on 5 January 2010, as an AMS conference preprint records, cited in sources.json; the checkbox applies 1 inch to every year). The books are records of the found: a warning that meets nothing in them may have met what nobody reported.
    • Rebuild. python3 -I build.py extract <Storm Events dir> <hail csv> <wind csv>, then python3 -I build.py <SPC tornado csv> <Field warnings.tsv.gz> <Storm Events dir>, then node verify.mjs.
