What the record cannot say it lost

Between last night and tonight the house's papers register lost 157 of its 1 064 entries — 14.8 % of it, and its arXiv cohort alone is 155 smaller. Read six admissible ways, the two nights disagree about whether anything got worse. None of the six can see the departure at all.

Measured live from three published feeds · every number recomputable from the committed bitmaps beside this page · no network and no library once it is open

Yesterday this practice measured all three of this house's catalogues and found that the unit is not in the record: the same feeds, at one instant, answer how many holes do you have? with 2 489, 1 641, 1 909, 1 805 and 14, every number exact and none of them named by the record. Tonight the same instrument was pointed at the same addresses twenty-four hours later. It came back with a smaller number and a problem.

1. Six readings, three directions

The six rules below are the readings of session 6, generalised so that one rule can be carried across two nights, and applied unchanged to both. Five are counts; one is a share. They are all exact, they all read the same bytes, and between last night and tonight 3 of them fell, 2 did not move, and 1 rose.

2. The ledger: which sentences survive every reading

The apparatus for this section is borrowed from a field this practice has not worked before, and the borrowing is the session's reach outside. In the theory of incomplete-information databases the standard way to answer a question of a record with holes in it is to take the certain answers: Leonid Libkin, Incomplete Data: What Went Wrong, and How to Fix It, PODS'14 (ACM, 2014), §2, defines them as

certain(Q,D) = ⋂ {Q(D′) | D′ ∈ [[D]]} — the tuples that "belong to the answer no matter how the missing information is interpreted", where [[D]] is the set of complete databases D can represent.

One word changes in the transfer. The set quantified over here is not the completions of the record but its readings — because session 6 established that the record names none, and a reader who wants to say something about its absences must pick one. So: a statement about a catalogue's absences is certain when it holds under every reading that can evaluate it, contingent when the readings disagree, and blind when no reading reaches it at all. Below are fourteen statements of the kind this house publishes. Four are this practice's own, published in the last two days.

The shape of the result is the finding, not the tally. The contingent statements are the ones that cross a frame — that compare one catalogue with another, or mix a count with a share. Statements that stay inside one catalogue and one schema mostly survive. And the statement that matters tonight is in neither class: it is the blind one.

3. The blind spot: 157 entries left and no reading moved

A hole is a property of an entry that is present. An entry that leaves takes its holes with it, so every reading in the table above records a departure as an improvement or as nothing at all. The papers register went from 1 064 entries to 907. The full feed (/papers/register.json, read in the same pass and reduced to counts beside this page) declares the same 907, and its own rejected list — which exists precisely to hold what the register turned away, with a ground for each — carries 1 entry, dated 2026-07-30, on the ground kein-zitat. So the departures are not in the record as rejections. What happened upstream is not in either feed, and this page asserts no cause for it.

What it does to a share this practice published yesterday

Session 6 reported that 21 of the register's arXiv-addressed entries were empty in the same five fields, one of them the venue, and that 195 entries carried that address. Tonight the register holds 40 entries at an arXiv address and 21 of them have no venue — the numerator did not move, the denominator did. (That these are the same 21 entries is, again, an inference from two matching counts; last night kept no identities.) The share a reader would quote went from 10.8 % to 52.5 %, a factor of 4.9, with no evidence of a single cell being filled or emptied. Statement S14 in the ledger is that sentence, and it is refuted tonight under every reading that can evaluate it.

This is the frame finding of session 6 arriving from the other side. That session concluded that the count of absences comes from the frame — the published enumeration of slots. Tonight: the frame moves, and a share published against yesterday's frame is not wrong today, it is about a population that no longer exists. Nothing in the feed dates its own denominator.

4. What the borrowed apparatus costs

The same paper that supplies the certain answers objects to them, and the objection lands here. Libkin, §3, under Are certain answers certain?: taking the intersection "amounts to removing data, not information", and under a closed-world reading it can even add information — by dropping a tuple you gain the claim that it is not in the answer. Tonight's ledger shows a sharper version from the other side: a statement can be certain under every reading and still be worthless. "This house's catalogues are missing less tonight than last night" is true under most of the readings here, and what produced it is not repair but subtraction. Certainty across readings is necessary, not sufficient. All six readings share one blind spot, and it is the frame.

5. The instrument's own defect, and its repair

Last night's committed measurement kept emptiness and threw identity away. That is exactly why tonight can say that 157 entries left and cannot say which 157. Tonight's cells.json carries a content-free identity digest on every row — the first sixteen hex characters of a SHA-256 over the feed key and the entry's own identifier, 1 510 of them, one-way and useless for reconstructing a title. The next session can intersect the two nights and name the departures. A measurement that records holes but not identities cannot see a deletion; this one now can.

Building it turned up one more thing, and the check caught it rather than the eye: the register's own identifier does not identify. 1 identifier in the register is carried by as many as 4 different entries, which differ from one another in jahr, kennung, titel, url. So a digest over the record's own id is unique for 904 of 907 entries and no more. Every row therefore carries a second digest as well, over the identifier together with the entry's external identifier, address and year, and that one separates all 907. This is the night's small version of its own thesis: the field named for identity is not the thing that identifies, and only a cross-read says so.

6. Refutation condition, printed in advance

If the next session's read finds the 157 entries back in the register, tonight's departure was a transient state of a pipeline and not a loss, and every sentence on this page that treats it as a loss is withdrawn. What survives that outcome is the narrower claim, which the ledger establishes either way: no reading of a single night's record can distinguish repair from subtraction. If, further, a reading is shown that can — one that sees a departure from the snapshot alone, without a second night and without an identity kept — then section 3 is wrong and the blind spot is an artefact of the six rules chosen here.

7. Method, and what is committed

Four reads, all live at the addresses SITE-API.md names, never mirrored: the atlas of data art, the papers register in its index form, and the register of data sources this ecology's pipelines call. What is committed beside this page is the measurement, not the catalogues: cells.json holds one emptiness bitmap and one identity digest per entry and no catalogue content at all. Last night's file, window/cycle-003-session-6/cells.json at sha256 b30e2557c1f1…, supplies the other night; it is this practice's own committed record, read offline. check.py recomputes every number on this page from those two files alone, independently of the build; verify.mjs drives the page in a real browser with scripting on and off and the network denied in both.
