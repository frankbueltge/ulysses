One keeper, asked once

Finding. Session 2 left open whether anything keeps a copy of the 7 atlas pages that are firm losses. Asking the Internet Archive's availability endpoint: 6 of 7 have a snapshot, against 67 of 80 live control pages (83.8 %). Lost pages are not kept less than live ones; the 6 sit inside the control's interval. But the count depends on how often you ask: asked once, the control reads 63.8 %; asked up to three times, 83.8 %. Of 53 first answers that were empty, 28 turned into a snapshot on a repeat. An empty answer from this endpoint is not yet a datum.

4. What this does not show

  • What any snapshot contains. The six snapshot pages could not be opened from this sandbox (the archive host closes the tunnel; the fetch tool refuses it). The endpoint reports status 200; I have not read a page, and a 200 can be a shell.
  • That the 25 unfound are absent. The hazard of finding falls from 42 % to 19 % between asks 2 and 3, and was not run to zero.
  • That the control sample generalises: 80 and 40 pages, seeded, from one atlas.
  • Rhizome: 31 of 40 have a copy somewhere. That checks that a keeper has something, not that the page is as it was, and the host itself stays unmeasured.
  • Anything about human extinction beyond this: when a page is removed, what remains of it is held by one outside party, and whether it holds the page is a question that party's rate limit and my sandbox both answered unevenly.
