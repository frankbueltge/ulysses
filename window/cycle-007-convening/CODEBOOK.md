# Codebook — who is written as having stopped it (cycle 007 convening, 2026-10-13)

A false alarm that nearly led to a nuclear strike has two ends: what **started** it and what
**stopped** it. This codebook asks, of one passage telling such an alarm, how it writes each end,
and whether it writes what was stopped. Use only the passage's own words: nothing you know of the
event, nothing from another telling.

Code each passage with four answers.

- **K — cause.** Does the passage name what produced the false warning (a tape, software, a test,
  a chip, a scenario, a person loading something)? **yes / no.**
- **E — ending.** How does the passage say the alarm was known to be false?
  - **silence**: another system or source is said to have detected, reported or shown *nothing*
    ("not reporting", "no other system detected", "showed nothing unusual");
  - **report**: another system or source is said to have positively *confirmed* that it was false;
  - **none**: the passage says only that it was, turned out to be, or was declared a false alarm,
    or that word or a call came, without naming where the knowledge came from.
- **P — a person's act.** Does the passage present a decision of a named or described person as
  holding back the sequence: choosing to wait, demanding confirmation, refusing to pass the alarm
  on? Carrying a message (a call, an apology) is **no**; preparing to act is **no**. **yes / no.**
- **W — what was stopped.** Does the passage name the act that would have followed?
  - **intended**: someone is said to have been about to recommend, order or carry out retaliation,
    a counterattack or a strike, or to have been convinced it must be done;
  - **pending**: retaliation is named only as a decision that would have to be made;
  - **none**: no such act is named (preparing to inform or call someone, without saying what for, is
    **none**).

Then quote, for E and for P, the words your answer rests on (or write "—").
