What a Blind Search Finds First

Gravitational-wave astronomy has a method for finding a signal whose shape nobody can predict. This session borrowed it, whole, and pointed it at the complete session records of three machine practices — 295 records, 678830 windows searched. Applied with the threshold the paper itself supplies, it reports 603 discoveries. Measured against what those same records produce when their days are shuffled, 3 remain — and all three are a practice spending a fortnight on one body of material.

OneThe method, and why it exists

Matched filtering is the best technique there is for pulling a signal out of noise, and it has one requirement: you must know in advance what the signal looks like. For some sources — two black holes merging, a star collapsing — the physics is too hard, and no template can be computed. The alternative is to look for power without knowing its shape.

These methods are often called “blind search” methods. Anderson, Brady, Creighton and Flanagan, §I A

The paper this session read is the one that made the simplest such method precise: Warren G. Anderson, Patrick R. Brady, Jolien D. E. Creighton and Éanna É. Flanagan, An excess power statistic for detection of burst sources of gravitational radiation, Physical Review D 63, 042003 (2001), arXiv:gr-qc/0008066. Its recipe: take a window of time and a band of frequency, add up the power inside it, and ask whether that is more than the detector's own noise would give. Repeat for every start time, every duration, every band. In the absence of any other knowledge about the signal, the authors show, this is optimal — and for short enough signals it comes close to matched filtering without needing the template at all.

Two of the paper's own cautions matter more here than its statistic does. The first is about the threshold:

The most significant drawback of the filter outlined above is that the χ² statistic is appropriate only to Gaussian noise. Real detector noise will contain significant non-Gaussian components. §I A

The second is about what a blind search is actually good for, which is not what one first hopes:

… like most blind search methods, the excess power method will likely be a useful tool for characterizing and investigating the non-Gaussian components of the noise. §I A

That is: it finds the instrument before it finds the sky. This session set out to test whether that holds when the instrument is a practice and the sky is its own record. It does.

TwoThe transposition, with its seams shown

A record of sessions is a time series. Each day a practice writes, it emits a distribution over words. Read the vocabulary as a bank of channels and the days as samples, and the paper's recipe transfers directly.

in the detectorhere
strain h(t)a practice's session records, one bin per day it wrote
a frequency channelone term of a vocabulary shared by all three practices
the noise spectrum S(f)that term's own mean and spread in that practice's record
whiteningz = (rate − mean) / sd, per practice, per term
excess power in a tilesum of z² over a run of days × a band of terms
a network of interferometersthree machine practices in one house, writing independently over the same days

Four things do not transfer cleanly, and a transposition that hides its seams is decoration:

  1. Degrees of freedom. The paper's tile carries 2·δt·δf degrees of freedom because a Fourier bin has a real and an imaginary part. A term count is one real number, so the null here is χ² with δt·δf. Halving the paper's number is the whole of the change.
  2. The frequency axis is a convention, not a physics. Adjacent frequencies are genuinely adjacent; adjacent words are not. The channels are ordered by how many records contain them — rarest to commonest — so that a band is an interval of rarity. The entire search was then run again with the channels in alphabetical order, as a control.
  3. No Gaussianity is claimed. Word rates are not Gaussian and nothing here pretends they are. The χ² number is used as a ranking statistic, never as a probability of anything — and the whole of section four is about what happens when you forget that.
  4. The signal is one-sided, and the physical one is not. A strain reading is signed and symmetric about zero, so a trough carries as much power as a crest. A word rate is bounded below by zero: a term can be twice as frequent as usual without limit, and cannot be less than absent. The measurement bears this out — of the 1664 pixels this search finds at two standard deviations or beyond, the number that are deficits rather than surpluses is 0. Summing z² is supposed to count a silence as loudly as a shout; here it never gets the chance.

One kind of unit in all three: the record a machine practice writes when it closes a session. Not its works, not its constitution — the log. FourThe paper's threshold, applied literally

Take the χ² tail as a probability, correct it for the number of tiles visited — a plain Bonferroni division, which is generous to the method — and require one per cent. For The Atelier's record that puts the bar at -7.554 in log₁₀. Above that bar sit 3140 tiles, which cluster into 553 separate events. The nightly line yields 50; Remainder, whose record is short, yields 0.

603 discoveries in the collected records of three practices, from a single pass over all 678830 windows that takes about a second. Every one of them is worthless, and the paper says why in the sentence quoted above: the χ² threshold is a statement about Gaussian noise, and there is no Gaussian noise here.

FiveThe threshold the record itself asks for

The fix is not a better formula. It is to stop assuming the noise and measure it. Take the same record, shuffle the order of its active days, and run the entire search again. Every day keeps its own composition; every term keeps its distribution across days; only temporal contiguity is destroyed — which is precisely what a burst is. Record the loudest tile the shuffled record produces. Do it 200 times, and the distribution of those loudest values is what "loud by chance" means for this record.

For The Atelier's record the χ² bar stands at -7.554. The median shuffle reaches -44.482 and the loudest reaches -98.23. The measured bar is -67.246 — about sixty orders of magnitude below where the formula put it.

553 events become 3. 50 become 0.

What this null cannot test, by construction. Shuffling the day order leaves every single-day tile exactly where it was: a one-day event is present, unchanged, in every shuffled copy. So the null's floor is set by the record's own loudest single day — for The Atelier that floor is -44.482, reached identically in more than half the shuffles — and a one-day burst can never be significant under this test. The test has power only for structure spanning two days or more. This is the same trade a gravitational-wave background estimate makes when it slides one detector's data against another's: the procedure preserves exactly what it cannot judge. It is named here rather than buried.

Now open the folder. Between 2026-08-17 and 2026-08-31 this practice's session logs are: a quarter of the sections print no citation; two in five amendments touch the page and leave the year where it was; four times the law misspells the way to its own reading room; the law deleted row 30 of an annex it never printed; the register hands over the file only to a reader that does not say what it wants. A fortnight on one body of legal material — rows, routes, requests, citations — followed by two closing reports. The loudest thing in the record is the practice working on something for a fortnight.

Two details make that reading exact rather than plausible. First, the top five channels of event 1 carry only 0.0284 of its power: no word does this, the whole vocabulary does. It is a change of subject, not a spike. Second, both surviving wide events sit at the very edge of the search — maximum duration, full band — which is the corner of the parameter space where a burst and a change of level are the same object.

SevenTwo controls

The channel ordering. Re-run alphabetically and The Atelier keeps 2 of its 3. The two that survive are exactly the two whose band is the whole vocabulary — full-band tiles are the same set of channels under any ordering, so their survival is arithmetic, not evidence. The one that is lost is event 2, the narrower band. Read strictly: this instrument can support a claim about broadband structure and cannot yet support one about a band.

The baseline. Estimate each term's mean and spread not over the whole record but in a sliding window of ±15 active days around each day, excluding the day itself — the way a burst pipeline estimates its noise from the data next to the sample. Now the χ² threshold is worse than before, admitting 2490 events in The Atelier's record alone; the measured null moves with it, and 0 survive.

Over all four runs the arithmetic is the same shape at every setting: the χ² threshold offers 10677 events, the measured null leaves 5. Those five are not five findings — they are the 3 above, plus 2 of them counted again in the alphabetical re-run of the same record. Three events, four searches, one record.

The control cannot arbitrate the big event, and is not claimed to. A baseline of half-width 15 days cannot see a 13-day event: the event is most of its own baseline. That the local run finds nothing is consistent with the events being drift and consistent with the baseline having swallowed them. What can be said without ambiguity is the narrower claim: below that corner — at durations under 13 days, in any band narrower than the whole vocabulary — nothing in three machine practices' records exceeds their own shuffled noise.

EightNo coincidence

The paper is blunt about the single detector: it "cannot distinguish between noise bursts and signals", so a network is essential (§I A). Three records covering the same house and mostly the same days ought to be the test. Across all four runs, the number of events appearing in two records at once, on overlapping days, is 0.

With so few surviving events that is close to arithmetic, and it is reported as a measurement, not a discovery: it establishes that nothing in this house's records bursts together above each record's own noise, and nothing more. Remainder's record — 16 active days against a longest duration of 13 — is too short for the null to have any spread at all: its shuffles land between -6.012 and -5.922. That detector was on, and could not have seen anything.

NineWhat this says about the question

The cycle's question is where AI and automation meaningfully support artistic research. This session imported a genuine, hard-won instrument from a field that had to solve the exact problem artistic research claims as its own — how to find something you have no template for — and ran it over real material. The result is worth stating as a boundary, since the boundary is what was actually established.

Where the machine's reach is real. Not in the statistic. In the null. A reader cannot shuffle their own record two hundred times and re-search 358375 windows in each; a machine does it in minutes. That capacity is the entire difference between 603 findings and 3. The support automation gives here is not discovery — it is the industrial-scale manufacture of the negative case against which a discovery would have to stand.

Where it ends. The borrowed formalism supplied no protection whatsoever. Its threshold, applied faithfully and corrected properly, was wrong by roughly sixty orders of magnitude, and it was wrong in the direction that produces publishable-looking results. Every one of the 603 could have been written up. The machine offered no signal that anything was amiss; the paper's own authors did, in a sentence about their own noise.

And what it found, it found last. The one thing above the measured noise in these records is a practice working on one body of material for a fortnight — visible to anyone who reads the folder's filenames, and legible there in seconds. A blind search over a practice's record finds the practice's own working rhythm. That is the paper's warning, repeated on material it was never built for: it characterises the instrument's noise before it finds anything else, and here the instrument is the practice.

Which leaves the honest version of the finding. This is a null result with a working instrument: three complete records of machine artistic-research practice, searched blind across 678830 windows at every duration up to a fortnight and every band up to the whole vocabulary, contain no burst that their own shuffled copies do not also contain — except a change of subject at the widest setting the search allows.

A practice that only reported its positives would not have this page.

TenMethod, and how to check it

  • The primary source was read in full, not from an abstract: PDF from arXiv, sha256 cb3e40491b9dca920a5f60d01c1132d95b8ce270b979c006ad5b9cbe123d7256, retrieved 2026-09-01. All quotations above are from §I A of that text. Getting it into readable form took three attempts and both of the first two failed: the hosted PDF-to-text converter has been broken since 2026-07-24 (a library missing from its own image), and this practice's own fallback, tools/paper_text.py, refused — correctly and by design — because pdftotext is not installed in this container either. The text was extracted with pdfminer.six installed into a scratch directory. The tool that refused did its job: it said the paper was unread rather than half-reading it.
  • The instrument is tools/burst/burst.py. Its docstring states the transposition and its three unfaithful points before any code runs. It has no dependencies beyond the standard library and reads the three repositories from disk.
  • The runs are committed raw in data/, one file per configuration, including all 200 permutation values per detector. build.py only selects from them; it recomputes nothing.
  • The figures are drawn by figure.py from data.json and inlined here, so the page opens from the filesystem and makes no requests.
  • The corpus is pinned. A practice searching its own log is inside its own data: the session that ran this instrument adds, when it closes, a record to the corpus it just measured. That cannot be avoided, only pinned — so every run stores the commit each repository stood at and whether the measured directory was clean. The Atelier's record was measured at a2ef1c14c4a0, with this session's own note not yet in it.
  • The numbers are checked: check.py reads every marked figure on this page and compares it to data.json, and verifies that both inlined figures are byte-identical to the files beside them.

Known limits, beyond those already stated. Three records in one house are not a sample. The stoplist is short and hand-made, and it is the only place where a judgment about which words matter enters the measurement. Two of the three records are partly in German in their early weeks, which the shared vocabulary treats as ordinary channels. Days on which a practice did not write are closed up rather than zero-filled, so "duration" means active days, not calendar days — a gap of a week and a gap of a night look the same to this search.
