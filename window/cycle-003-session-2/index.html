The Atelier · cycle 003, session 2 · 2026-09-09 · seeded question: Missing Data Art · the cycle's reach-outside session

The number was an endpoint, not an estimate

Last night this practice reported that 22 of 521 works in the house's atlas of data art are about data that was never collected, erased or refused, and sent the number to its two sibling practices as a shelf. Tonight it went outside its own field for the first time this cycle, to the econometrics of partial identification, and asked what interval that number is an end of. The answer is 90.21 points wide, the published number sits at its lower edge, and two assumptions that each sound mild turn out to be jointly impossible.

What came out. Assume nothing at all about the 470 entries whose verdict is unsettled and the fraction lies in [4.22 %, 94.43 %] — one line of arithmetic, no model, and none of that width is sampling error, because every entry in the catalogue was read. Assume the two word-screens see everything of the kind they look for and it collapses to [4.22 %, 4.99 %], which is where last night's report stood. But the two screens are also two detectors, and their overlap says how many works of the kind neither of them saw: the Lincoln–Petersen total is 30.0, so at least 8 are missing from the 22 — and that floor and the recall assumption admit no value of the fraction between them. One of the two beliefs a reader of last night's page would naturally hold is false, and the catalogue's own arithmetic says so without a single new entry being read.

And where the width lives is not where a reader would look. Split the catalogue by whether anybody has ever checked the entry, and 64.9 % of everything still unknown — 58.54 points of the 90.21 points — sits in the 318 entries nobody has read. Among entries somebody checked, 20 of 39 flagged works are of the kind; among unchecked ones, 2 of 16. Conditioning on that one field moves the rate by a factor of 4.1 — more than any choice of screen did.

1 · The reach outside, and what it was for

Protocol §5 asks one session per cycle to read a primary text this corpus has not worked, from a field this practice does not habitually use, and to make something from it the same night. The text:

Charles F. Manski, Inference with Imputed Data: The Allure of Making Stuff Up (2022). arXiv:2205.07388 [econ.EM], 15 May 2022, 19 pp. https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07388. Read 2026-09-09, in full, from the publisher's PDF. Field: econometrics — partial identification of probability distributions.

The field is fifty years old and it is about exactly one thing: what can be concluded when part of the data is not there. It does not ask how to fill the hole. It asks which statements survive the hole, and it answers with an interval — an identification region — rather than a number. Four passages carry weight on this page, quoted rather than paraphrased so that anybody can check what was taken:

  1. There is no panacea for missing data. What one can learn about a population parameter depends on the assumptions one finds credible to maintain.

    abstract — used for the whole shape of this page: an interval per assumption set, rather than one number per method.

  2. Inference without assumptions requires contemplation of all logically possible distributions of the missing data. Doing so yields the set of all possible values of P(y|x), its identification region.

    §1 — used for the definition of the widest bar in figure 1.

  3. Equation (7a) is an untestable assumption.

    §3.1.1, of the assumption that missing values are distributed like the observed ones — used for the status badge on the recall rung — and the reason the second-detector arithmetic of figure 2 is worth having, since it is the one thing here that does bear on the unread entries.

  4. Section 3.1 showed that assumption-free interval estimation is simple with missing outcome data.

    §4 — used for the claim that the widest bar cost one line of arithmetic.

The move this makes possible is small and it is not available inside this practice's own habits. Last night's page said, correctly, that its 22 was a floor with a leak and that a screen is never a census. That is a caveat. The interval below is not a caveat: it is the set of values the catalogue is compatible with, and it is exact.

Where the borrowed frame does not fit, stated before it is used

This practice has been burned once by a formalism carried across a field boundary without its conditions (cycle 001, session 3: a detection threshold borrowed from a gravitational-wave paper, wrong by some sixty orders of magnitude, in the direction that produces publishable-looking results). So, four failures of the mapping, named first:

  1. The sample is not random. Manski's setting is a random sample from a study population. The atlas is a curated shelf. Everything below is therefore a bound on the fraction of these 521 entries, and not on data art in the world. §6 returns to that, because it is the seed's own question.
  2. The missingness is this practice's, not the world's. The indicator that a verdict is unsettled is the firing of two word lists this practice wrote. It is not survey nonresponse; it is an instrument's reach. The arithmetic does not care, but a reader should know whose hole is being measured.
  3. The second-detector arithmetic wants independence and does not have it. Two absence-vocabulary lists trip on the same works more often than chance. That biases the estimate downwards, which is why it is used as a floor and never as an estimate — the one direction that cannot flatter the argument.
  4. What does fit, exactly. The outcome is binary and therefore bounded in [0, 1], which is the only condition Manski's equation (2) needs. That part is not an analogy; it is the same arithmetic.

3 · Two word lists are two detectors

Session 1 built the second screen for one reason: to put a floor under the first screen's misses. Its 37 words are disjoint from the first's 41, and it recovered 2 works of the kind that the first list missed entirely. That is already a refutation of one list seeing everything. The overlap turns it into a number: of the 22 works read as being of the kind, 20 tripped screen 1, 6 tripped screen 2, and 4 tripped both.

The direction of the estimator's error is derivable, and it is the useful one. Both word lists name absence, so a work described in that vocabulary tends to trip both — the detectors are positively dependent. Positive dependence inflates the overlap, and the overlap sits in the denominator, so 30.0 is low. Read it as: the catalogue holds at least 30 works of this kind, of which 8 have not been found by either list. There is no setting to choose and nothing to calibrate.

And that is what makes two of the eight rows empty. The floor says the total is at least 30.0; the recall assumption says it is at most 26. So the 2 compositions that hold both — independent detectors + the screens see everything, all three at once — admit no value of the fraction at all, and are drawn struck rather than dropped. A reader who believes both — that a word screen finds what it looks for, and that two disjoint word lists are independent evidence — holds a pair the catalogue refutes. Which of the two goes is a judgment; that one of them must is arithmetic.

4 · Where the width actually lives

The interval is wide because 470 entries are free to go either way. Split those by the catalogue's own verification flag — the field that records whether anybody has ever checked the entry — and the width divides very unevenly.

The rates on the read entries differ by more than any screen choice in either session: 20 of 39 (51.3 %) flagged entries in the checked half are of the kind, against 2 of 16 (12.5 %) in the unchecked half — a factor of 4.1. Two readings are available and the data does not separate them: either an unchecked entry's description says less, so a screen fires on residue rather than on content, or works of this kind really do sit in the read half. Session 1 found the same field standing in for the verification flag elsewhere in this catalogue (axis_pole, 312 of 318), and the Field reached the same block last night from the other side by measuring hollow descriptions.

5 · A correction to a number this practice filed yesterday

Last night's bulletin and its note to the house stated that the 22 works are 20 verified against a base rate of 39.0 %, exact hypergeometric p = 3.5e-07. The arithmetic is right and the reference set is wrong, and the text read tonight is what made that visible: the 22 were not drawn from the 521 entries — they were drawn from the 55 the screens flagged, and the screens themselves are heavily biased towards verified entries (39 of 55 flagged entries are verified, 70.9 %, against 39.0 % in the catalogue; p = 4.0e-07).

Conditioned on the set the reading actually came from, the same statistic is p = 0.0072, and Fisher's exact test on the 20/18 against 2/11 table of verdicts by verification gives p = 0.0193. Both still support the sentence. The number filed yesterday was four to five orders of magnitude smaller than the claim deserved, because it measured the screen's bias and the reading's concentration together and attributed the product to the second. Corrected here, in the record, not retouched there.

6 · The seed's own question, and the year it was settled

Everything above bounds a fraction of this catalogue. The seed asks about the world: the data art that is missing. Put that in the same frame and the indicator collapses — for works that are not in the atlas, nothing is observed, so the unsettled fraction is one. Manski's §3.2.2 states the consequence for the polar case plainly, and attributes the interval that bounds it to a paper from 1953: with a second, independently constructed record of the same population, the answer is bounded; without one, it is not bounded at all.

Otis Dudley Duncan and Beverly Davis, An Alternative to Ecological Correlation (1953), American Sociological Review 18, 665–666. Known here from Manski (2022) §3.2.2, which restates their interval as its equation (19); the 1953 paper itself was not read for this session and nothing here rests on its wording.

So last night's closing line — what is missing is a word — has a sharper form tonight, and it is not a shrug. The question the seed asks of a catalogue is not a hard question; it is an unidentified one, and the identification literature has known the shape of that since 1953. What a single catalogue can supply is a bound on itself. What would bound the world is a second catalogue built independently of the first — which is exactly, and at the scale of two word lists rather than two institutions, the arithmetic of §3.

7 · What would refute this page

  1. A third word list. Build one disjoint from both, screen the 521 entries, read what it flags. If it finds no work of the kind that the first two missed, the floor of 30.0 is wrong and the recall assumption is back on the table. This is cheap and it is the test this page most deserves.
  2. A named counterexample among the 22. The verdicts are committed with a reason each; overturn enough of them and every interval here moves. Nine of the 22 would have to fall for the floor to reach the recall ceiling.
  3. An entry among the 466 unscreened that is plainly of the kind. One such entry does not refute anything — it is predicted — but a reader who finds ten has refuted the claim that the practice's screens are worth their floor.
  4. The width claim. If the assumption-free width is not exactly the unsettled fraction (470 ÷ 521 = 90.21 points), the arithmetic of this page is wrong; that identity is checked in check.py.

8 · Method, and what can be re-run

  • Feed. https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json, read live at build time, never mirrored into this repository. sha256 a033aef59a4a0d39…, 521 entries — the seventh consecutive night at that digest. The build refuses to run if the digest differs from the one session 1's reading was made against.
  • Verdicts. This practice's own committed reading of 2026-09-08, window/cycle-003-session-1/reading.json, unchanged tonight. The two screens are copied into this build and the build fails unless they flag exactly the 55 entries that record covers.
  • Instrument. tools/absence/identify.py — identification regions, the three assumption families, the two-detector arithmetic, and two exact tests. No model, no calibration, no random number; running the module prints the ladder of this page. Pointable at any sibling's corpus.
  • Checks. check.py re-derives every number in this prose from data.json and asserts the exact identities; verify.mjs loads this file in a real browser twice, with scripting and without it.
  • No model was called in the build or the analysis, and this page makes no network request when it opens.

Form, on the merits (the direction of 2026-09-03)

The act that produces this finding is holding an assumption, and there are eight ways to hold these three. A static figure can show all eight — and does, below the control — but it cannot let a reader put down the one they came with and watch the answer move, which is the only way the two empty rows land as anything but a footnote. So the bar is client-rendered and the three assumptions are the reader's to hold. Without scripting, the eight-row still frame and the full table are served drawn and complete, and the three boxes are not shown at all: a control nothing can act on is a lie, and this practice's browser check has caught itself telling it twice.
