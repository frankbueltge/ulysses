The licence line: how much of 135 licensed readings may cross to the 1,390 that may not be shown

Finding. The Studio read 135 licensed photographs of one tortoise and found 5 that show no living animal, then read 135 drawn at random from the 1,390 it may not show and found 0. Is the licensed lot a model of the other? I let the licensed counts count a0 times (a power prior, Chen & Ibrahim 2000), from 0 (the lots are strangers) to 1 (they are one population), and ask how well each weight predicted the draw. Counting all five odd frames, the draw prefers strangers by a factor of __R5__ — and a quarter of that goes when the clusters are priced (__R5D__). Counting only the three plain cases (a bone, two empty frames) it prefers a shared population by __R3I__; counting the bone alone, by __R1I__. The sign of the evidence turns on whether two unclear frames count. The draw is not weak evidence about a population; it is a measurement of what a single reader's "unclear" is worth. Estimates throughout; one query day (2026-10-07); one reader.

1. Choose what counts as odd

2. How far does the licensed lot cross?

The curve: how probable the draw's result (0 odd of 135) was, if the unlicensed rate were learned from the licensed lot at weight a0, with a Jeffreys Beta(½,½) beneath. A flat start (a0 = 0) gives about 0.049, the rule of thumb 1/√(πn). The ratio of the two ends is the "factor" above. Dot: the chosen weight.

3. The verdict is a function of how many frames you count

Bars: the factor by which the draw prefers strangers (a0 = 0) to one population (a0 = 1), for 0 to 5 licensed frames counted odd. Above the line at 1: strangers. Only the number counted enters, not which frames. Two of the five are "unclear", the Studio's three hard frames in the draw were all settled alive when re-read at full size; whether the licensed unclear ones were re-read the same way is not recorded here.

4. What it leaves among the frames nobody has read

The 1,390 unlicensed frames: 135 read (all living), 1,255 unread. The interval is the 2.5–97.5 % range of the odd count among the unread under the chosen weight (exact beta-binomial, no sampling). Counts, not shares; the Studio's weighted join reads "about 5, 2 to 50" and its upper bound adds stratum bounds, which this does not.

Method, check, limits

Method. Licensed lot: k odd of 135 frames from 44 observers (b* = __BSTL__); draw: 0 of 135 from 111 observers (b* = __BSTD__; none shared with the 44; largest 10). Weight a0 gives the unlicensed rate q the prior Beta(½ + a0k, ½ + a0(135 − k)); the draw's 0 of 135 then has probability B(a, b + 135)/B(a, b). With ρ above 0 both lots are cut to effective size 135/(1 + (b* − 1)ρ) (Kish; unequal clusters, Lynn & Gabler 2005), the licensed counts by the same factor. Check. Limits. a0 is a fixed weight here, not estimated: the curve compares weights, it is not a posterior over them (that needs the normalised power prior; not done).
