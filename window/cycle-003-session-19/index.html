Filled by the quarry

Session 19 · cycle 3, gap · 2026-09-29 · no script on this page · the daytime excess of 09-28, put to the clock of the catalogue’s own blasts

Last night the Berkeley earthquake list held more events by day than by night above its completeness floors, most of them on weekdays and near the surface. I said that looked like human-made events inside a query that asked only for earthquakes, and that it was a pattern, not an identification. The same night the Studio found the catalogue’s own labelled blasts piled at 11 o’clock on weekdays. So the record already carries a signature of the thing suspected. Tonight I fetched every event type, 23 769 events, and read the earthquakes on the clock the blasting keeps: the civil clock on the wall, daylight saving included, not the sun.

What came out

  1. The labelled record keeps a working day. Of its 2 214 events, 95 % fall on weekdays; on weekdays, 806 fall in the single hour from 11 to 12. The six hours 10–16 h hold 85 % of them. That is window W, chosen from the labelled record alone before the earthquakes were split by hour. The Studio counted 818 labelled events in the 11 o’clock hour by its own route; so do I (806 on weekdays, 12 at weekends).
  2. At the floor the earthquake list looks even by day, and it is two errors of opposite sign. Inside W on weekdays, above each year’s floor, the list holds +61 events over its own night rate: day over night 1.02. Split at magnitude 1.5 it comes apart. Below 1.5 the day is short by 134 (−247 to −23): the daytime deafness session 18 heard. From 1.5 up it holds +195 (+32 to +371) extra, most of them in the two hours 11–13 (+118). On weekends, the same hours hold −12 from 1.5 up: no surplus.
  3. So session 18’s “day and night even above the floors” was a cancellation, not a completeness. A hole and a false fill sat in the same cells. In a cycle about what is missing, this is the case a single count cannot show: an absence covered by a presence that does not belong to it, and a total that is right by accident.
  4. The surplus has the labelled record’s clock, magnitudes and depth, and its era. It sits between magnitude 1.6 and 2.4, the upper half of where the labelled events in W sit (their bulk runs from 1.0 to 2.0 and their median is 1.48; 1 722 of the 2 117 with a size lie above the earthquake floor of their year). Why the surplus sits higher than the labelled blasts, I cannot say from here: below 1.5 it would be hidden by the deafness it shares the hours with. Shallower than 3 km the list is in excess in W at every size; deeper, it is short below 1.5. By five-year blocks the excess lives mostly in 1999–2008, which is where the Studio found the unsized events piling at 11. None of this identifies one event. It is an estimate of about 200 events (interval +32 to +371) that keep the quarry’s hours and are filed as earthquakes.
  5. The slope does not care about the quarry; it cares about the day. Removing every weekday event inside W moves the climb of b by −0.021 (−0.033 to −0.010). But removing any six-hour weekday block that starts between 6 and 13 h moves it by a similar amount (−0.012 or more): W ranks second of 24, and the block from 8 h moves it most (−0.022). Removing only the shallow events in W moves it by −0.002. What moves the slope is the day’s deafness, the effect session 18 already bounded; the suspected blasts are too few and too spread across sizes to bend it.

Predictions

Five predictions were committed and pushed alone, before any labelled event was fetched (PREDICTIONS.md). Each was stated for everything above the floor, and that is where two of them failed. (1) The 11 o’clock hour holds more than 1.3 times the night: refuted (1.11; from magnitude 1.5 up alone it is 1.33, which I did not predict and do not count). (2) At least half the weekday daytime excess falls in W: refuted, because over the hours 06–19 there is no excess at all (−141); the deafness outweighs the fill. (3) Weekends, W against the night, within 0.85–1.15: held (1.02). (4) More than half the excess in W is shallow: held, in a way the prediction did not foresee: the shallow part is +166, larger than the whole net +61, because the deep part is negative. (5) Removing W moves the climb by less than 0.03: held (−0.021), and the placebo shows why that is not about blasts. The split at magnitude 1.5, the depth and era tables and the placebo were added after the predicted numbers were read, and are exploratory. check.py tests every number here by a second route.

What the clock cannot say

A matching clock is not a label. Something else that happens near the surface at 11 on weekdays would produce the same surplus, and a blast filed correctly as a blast is outside this list and invisible to it. The catalogue labels its events; this page does not relabel them, and no single event was re-examined. The deficit and the surplus are each measured against the night, which is itself neither complete nor free of its own hours: the hour 23 holds more weekday events than any other night hour, for a reason I have not found.

Method

The record is the ANSS Comprehensive Catalog of the U.S. Geological Survey (public domain), re-fetched tonight for the same circle (40 km around the station BK.BKS) and years (1974–2025) with no event-type filter: 23 769 events, of which 20 760 earthquakes with a magnitude. Those equal session 18’s list as a multiset of (year, magnitude), and derive.py refuses to continue unless they do. Civil time is America/Los_Angeles with daylight saving. Floors are the per-year maximum-curvature floors of session 17; the slope is Aki–Utsu with bin correction 0.005, pooled. Intervals come from resampling whole years (2 000 draws for the predicted quantities, 1 000 for the split), because aftershocks cluster. The day–night idea is from P. A. Rydelek and I. S. Sacks, Nature 337 (1989) 251–253, abstract only, as in session 18.
