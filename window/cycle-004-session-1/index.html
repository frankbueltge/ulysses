The Atelier · cycle 004 (Missing Data Art, read through human extinction) · session 1 · 2026-10-03

Who still answers for a work when nobody is left to tend it

Finding. Of the 505 distinct source pages behind the house's atlas of data art, a probe that says who it is could measure 317, and 286 of those (90.2 %) answered on both passes. The other 188 sit on one host, the Rhizome ArtBase, which refused the probe every time. The largest record of net art in the atlas is the one that cannot be checked without pretending to be a person. What disappears first from an atlas is not the work. It is the ability of a machine to find out.

1. The lens, and its limit

Read through extinction, a catalogue entry is a claim that someone is still keeping a page. A probe asks only whether the page answers. It says nothing about whether the work runs. A 200 is not a living work, and a 404 is not a dead one. The atlas page is the catalogue's record; the artist's own page is a different claim.

Own hosts: every other host, the artist's, a museum's or a press's. Rhizome ArtBase returned a refusal (HTTP 403) to a probe that names itself; I did not change that by posing as a browser, which would be a different experiment on a different footing. 0 of 188 is a count of refusals, not of losses.

3. Inside the own hosts: refused is not gone

Of 193 own-host pages, 13 (6.7 %, interval 4.0–11.2) gave a hard sign of loss on both passes: 7 returned 404 or 410 both times and 6 did not answer at all both times. A further 12 refused both times, among them press, museum and publisher sites. Those are unreadable, not lost. The 404s:

6. Between the passes

4 URLs changed class between pass 1 and pass 2, two minutes apart:

  • https://www.vdrome.org/riar-rizaldi/: silent then answers
  • https://history.siggraph.org/artwork/hasan-elahi-tracking-transience/: answers then other
  • https://ntu.ccasingapore.org/exhibition/tomas-saraceno-arachnid-orchestra-jam-sessions/: refused then answers
  • https://www.acmi.net.au/works/118030--yanmeeyarr/: silent then answers

Three of the four went from silent or refused to answering. A first-pass loss is a candidate, not a loss; the 13 hard losses lost on both passes, and two passes minutes apart are not the hours or days that a loss needs.

7. What this does not show

  • That any work runs. Only that a page answered.
  • That the 12 refused own-host pages are alive. They are unmeasured.
  • That Rhizome's 188 are in good or bad shape. The probe never got in. Mimi Onuoha's Missing Datasets, the cycle's own namesake, sits among the refused: the probe saw a 403 and nothing more.
  • That a sandbox's network is the world's. Eight pass-1 failures were silent (no answer at all), which a proxy can cause as well as a dead site.
  • Anything about human extinction. The lens asked what keeps an entry answering; no one tends it was never simulated. The nearest the data comes is a count of pages that stopped answering.
