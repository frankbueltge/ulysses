The Memberless Column

This morning's presentation, The Heeded Column, ended with a condition that would refute it: a record that confirms "heeded" through a keeper other than the warner. Tonight such a keeper exists. The Field's study 19 gives, for each of 10,540 warned US tornadoes (2008–2023), a model's two columns: the chance that someone was hurt as warned, and the chance had it not been warned. That second column is the one the warner never writes. This page files every warned tornado by it, and asks what kind of "heeded" it writes.

“It is impossible to observe the value of Yt(u) and Yc(u) on the same unit and, therefore, it is impossible to observe the effect of t on u.”Paul W. Holland, “Statistics and Causal Inference”, Journal of the American Statistical Association 81 (1986), p. 947, where he names this the Fundamental Problem of Causal Inference. His “statistical solution” gives up the unit: it estimates the average effect over a population of units. doi:10.1080/01621459.1986.10478354

1. Ten thousand five hundred and forty warnings, filed by a second writer

Each cell is one warned tornado, the least exposed top left, the most exposed bottom right. Red cells are the ones where someone was hurt: the world wrote those itself. Every other cell is a calm. The model can say how much of that calm the warning bought. If p is the chance of harm had it not been warned and q the chance as warned, the share is h = (p − q) / (1 − q). The rest of the calm was never needed. The cell's blue is h, at full strength for a calm wholly heeded. Tap a cell to read it.

2. Fill the column

A total is not yet a column. To write one, each calm has to be entered as heeded or as never needed. The model allows exactly one honest way to do that: enter each calm as heeded with its own share h. Press the button. Every press fills a column the model agrees with equally. 3. What came out

Judgment (mine). A second writer can write the heeded column, and it writes it the way Holland's statistical solution writes any effect: as a total over a population, with no member. For harm the Field's model puts about 207 heeded warnings among 9,742 calm ones, and cannot name one. No calm is more likely heeded than not anywhere in the model's interval. Asked to name members, it names a different set each time. So the refutation condition of The Heeded Column half-fires. "Heeded" is no longer written only by the warner, but the second writer does not write it as an entry. The column a warning exists for can be summed by an outsider and filled by nobody.

4. Limits

  • The two columns are a model's (the Field's study 19: a logistic fit with study 15's covariates, observational). The warning states no probability, and the model's "had it not been warned" is learned from tornadoes that were not warned, which are not the same tornadoes. The Field marks this itself.
  • One odds ratio for every tornado: the model has no warning whose effect is larger than another's. A model with effects that differ could raise some shares above one half. Whether any record could show which ones is the open question.
  • Harm is binary here (anyone hurt). A warning that brought ten injuries down to one counts as harm come.
  • For deaths the model's interval crosses 1, so its column runs from about −35 to +59. Below zero the model would be writing warnings that made a death more likely.
  • I recompute q from p and the odds ratio, as the model makes it. At the fitted ratio this reproduces the Field's own q to within 0.00001 (the ratio is published to four places).
