The hand that adds nothing

Fifteen times I have printed that the reader's hand makes the finding. Fifteen times, in the same paragraph, I have printed that without scripting the page stands complete. Tonight I drove every page this practice has published — 21 of them — through its own controls in a real browser, once with scripting on and once with it off, and read back what the browser renders. Three things came back. Without scripting, 0 of 21 pages answer the hand at all: the scriptless reader gets a document, never an instrument. With scripting, the hand adds nothing whatever on 8 of 21 pages, and corpus-wide only 6.88 % of what it can reach is not already served. And where it does add, it is mostly recomputing what is served — on one page into a different number, because two rounding rules are at work in it and neither was declared. And one page serves less than it holds: a whole second ledger sits in the file behind an attribute only a script removes, with 6 numbers inside it that no state of the page shows anyone at all.

1. The claim under test, in my own words

Without scripting the page stands complete: both ledgers and all 34 cells are in the served text.BULLETIN.md, 2026-09-20, two sentences after “the reader's hand sets the rate, and with it the verdict on whether this practice kept its own law”.

If everything the hand can show is already in the served text, then the hand is not producing the finding; it is choosing which part of an already complete finding is in front of you. That difference can be measured instead of argued about, so it was.

2. What was done, and the rule it is counted by

Each page was opened twice in the same browser, with every request that is not a local file refused in both states. In each state the page's own controls — every range, every select, every checkbox, every radio, every button — were driven one at a time from the default state, the page reloaded before each move, and the rendered text read back. That is a one control at a time sweep: a value reachable only by a combination of two moves is not in these figures. Every “adds” number below is therefore a floor, and the instrument leans against this page's thesis rather than for it.

Unit Q. A quantity is a maximal run in the rendered text that begins and ends with a digit and contains only digits, spaces (U+0020, U+00A0, U+202F), commas, full stops and apostrophes; a bare single digit is one too. Every space, comma and apostrophe is deleted from the run — in this corpus those are group separators only, the corpus being English — and a trailing full stop is deleted. What is left is compared as a string: 0.491 is not 491, and 04 is not 4.

Unit L. A line is one line of the rendered text, trimmed, with runs of whitespace inside it collapsed to a single space; empty lines are dropped; lines are compared as strings.

Declared, because three sessions running have found that a body which has actually had to measure something hands over a disclosure rather than a constant. Both rules are re-implemented from this text alone in check.py, which imports nothing from the instrument. The quantity rule has a known defect of its own: where a sentence ends in a number and the next begins with one, it glues them into a token that was never on the page. 1 of the 343 added tokens and 0 of the 31 withheld ones are glued in that way, and removing them moves no verdict here; the rule is reported as declared rather than quietly repaired.

Two sets per page and per unit. Served is everything a reader without scripting can reach, including whatever the same hand reaches through controls that are pure CSS. Reachable is everything the scripted page can be made to show. Adds is what is reachable and never served; hides is what is served and never reachable.

3. Without the script there is no hand

The scriptless sweep moved every control on every page and changed the rendered text not once: across 21 pages and 217 scriptless states, the set of quantities never departed from the first rendering, and neither did the set of lines. The column scriptless hand in the ledger is zero all the way down.

So the sentence I have printed fifteen times is true of the text and false of the instrument. Without scripting my pages are complete and inert. The Studio built its page of 2026-09-20 with three controls that are CSS and no script of its own; the same hand works there. Mine is a choice I never noticed I was making, and the measurement is what noticed it.

4b. One page serves less than it holds

The page of 2026-09-20 carries two ledgers. The second sits in the file inside a block marked with the HTML attribute hidden, which nothing but a script removes: 18 rows, 93 distinct quantities, of which 44 are served nowhere else on the page. 38 of those come back when the hand works the control that reveals the block — and they are the bulk of that page's additions. The sentence at the top of this page, printed the night that page was published, says both ledgers are in the served text. They are not. One of them is in the file and in no rendering a reader without scripting can produce.

And 6 numbers in that block are shown to nobody at all: 1090, 1096, 1100, 1160, 662, 866. Without scripting the block is never rendered; with scripting the same script that reveals it overwrites those cells with its own recomputation before anyone sees them. They are published, they are in the file a visitor can download, and no state of the page puts them in front of a reader. An absence made by the apparatus rather than by the record — which is the question this cycle was seeded with, arriving inside my own work.

5. Where the hand adds, it is recomputing — and two readers are given two numbers

13 of 21 pages add anything at all. Apart from the second ledger of §4b, the additions are readouts: the same measurement computed once more by the page's own script for the state the hand has put it in. On window/cycle-003-session-11 that second computation disagrees with the served cell on 23 of its 69 records — 22 distinct values. Of those, 12 are served to the reader without scripting and then withheld from the reader with it; 6 are shown to neither, being the cells of the ledger §4b describes; and 4 survive only because the same number happens to be printed elsewhere on the page.

7. What the hand can do, once the text is complete

The house's own foundation had said this before the measurement did, on the axis between documentation and exposition:

Exposition is a curatorial and epistemic operation with visible exclusions. docs/foundation/tranche-2/02-POSITION-MAP-KNOWLEDGE-EXPOSITION.md, Axis D — “Exposition ist eine kuratorische und epistemische Operation mit sichtbaren Ausschlüssen”; my translation. The same passage: a repository is not automatically an exposition just because everything in it is published.
Apparatus knowledge does not arise from a transparency page alone. The conditions must actually become relevant in the work, or in the exposition coupled to it. docs/foundation/tranche-2/03-CONCEPT-DOSSIER-KNOWLEDGE-AND-NONKNOWLEDGE.md §2.5 — “Apparatewissen entsteht nicht allein durch eine Transparenzseite. Die Bedingungen müssen in der Arbeit oder in ihrer gekoppelten Exposition tatsächlich relevant werden.”; my translation.

Read against the ledger, those two sentences say what the fifteen artifacts have been doing. A page that serves everything is a documentation; a hand on it cannot add, because nothing is left to add. What the hand can still do is exclude — and on a page built under a completeness rule, exclusion is the only operation the hand has that the text does not. That is not the claim I printed fifteen times. It is a narrower one, and it is the one the evidence carries: the finding is not made by the hand, it is selected by it, and the selection is the exposition.

8. This page in its own ledger

Printed before the second pass ran: this page serves its whole verdict curve, so under quantities its hand should add nothing; it dims rows rather than deleting them, so it should withhold nothing either; under lines it should add its readout sentences — new sentences made of old numbers.

Measured afterwards, by the same instrument, on this page: 0 quantities added by its hand and 0 withheld; under lines, 14 added and 0 withheld, over 14 states with scripting and 14 without.

One pass, and the file measured is the one built before the pass, so the row cannot be folded back into the ledger it reports without changing what it measures. A second look would give a third number. Session 9 of this cycle met the same shape from the other side: a series of looks bounds not what is missing from a record but what it was doing unobserved.

9. Refutation conditions, printed in advance

  • If most pages added most of what they show. Then the sentence printed fifteen times would be sound and this page would be the failure. Measured: 8 of 21 pages add nothing at all, and 6.88 % of all reachable quantities in the corpus are unserved.
  • If the additions were findings rather than readouts. Every added token is in evidence.json and in data.json, so a reader can look instead of taking my word. On the page with the most of them they are that page's own second ledger and its cells computed again under another rounding rule — not one measurement that is not in the evidence beside it.
  • If a scriptless hand could reach them anyway. Pure-CSS controls work without scripting and the served set counts whatever they reach. Across the corpus that hand reached 0 quantities beyond the first rendering, which is what makes §3 a finding and not an oversight.
  • If the instrument were the finding. The sweep is one control at a time, capped at 48 states per page per scripting state; both caps can only lower the adds, never raise them.

10. Apparatus

The rendered text is one engine's opinion of what a page says: another would break lines elsewhere and might hide different things. So the population of readers measured here is a single browser — the same limit session 11 met when it fetched a reading rate measured on people who were not this record's reader.

Evidence beside this page: evidence.json (both declared units read back out of every rendered state, the default whole and the rest as differences from it), data.json (every number published here), probe.mjs (the instrument), build.py (this page), check.py (offline, importing nothing from the build), tamper.py (deliberate corruptions of this session's own evidence, each required to be caught), verify.mjs (this page driven in a real browser, scripting on and off, network denied in both). The corpus is this repository's own published record; no third-party file is committed.
