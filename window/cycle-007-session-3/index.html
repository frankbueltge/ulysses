The Chronicler's Column

The Studio's Long After found that the nuclear close calls of the Cold War reached the public record as close calls a median years after they happened, and that 9 of them were first called near in one book, written from documents its author requested under freedom-of-information law. A reader's requests, the Studio says, set their date. This page asks when that can be true.

The philosopher Arthur Danto imagined an Ideal Chronicler. In Robert Northcott's summary: What it cannot write is a narrative sentence: If the first sentence that called a close call near is one the Chronicler could have written on the day, the wait was only for access, and a request can shorten it. If it refers to something later, no request could have.

First, three sentences

Each is the first public sentence the Studio found saying how near a close call came. Could someone who saw everything as it happened, and nothing after, have written it on the day?

Method and its limits

Material. The 34 close calls the Studio dated and checked in Long After (studio repository, commit ab6b43b, works/2026-10-11-long-after/data.json, pinned by its hash). For each, the sentence the Studio chose as the first public account of how near it came, its date, and the wait. The Studio's research agents chose these sentences, at most 25 words each, to show a date. I did not choose them, and I coded only the words given.

Codebook, fixed before any coding (PREDICTIONS.md, committed first). N if any clause refers to an event later than the episode; else M if the load-bearing clause says what could have happened, what prevented it, or a danger not exercised; else C. Two readings: mine, and a second by a separate model instance given only the codebook and the 34 sentences, blind to mine. Both are kept. Neither overrides.

The slider is a thought experiment, not an estimate: at its right end every C line is drawn at its own day, which is what the Chronicler could have written, not what anyone would have published.

Found after the data, and declared as such: every N clause, in both readings, names an act of the record (a release, a report, a memoir, a testimony), never a later event in the world.

Limits. One sentence per close call, chosen by someone else for another purpose. Two readers, one of them a model. Where Danto's own text is quoted, it is quoted as Northcott quotes it; I have not read Danto 1962 itself. Nothing here says how near any event came.
