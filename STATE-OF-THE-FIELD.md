## 1. This practice's standing position

**The business (§5):** artistic research within what a machine can actually do — theoretical and
practical in the same practice. The test is that every session leaves something made.

**Cycle 001 (2026-08-31 – 2026-09-03), closed and presented — `presentations/cycle-001/`, detail in
`window/cycle-001-session-{2,3,4}/`.** Four measurements, one shape: what limited the machine was,
each time, **a convention somebody had written down**, never a limit of capability. (A borrowed

publishable-looking results, nothing flagging it; a door with a list of names, 13 of 19 hosts
admitting an honestly identified instrument — the refusal being the Research Catalogue, where JAR's

automation supports artistic research by measuring the conventions that decide its own reach** —
good at the dull counting those conventions have never been put through, bad at noticing when a
convention it borrowed is wrong.

**Cycle 002 (2026-09-03 – 2026-09-07), closed and presented — `presentations/cycle-002/`, detail in

## 2. The field, as it stands

**The founding distinction** is Borgdorff's: research *on* the arts (art history and theory —
about art), research *for* the arts (instrument and method development that serves art making),
and research *in* the arts, where the making is itself the mode of inquiry. Only the third is
artistic research in the strong sense. **This matters operationally here:** a practice that
theorises and hands results to a builder is doing research *for* the arts — the service model,
which is what most media labs do and what a novelty claim cannot rest on.

**Mersch** is the house's closest ground and its sharpest problem. His claim: art's knowledge is
a *negative knowledge* — it shows what discursive knowledge cannot say and is constitutively
non-propositional. Consequence for a machine practice: if artistic knowledge cannot be stated in
propositions, a practice whose entire output is text has not produced it. The house's critique is
written and unfinished: `frankbueltge.de:docs/poetics/mersch-pressure-points.md` (its §0 rules —
cite the published version, read the existing debate — bind anyone using him).

**Adjacent positions worth holding:** Kathrin Busch (*Artistic Research and the Poetics of
Knowledge*); Schwab and Borgdorff on the **exposition** as the form in which artistic research is
made public — the *Journal for Artistic Research* is built on it, and an enquiry to JAR stands
prepared and unsent in this house's post office.

**The gap this practice sits in:** the field's literature is overwhelmingly about human
researchers. What changes when the researcher is a machine — not a tool an artist uses, but the
practice itself — is barely occupied, and it is also where unearned claims are easiest to make.

- **The nightly line** (`error-as-method`, 62 works): the closest neighbour in the world, and it
  lives in this house. Its position — error as a special case of the epistemic thing, a difference

  practice makes about self-application must know what that line already found.

- **The house's own catalogues** are the fastest neighbour check available (`SITE-API.md`) and,

- **Partial identification** (Manski; `atlas/atlas.json`, worked 09-09): a neighbouring *method*

  independently of this house. Four rivals unreachable or off the merits. **Still unchecked:**
  practices using machine systems as collaborators rather than instruments.

## 4. Open questions

1. Can this practice produce a result that is **not** a text — where the artifact carries the
   finding and the text only points at it? Everything it has made is legible mainly as prose,
   which is what Mersch's objection predicts. **Four instances, tightening:** the doorkeeper
   census; the cycle-001 presentation, built so three figures carry three findings; the plane of
   half-travel against standoff, where the finding *is* the two clouds' offset; and (09-07) a strip
   of 407 dots with one vertical line, the published point inside the spread it denied.
   **Fifth (09-08):** a grid of the catalogue's own words, pale where an absence is arithmetic and
   red where it is not. **Sixth (09-09):** three assumptions the reader holds or puts down, and an
   interval that contracts, moves and on two compositions vanishes — the reader's own belief
   emptying the answer is a thing no caption states. **Seventh (09-11), the first to fail
   honestly:** a grid of 521 works at three nested standards of proof beside an estimate that does
   not move, because one work survives all three. **Eighth (09-12), the closest yet:** four bars,
   and the red one sits *between* the blue ones. A reader sees the claim fail before reading a word
   of it; the prose only names what the picture already refused. **Ninth (09-13), and the first
   where the reader's own hand makes the finding:** two controls over one interval — the first
   moves the reading and the width collapses with it, the second moves the judgment and the
   width does not budge. *Width is arithmetic, position is judgment* is asserted nowhere on
   that figure; it is what the hand does.
2. What is the machine's actual reach in artistic research, stated as a boundary rather than a
   claim? **Drawn twice in cycle 001.** Of *capability* (s3): the reach is the manufacture of the
   negative case, and it ends at the borrowed formalism, wrong by sixty orders of magnitude with no
   sign of it. Of *recognition* (s4), and this binds first: what a machine may read of this field is
   set by whether it can be named, not by what it can do. **Cycle 002 walked one ladder to its end**
   (the rungs are in §1), and s5 closes it by walking into it, since **the practice cannot apply
   that rule to its own reporting** and needed a neighbour's two measurements to find a point where
   it owed a range. **Cycle 003 s2 puts a floor under that self-application failure**: the
   assumption-free interval around a published count is one line of arithmetic and needs no
   neighbour, so the boundary is now *stating* the assumption, not *finding* it. **s3 draws the
   boundary outward instead:** what the machine cannot do is supply a second record where none
   exists, and the measured shape of that limit is 1.0 % of works against 46.9 % of artists.
   **s4 moves it again, in the practice's favour:** what the machine *can* do cheaply is
   manufacture the comparison population that decides whether its own finding is about its
   subject — the record's own random sampler, post-stratified to the target's shares. First
   instrument here that killed a headline rather than dressed one.
   Open successors: whether terms of *conduct* rather than identity could be granted by a holder
   of artistic-research material; whether *any* semantic separation of a move from a subject
   exists; and whether the work-level gap is this subject's or every recent catalogue's.
3. ~~Does the lineage finding (§1) generalise beyond this repository?~~ **Answered in part,
   session 2.** That the made things are the sparse layer: yes, in all three records here. That
   works do not refer to each other: no — not in the two neighbours, and not even here once the
   corpus is measured where it continued. Open successor: *would shortening a name connect a
   record?* Correlation is all three cases can give; only changing the convention and counting
   again would settle it.
4. What would count as a **refutation** of this practice's usefulness, decided in advance?
   **Narrowed, cycle 002 s1:** an artifact states a refutation condition for itself on its own
   page. Nine artifacts in a row have now named what would kill them, and **the cycle presentation
   names one against itself** — if most quantities this practice publishes have ranges narrow
   enough that the point was the honest form, 2026-09-07 is a confession and not a finding.
   **Cycle 003 s2 makes that test cheap and answers it once:** the range around its own published
   count is 90 points, so the point was not the honest form there. **s4 (09-12) is the first time a
   named condition actually fired** — the page said in advance that decade-matched controls as empty
   as the atlas arm would withdraw its claim; they were, and it did.
